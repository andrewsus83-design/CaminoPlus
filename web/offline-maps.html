Offline maps

Signal drops. Your map doesn't.

Large parts of the Camino have weak or no mobile signal. CaminoPlus is built offline-first, so a full walking day works with your phone in airplane mode.

How it works

Four steps, then just walk.

Pick your route

Choose a Camino and the town you start from. CaminoPlus lists your stages.

Download over Wi-Fi

At home or at the albergue, download the next stages. Each pack is about 2–5 MB.

Walk without signal

Map, GPS position, stays, cafés and points of interest all work offline.

Sync when you're back

Posts and stamps you made offline upload on their own when you reconnect.

Small downloads

A whole stage in a few megabytes.

Stage packs are compact vector maps, not heavy images, so they download quickly even on albergue Wi-Fi and barely touch your phone storage. Stage 1 of the Camino Francés, Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port to Roncesvalles over the Pyrenees, is about 4.6 MB.

Inside each pack

What works offline.

Vector map

Paths, roads, buildings, water and terrain, crisp at every zoom level.

Your route line

The waymarked route, stage start and end points and distances.

Stays & cafés

Albergues, hostels and cafés on that stage, with the details you need.

Points of interest

Churches, fountains, pharmacies and more, with their stories.

Battery-saving GPS

GPS needs no mobile data. CaminoPlus uses a location mode tuned for walking, with a target of under 15% battery over a 7-hour day.

Outbox that syncs itself

Anything you post, stamp or plan offline is saved on your phone and uploaded in order when you have signal again.

“Updated X ago”

Live info like crowds always shows when it was last refreshed, so you know how fresh it is.

Built on OpenStreetMap

Offline maps are made from OpenStreetMap, the free, community-built map of the world, which is especially detailed along the Camino. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License.

Offline downloads are part of Pilgrim Plus ($12 a year).
