Buen Camino!

A design system for the Way of St James

CaminoPlus pairs Airbnb's calm, spacious hospitality with Waze's friendly, map-first guidance. Four colors drawn from the waymark shell, the Cross of St James and the pilgrim's credencial. Every value below is a CSS variable in tokens.css, mirrored in tokens.json for React Native.

01

Spacious

16px screen margins, 40px between sections. One idea per card. When in doubt, add air.

02

Pill-first

Buttons, inputs, chips and the tab bar are fully rounded. Cards use 28px. No sharp corners.

03

Map-first & playful

Waze-style floating controls and one-tap reports, plus a light RPG layer: XP, quests, churches and audio stories.

04

Reverent, not churchy

Gold and red are earned accents: Jubilee moments, stamps, alerts. Parchment and blue carry the rest.

01 · Color

Four colors of the Way

Each color comes from a real Camino symbol. Values were checked against WCAG 2.2. Gold is too light for text, so it gets a darker "ink" companion.

Shell Gold#D9A21B · --cp-color-gold

The scallop shell on every waymark, and the gold of the Jubilee (Año Santo).

Use for: highlight fills, active tab pill, progress, Report button, Jubilee / premium badge. Text on gold is always Atlantic Blue (5.3:1). For gold-colored text use --cp-color-gold-ink #7A5A0C.

Do one gold moment per screen
Don't gold text on parchment (2.0:1)
Atlantic Blue#14365D · --cp-color-blue

The blue field behind the shell on Camino signs, and the Atlantic at Finisterre.

Use for: all body text and headlines (10.7:1 on parchment), primary buttons, selected chips, journey card, route line on the map.

Do use as "black" everywhere
Don't use pure #000 or grey text
Parchment#F6EFE2 · --cp-color-parchment

Old stone, warm paper and the pilgrim's credencial.

Use for: app background on every screen, text on blue. Cards sit on top in Paper White #FFFDF8 so they lift softly without heavy shadows.

Do parchment bg + white cards
Don't pure #FFF backgrounds
Cross Red#A63D2F · --cp-color-red

The Cross of St James (Cruz de Santiago), the sword-cross of the Apostle.

Use for: danger, errors, path hazards, SOS, "Full" status, and stamp ink. White text on red is 6.3:1. Use sparingly: about 2% of any screen.

Do alerts, hazards, stamps
Don't use as a decorative brand fill

Action rail: love, bookmark, comments, share

One rail for the whole app. Discover is the standard and the full-screen journey viewer (Me, Journey) draws the very same component, components/ActionRail.tsx, fed by lib/actionRail.ts. A screen never draws its own like, bookmark, comment or share buttons, and never decides where the rail sits: the rail places itself, so the column is in the same spot of the screen in Discover and in the viewer. The same comments sheet opens from both.

.cp-rail · 56 circles, 14 apart · right edge 16 (screen margin) · lowest circle 120 above the safe area, on every screen · rgba(255,253,248,.85) · heart 28 red, bookmark 28 blue, chat 26, share 26 · count 11/13 under the icon

WhatThe standard
OrderLove, bookmark, comments, share, top to bottom. Never reordered, never hidden for a kind of content.
Circle56 × 56, pill radius, paper white at 85 % so it reads on any photo. 14 apart, centred, the right edge on the 16 screen margin and the lowest circle 120 above the safe area, on every screen that has the rail. That height clears Discover's floating tab bar; the full-screen viewer has no tab bar and keeps the same 120, so the buttons are under the thumb in the same place on both. The caption, and the text of a text-only post, keep clear of the column.
IconsHeart 28 in Cross Red, bookmark 28 in Atlantic Blue, chat 26 and share 26 in blue. Heart and bookmark are filled when on.
CountsUnder the icon, 11/13 blue. Compact (999, 1.2K, 12.3K, 1M), rounded down, never more than the real number. Shown only when the count was read: a real 0 shows 0, a count nobody read shows nothing. Screen readers hear the whole number: "Love, 1,234 likes".
StatesOff, on, and "not available yet": dimmed to 45 %, disabled, and the label says so. The labels are Love / Remove love, Bookmark / Remove bookmark, Comments, Share.
Dark modeThe circles are cream and the photo behind never changes, so the circle and every icon on it keep their light token values in both modes (never the theme colours, which turn light blue and light red). Same rule for white text on a scrim.
Sample contentDiscover's sample stories and the sample journey: love, bookmark and comments stay on the phone, and share sends the invitation to the app, never the sample itself.
Real postsA tap shows its result at once and puts the old state back, with one short sentence, if it fails. These need the network: offline nothing changes and it says so.

Settings is not a tab. It opens from the gear at the top right of Me, next to the inbox bell, as a screen of its own: a back button on the left, light/dark and log out on the right, and no tab bar under it. Library is the fifth tab. It has no title: the row on top is a search field and the round Bookmarks button in the corner (the same size as the bell and the gear on Me), and directly under it the tabs Bible, Books and Notes, then the banner of the tab. The Bible tab is real: the World English Bible, Catholic edition, all 73 books, in the app. It starts with a compact daily reading banner (one handwritten note and a gold pill), then a segmented pill that chooses the part (Old Testament, Deuterocanon, New Testament; it is in the page and scrolls with it), then the books of that part as square tiles, three in a row. A tile opens the numbers of the chapters of the book, a number opens the words, and Previous and Next name where they go and carry on across the books. Books shows twelve example rows and says so. On Notes, which is the diary, the gold button writes a note, and the search row and the tabs stay at the top while the notes scroll under them. The diary (Library, Notes) keeps its pages only on the phone: the list says so, nothing is uploaded, logging out keeps the pages, deleting the account deletes them, and a page is saved as it is typed, so there is no Save button.

Library search looks through the Bible and Books tabs at once, as you type (every book of the Bible and the twelve books; Notes are not searched here), and lists what it finds grouped by tab (Bible, Books) under one Preview notice. A word counts when it starts a word of a row (a name or title, the author, the part of the Bible, the line under it, or the name of the tab), so "new testament" lists the 27 books of the New Testament. With nothing found it says "Nothing found for ..." and offers Clear search; clearing the field brings the tabs and the banner back. Bookmarks is a screen of its own, opened from the round button in the corner: a back button, no tab bar, then two parts. Library lists the rows bookmarked from the tabs; they stay only on the phone and logging out clears them, and each is still an example. Posts lists the posts bookmarked in the journey viewer; they stay with the account, and with no signal the list from the last visit is shown, with a line that says so. A row that is taken off stays in place, with an empty bookmark, until the screen is left, so a slip can be put back with one tap.

Chapter 1

¹ In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. ² The same was in the beginning with God. ³ All things were made through him. Without him, nothing was made that has been made. ⁴ In him was life, and the life was the light of men. ⁵ The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness hasn’t overcome it.

⁶ There came a man sent from God, whose name was John. ⁷ The same came as a witness, that he might testify about the light, that all might believe through him. ⁸ He was not the light, but was sent that he might testify about the light. ⁹ The true light that enlightens everyone was coming into the world.

¹⁰ He was in the world, and the world was made through him, and the world didn’t recognize him. ¹¹ He came to his own, and those who were his own didn’t receive him. ¹² But as many as received him, to them he gave the right to become God’s children, to those who believe in his name: ¹³ who were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God.

¹⁴ The Word became flesh and lived among us. We saw his glory, such glory as of the only born Son of the Father, full of grace and truth. ¹⁵ John testified about him. He cried out, saying, “This was he of whom I said, ‘He who comes after me has surpassed me, for he was before me.’” ¹⁶ From his fullness we all received grace upon grace. ¹⁷ For the law was given through Moses. Grace and truth were realized through Jesus Christ. ¹⁸ No one has seen God at any time. The only born Son, who is in the bosom of the Father, has declared him.

¹⁹ This is John’s testimony, when the Jews sent priests and Levites from Jerusalem to ask him, “Who are you?”

²⁰ He declared, and didn’t deny, but he declared, “I am not the Christ.”

²¹ They asked him, “What then? Are you Elijah?”

He said, “I am not.”

“Are you the prophet?”

He answered, “No.”

²² They said therefore to him, “Who are you? Give us an answer to take back to those who sent us. What do you say about yourself?”

²³ He said, “I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness, ‘Make straight the way of the Lord,’ as Isaiah the prophet said.”

²⁴ The ones who had been sent were from the Pharisees. ²⁵ They asked him, “Why then do you baptize if you are not the Christ, nor Elijah, nor the prophet?”

²⁶ John answered them, “I baptize in water, but among you stands one whom you don’t know. ²⁷ He is the one who comes after me, who is preferred before me, whose sandal strap I’m not worthy to loosen.” ²⁸ These things were done in Bethany beyond the Jordan, where John was baptizing.

²⁹ The next day, he saw Jesus coming to him, and said, “Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world! ³⁰ This is he of whom I said, ‘After me comes a man who is preferred before me, for he was before me.’ ³¹ I didn’t know him, but for this reason I came baptizing in water, that he would be revealed to Israel.” ³² John testified, saying, “I have seen the Spirit descending like a dove out of heaven, and it remained on him. ³³ I didn’t recognize him, but he who sent me to baptize in water said to me, ‘On whomever you will see the Spirit descending and remaining on him is he who baptizes in the Holy Spirit.’ ³⁴ I have seen and have testified that this is the Son of God.”

³⁵ Again, the next day, John was standing with two of his disciples, ³⁶ and he looked at Jesus as he walked, and said, “Behold, the Lamb of God!” ³⁷ The two disciples heard him speak, and they followed Jesus. ³⁸ Jesus turned and saw them following, and said to them, “What are you looking for?”

They said to him, “Rabbi” (which is to say, being interpreted, Teacher), “where are you staying?”

³⁹ He said to them, “Come and see.”

They came and saw where he was staying, and they stayed with him that day. It was about the tenth hour. ⁴⁰ One of the two who heard John and followed him was Andrew, Simon Peter’s brother. ⁴¹ He first found his own brother, Simon, and said to him, “We have found the Messiah!” (which is, being interpreted, Christ). ⁴² He brought him to Jesus. Jesus looked at him and said, “You are Simon the son of Jonah. You shall be called Cephas” (which is by interpretation, Peter).

⁴³ On the next day, he was determined to go out into Galilee, and he found Philip. Jesus said to him, “Follow me.” ⁴⁴ Now Philip was from Bethsaida, the city of Andrew and Peter. ⁴⁵ Philip found Nathanael, and said to him, “We have found him of whom Moses in the law and also the prophets, wrote: Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph.”

⁴⁶ Nathanael said to him, “Can any good thing come out of Nazareth?”

Philip said to him, “Come and see.”

⁴⁷ Jesus saw Nathanael coming to him, and said about him, “Behold, an Israelite indeed, in whom is no deceit!”

⁴⁸ Nathanael said to him, “How do you know me?”

Jesus answered him, “Before Philip called you, when you were under the fig tree, I saw you.”

⁴⁹ Nathanael answered him, “Rabbi, you are the Son of God! You are King of Israel!”

⁵⁰ Jesus answered him, “Because I told you, ‘I saw you underneath the fig tree,’ do you believe? You will see greater things than these!” ⁵¹ He said to him, “Most certainly, I tell you all, hereafter you will see heaven opened, and the angels of God ascending and descending on the Son of Man.”

Library · Books and the reader

The Books tab shows its twelve books as covers: an A5 page, portrait (148 by 210 mm, so as high as 210/148 of its width), two in a row, 16 from the edge and 12 apart (173 by 245 on a 390 phone, 138 by 196 on a 320 phone, 193 by 274 on a 430 phone). Each is drawn like a book: a spine with a groove and the shade of the hinge, the edges of the pages on the right, a small radius, a soft shadow, the glyph of the book in a ring, the title set like a cover in Fraunces (two to four lines, as large as fits, never a word cut), the author in small spaced capitals, the era in JetBrains Mono and the mark Free (a book whose text is in the app) or Soon (an empty ring: its edition is still being checked). The paper does not change with light and dark, nor does the navy printed on it. A cover is one button ("The Way of a Pilgrim, Anonymous, free") and the ribbon at its top right is another, 44 by 44, that bookmarks the book (the same key as ever, books:id). The banner is the compact one of the Bible tab: a label, a title, one short handwritten note and a gold pill; the pill says Start reading (the first book that has a text) or Continue reading (the book read last, at the line where you stopped), and with no text in any book there is no pill and the banner says plainly that the texts are being checked. A cover opens the book page: a larger cover, the title, the author, the era, how long it is, the blurb, the gold button (Start reading or Continue: Chapter 7), the table of contents under its parts with the chapters read ticked and the place where you stopped marked, and the credit of the edition. A book with no text says Not in the app yet and why, and has no button. The reader keeps a header while the chapter scrolls under it: the way back to the contents, two buttons for smaller and larger text (five steps, kept on the phone), Previous and Next named by where they go (they cross the parts and are off at the first and the last chapter), the title of the book with "Chapter 7 of 40" and a thin line that shows how far down you are. The chapter is Fraunces for its title, Inter for the words (a line 1.6 times the letter, about 70 characters across on a wide window), JetBrains Mono for the numbers; at the end Next and Previous again, and one handwritten note. Where you stopped, the chapters you read and the size of the text stay on the phone and nothing is fetched: the books work with no signal.

He that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, saith the Lord. These are the words of Christ; and they teach us how far we must imitate His life and character, if we seek true illumination, and deliverance from all blindness of heart. Let it be our most earnest study, therefore, to dwell upon the life of Jesus Christ.

2. His teaching surpasseth all teaching of holy men, and such as have His Spirit find therein the hidden manna. But there are many who, though they frequently hear the Gospel, yet feel but little longing after it, because they have not the mind of Christ. He, therefore, that will fully and with true wisdom understand the words of Christ, let him strive to conform his whole life to that mind of Christ.

3. What doth it profit thee to enter into deep discussion concerning the Holy Trinity, if thou lack humility, and be thus displeasing to the Trinity? For verily it is not deep words that make a man holy and upright; it is a good life which maketh a man dear to God. I had rather feel contrition than be skilful in the definition thereof. If thou knewest the whole Bible, and the sayings of all the philosophers, what should all this profit thee without the love and grace of God? Vanity of vanities, all is vanity, save to love God, and Him only to serve. That is the highest wisdom, to cast the world behind us, and to reach forward to the heavenly kingdom.

4. It is vanity then to seek after, and to trust in, the riches that shall perish. It is vanity, too, to covet honours, and to lift up ourselves on high. It is vanity to follow the desires of the flesh and be led by them, for this shall bring misery at the last. It is vanity to desire a long life, and to have little care for a good life. It is vanity to take thought only for the life which now is, and not to look forward to the things which shall be hereafter. It is vanity to love that which quickly passeth away, and not to hasten where eternal joy abideth.

Chapter I
Of the inward life
5 min · 927 words

The kingdom of God is within you, saith the Lord. Turn thee with all thine heart to the Lord and forsake this miserable world, and thou shalt find rest unto thy soul. Learn to despise outward things and to give thyself to things inward, and thou shalt see the kingdom of God come within thee. Christ will come to thee, and show thee His consolation, if thou prepare a worthy mansion for Him within thee. All His glory and beauty is from within, and there it pleaseth Him to dwell. He often visiteth the inward man and holdeth with him sweet discourse, giving him soothing consolation, much peace, friendship exceeding wonderful.

2. Go to, faithful soul, prepare thy heart for this bridegroom that he may vouchsafe to come to thee and dwell within thee, for so He saith, if any man loveth me he will keep my words: and my Father will love him, and we will come unto him and make our abode with him. Give, therefore, place to Christ and refuse entrance to all others. When thou hast Christ, thou art rich, and hast sufficient. He shall be thy provider and faithful watchman in all things, so that thou hast no need to trust in men, for men soon change and swiftly pass away, but Christ remaineth for ever and standeth by us firmly even to the end.

3. There is no great trust to be placed in a frail and mortal man, even though he be useful and dear to us, neither should much sorrow arise within us if sometimes he oppose and contradict us. They who are on thy side to-day, may to-morrow be against thee, and often are they turned round like the wind. Put thy whole trust in God and let Him be thy fear and thy love, He will answer for thee Himself, and will do for thee what is best. Here hast thou no continuing city, and wheresoever thou art, thou art a stranger and a pilgrim, and thou shalt never have rest unless thou art closely united to Christ within thee.

4. Why dost thou cast thine eyes hither and thither, since this is not the place of thy rest? In heaven ought thy habitation to be, and all earthly things should be looked upon as it were in the passing by. All things pass away and thou equally with them. Look that thou cleave not to them lest thou be taken with them and perish. Let thy contemplation be on the Most High, and let thy supplication be directed unto Christ without ceasing. If thou canst not behold high and heavenly things, rest thou in the passion of Christ and dwell willingly in His sacred wounds. For if thou devoutly fly to the wounds of Jesus, and the precious marks of the nails and the spear, thou shalt find great comfort in tribulation, nor will the slights of men trouble thee much, and thou wilt easily bear their unkind words.

Three rules for code

role.* first

Page, card, text, muted text, link, button, error, hero card, text on gold. Hero cards are role.inverse with role.onInverse text, never c.blue as a background. Text and icons on gold are role.onHighlight, never c.blue (blue on gold is 1.1 : 1 in dark mode).

c.* only when the meaning is the same

A status wash, a progress bar, a hairline. c.blue is the action color, not a surface. A color read once when a file loads (a top-level constant) never follows the mode: make it a function or a getter.

fixed.* for surfaces that do not change

A photo, a pastel story card, a cream round button on a photo, the slide of a post: what is drawn on them uses the light palette in both modes, because the surface behind it does not change. A map has its own day and night and uses fixed.* too.

Example: the tab bar

Do · inactive tab = role.tabInactive #BAC6D6, active = role.tabActive #FFFFFF, thicker stroke and a bold label.

Inactive 8.8 : 1 on the bar, active 15.3 : 1: the active tab has at least 1.5 times the contrast of an inactive one. In light mode the same two roles are the blue (12.0 : 1) and the stone (6.1 : 1) the bar always had.
