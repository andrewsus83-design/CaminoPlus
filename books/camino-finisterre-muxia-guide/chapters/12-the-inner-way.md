# The inner Way

People walk to the sea for many reasons, and they do not have to be yours. Some walk out of faith: the road begins at the cathedral of St James and ends at two places of old devotion, the Santo Cristo of Fisterra and the Virgin of the Boat at Muxía. Some walk for sport, or for a rest from work, or because the cathedral was not quite the end of something. Some walk out of grief, or at a turning point. Some are simply curious what a headland at the edge of Europe looks like at dusk. All are welcome on the road, and none needs to defend the reason.

## After the cathedral

Every other Camino ends in Santiago. This one starts there. It is a different kind of walking: not towards an arrival but away from one. The Xunta calls it the most faithful realisation of the pilgrims' old cry, *Ultreia*, "farther beyond".

There is a long human record of how pilgrims went. The Xunta's heritage report says that most of the accounts it quotes from medieval travellers show people who were passive, curious and even offhand: they went because they had been told in Santiago that one had to see the end of the world. Domenico Laffi, in 1673, called the trip a whim. If you are not sure why you are going, you are in old company.

## The old rites, without the fire

The Concello de Fisterra names two. The first is the bath: the pilgrim washed off the dust of the road at the beach of Langosteira, two kilometres before the village, and began the last approach clean. The second is the sunset, which the Concello calls death and resurrection: the sun dying in the sea, and rising the next day, as the pilgrim rises in the daily walking. You do not have to believe in any of it. You can take the first as a way of washing, and the second as an hour to be quiet.

Muxía has its own old gesture. On the Camiño da Pel, the "Way of the Skin", pilgrims washed at a fountain before they entered the sanctuary. The fountain is gone, the Xunta says, but its name is still on the path.

## Walking with a question

You do not need a question, but if you have one it will keep you company. Here is one for each day, to take or leave.

1. **Santiago to Negreira.** You have just arrived somewhere. What did it finish for you, and what did it leave open?
2. **Negreira to Olveiroa.** This is the long day. What do you carry that you could set down?
3. **Olveiroa to Corcubión.** You see the sea at last, far off. What are you walking towards?
4. **Corcubión to Fisterra.** What would you like to leave behind that cannot be burned?
5. **Fisterra to Muxía.** After the end of the earth, what comes next, and what does the road ask of you on the way back to a life?

## For every kind of walker

If you walk with faith, the sanctuary at Muxía and the church of Santa María das Areas at Fisterra are places for quiet. If you do not, they are still quiet, and worth a rest. If your reason is grief, give yourself leave to feel it at the edge of the sea. If you walk to get fit, let the beauty in anyway. The road does not ask for a reason at the door. It asks you to keep walking, and then to stand still and look at the sea.
