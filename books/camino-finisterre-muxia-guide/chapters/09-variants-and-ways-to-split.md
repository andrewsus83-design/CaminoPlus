# Variants, and ways to split the days

## How many days? The sources differ

No two sources cut this road the same way, and the CaminoPlus catalog gives no stage count for it. Here is what each says.

- **The Concello de Fisterra** counts three stages to the cape: Santiago to Negreira (~22 km), Negreira to Olveiroa (~33 km) and Olveiroa to Fisterra (~33 km). That is ~88 km, and Muxía would be a fourth day.
- **The Xunta de Galicia** gives four stages to the lighthouse, Santiago to Negreira (~20.9 km), Negreira to Olveiroa (~34.3 km), Olveiroa to Corcubión (~20.7 km) and Corcubión to Fisterra (~14.2 km), and a fifth to Muxía (~28.7 km). Its own introduction speaks of pilgrims walking at least four or five days more after Santiago.
- **Spain's official tourism website** has five stages with the same towns, at ~20, ~34, ~20, ~14 and ~29 km.
- **One travel agency's route page** also has five, but cuts them differently: ~20.6 km to Negreira, ~31 km to a town just over ~1 km off the route, ~21 km to Cee, ~16 km to Fisterra and ~31 km to Muxía.

So the honest answer is: three days to Fisterra and one to Muxía if you walk fast, five if you follow the Xunta, and more if you want to go slowly. This book follows the Xunta's five.

## Plans that fit

These are sums of the sources' figures, which the book has added up, so they are approximate and carry the sources' differences.

- **Four days, both capes.** The Concello's three stages and the Xunta's last. It means two days of ~33 km in a row, then ~29 km. Only for the very fit and well prepared.
- **Five days, both capes.** The Xunta's plan, and the one in the chapters above: ~21, ~34, ~21, ~14, ~29 km. The long second day is the one to think about.
- **Six or seven days.** Split the long second day and the last day. For the second, the Xunta's own list of waypoints names O Cornado, Santa Mariña and Lago, and the travel agency above breaks it at a town just off the route. For the last, Lires is the natural break.
- **A rest day.** Fisterra and Muxía are both good places for one. Give one of them an evening and a morning.

Whether you can sleep where you want to is a question for the app and the phone. Which places have a hostel changes from season to season, so this book names none.

## Fisterra first, or Muxía first

At the roundabout after Hospital, the road forks. The Xunta says this is the fork of the ways to Fisterra and Muxía. You have a choice.

**To Fisterra first, then Muxía.** This is the book's route: the sea at Cee, the cape, then the day along the coast to Muxía.

**To Muxía first.** From Olveiroa the Xunta gives a direct stage of ~32.7 km, rated high and estimated at ~8 h 15 min. It goes by Dumbría, the municipal capital, and San Martiño de Ozón, with asphalt and country roads alternating. Near the end the road passes the Romanesque church of San Xulián de Moraime, close to the sea, and arrives at the sanctuary. Santiago to Muxía by this way is ~87 km, says the Xunta, and ~86.5 km, medium to high and about ~16 hours "more or less", says the Concello de Muxía. That is a three-day walk: the Xunta's first two stages and this one add up to ~87.9 km. From Muxía the Xunta then gives ~32 km, rated medium to high, to Fisterra. The whole road in this order is ~119.9 km.

Which end do you want to finish at? In one order you end at the cape and its lighthouse, in the other at the sanctuary and its stones. The Xunta publishes both. Choose by the ending you want, and by the light you want to see it in.

## Check before you go

Waymarks, diversions and the state of the paths change. Before you set out, check the Xunta's pages for the stage you are about to walk, and the app.
