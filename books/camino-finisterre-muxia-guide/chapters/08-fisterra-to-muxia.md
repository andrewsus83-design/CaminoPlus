# The last day: Fisterra to Muxía

## The character of the day

The Xunta rates this stage high, gives it ~28.7 km and estimates ~7 h 15 min. It is the longest day since Negreira. The sources differ: ~32 km in the Xunta's figures for the road walked the other way, ~29 km on Spain's official tourism website, ~31 km on one travel agency's page. Treat it as ~29 to ~31 km, and leave the whole day for it.

The day is mostly the sea: beaches, pinewoods, narrow quiet roads and a last climb. It ends at the sanctuary of Muxía, beside the water.

## From Fisterra to Lires

Leaving Fisterra can be confusing, the Xunta warns, because yellow arrows point in opposite directions for pilgrims going to Muxía and those arriving from it, so keep a map or the app open at the start. You go back to Langosteira beach and, at the cruceiro of Baixar, take the way to the parish of San Martiño de Duio. The Xunta says the church may refer to a vanished Roman city, Dugium, which Book III of the Codex Calixtinus mentions. The Concello de Fisterra gives the old story: the disciples carrying the apostle's remains asked leave of the local queen, Lupa, who sent them to the governor of Dugium. He prepared an ambush, but they discovered it and escaped.

The path runs past hamlets, woods and wheat fields, close to the sea. It comes to the sands of the beach of O Rostro among the pines, runs beside the beach, and climbs to the rural village of Padrís before the woods that lead to Lires. The first part, from Fisterra to Castrexe, is ~8.6 km and rated medium. The Xunta's text treats Lires as the end of the first part of the day, which makes it the natural place to break it if you need to. It stands on a ría that the Xunta says is rich in bird life, with the Romanesque churches of Frixe and Morquintián nearby. Whether you can stay there is a matter for the app and the phone.

## From Lires to Muxía

After Lires you cross the River Castro, on a bridge that the Xunta says was inaugurated in 2011 and made the ford easier for pilgrims. Then come Vaosilveiro, Frixe and Morquintián, with stretches of asphalt and country road, and the last real effort of the day: the climb to As Aferroas, at ~289 m, and to Mount Facho de Lourido, which the Xunta calls an old lookout where local people lit bonfires to warn of danger on this coast. Then you skirt the beach of Lourido and enter Muxía by the Rúa Campo das Pinas.

The Xunta notes two things on the way into town. One is the tourist office, where you can obtain the Muxiana, the end-of-Camino certificate. The other is the Camiño da Pel, "the Way of the Skin", a path round Mount Corpiño named for a fountain that has disappeared. The Concello de Muxía says pilgrims and the sick washed there before entering the sanctuary, to avoid being infected with leprosy. At the foot of the hill stands the church of Santa María de Muxía, Maritime Gothic, from the second half of the 14th century.

## The sanctuary of the Virxe da Barca

Beyond the town, beside the sea, stands the sanctuary of the Virxe da Barca, the Virgin of the Boat, a Baroque church of the 18th century according to the Xunta. The legend, which the Concello de Muxía and Turismo de Galicia both tell, is that the Virgin Mary arrived here in a boat of stone to give courage to the apostle James in his preaching. The stones round the church are the remains of the boat. The Pedra de Abalar, the swaying stone, is a granite mass of ~70 tons that tradition identifies with the hull, and which Turismo de Galicia says now swings only intermittently because of a fracture. The Pedra do Temón is the helm. The Pedra dos Cadrís is shaped like a kidney, and tradition says you must pass under it nine times to be healed. The Pedra dos Namorados is the stone of the lovers.

The tradition is old. The heritage report quotes Sebastian Ilsung of Augsburg, who in 1446 called the stone boat the greatest of the miracles he had seen on his journey, and who adds that he saw the stone being moved.

The sanctuary has burnt and been rebuilt. On Christmas Day 2013 a fire, which Wikipedia says was caused by lightning, destroyed the roof and the altarpiece and left only the outer walls and a few furnishings standing, the Concello says. The church has been rebuilt several times, it adds, the last in 2015, and the number of visitors has risen since the fire. A great pilgrimage to the Virxe da Barca takes place every September, which the Concello says is recognised as being of national tourist interest.

Close by stands *A Ferida*, "The Wound", a sculpture by Alberto Bañuelos-Fournier made in tribute to the volunteers who fought the oil of the Prestige in 2002, as Turismo de Galicia notes.

## The last of the light

Mount Corpiño, which the Concello de Muxía's pages put at ~67 to ~68 m, has a stone path to the top. The Concello says the view from it takes in the town, the sunset, the ría and the headland of Cape Vilán across the water. If you reach Muxía on a clear evening, that is the place to be. Take care on wet rock, here as at the other cape. The stones of the sanctuary are by the sea, and the sea is not tame.
