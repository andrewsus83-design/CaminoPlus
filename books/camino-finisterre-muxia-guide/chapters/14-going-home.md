# Going home

A short walk ends quickly, and ordinary life returns faster than the walk leaves you. Do not try to settle it all at once.

Keep three things. Keep the credencial and any certificate you collected. Keep the page you wrote on the cape, if you wrote one, and read it again in a month. Keep the walk itself in a small form: twenty minutes on foot each day, without a phone, is enough to remember what the road asked of you.

Do not worry if you cannot explain it. The sea is hard to describe to someone who was not there, and the shorter the walk, the harder it is to say what it changed. Say one thing you saw, and let that be enough.

The Pilgrim's Office, on its page for after the pilgrimage, offers two thoughts. Some pilgrims, it says, would like to give back to the Way as much as they received, perhaps as hosts at a hostel. And if you wish to continue the spiritual journey you began, it suggests that you may talk to your parish priest, or join a local association of St James.

If you walk again, a road that ends in Santiago is waiting. It may look different now that you have walked from the cathedral to the sea.
