# With the app, and sources

## With the CaminoPlus app

**CaminoPlus** is the offline-first companion app for the Camino de Santiago, with maps that work without signal, stays and cafes, a pilgrim passport with stamps, a journal and friends. The paid plan is called **Pilgrim Plus**. The app is still in development, so look in it for what is available on the road to Finisterre and Muxía today, and use it, not this book, for beds, cafes, closures, bus times and weather.

A reminder about the passport: the sources in this book ask for the stamped paper credencial. A passport in any app is a keepsake until an official source says otherwise.

## Sources and thanks

This book rests on public sources, and the fact ledger that goes with it lists every fact against the page it came from. The chief ones are:

- **The Xunta de Galicia** (caminodesantiago.gal): the Fisterra and Muxía Way, its stage pages and their lengths, the advice for walkers, and its news archive. Also a 2013 heritage report on the old roads of Santiago, on a Xunta site, for the history of the road.
- **The Concello de Fisterra**, the town council, and its pages on the end of the Way: the stages, the rites and the Fisterrana.
- **The Concello de Muxía** (concellomuxia.com): the sanctuary, the stones, the history of the town and the route from Santiago.
- **Turismo de Galicia**, the regional tourism board: the cape and the sanctuary.
- **Spain's official tourism website** (spain.info): the route's five stages and the Muxiana.
- **The Pilgrim's Reception Office of the Cathedral of Santiago** (oficinadelperegrino.com): the rules of the Compostela, the credencial, the pilgrimage's history and the page on life after it.
- **AEMET** and **MeteoGalicia**, the Spanish and Galician weather services, as places for a forecast.
- **Wikipedia**, for history and geography: the articles on Cape Finisterre, Fisterra, Muxía, the Costa da Morte, the Camino de Santiago, Decimus Junius Brutus Callaicus, the Jacobean Holy Year, Santiago airport and Galicia, and, in Spanish, the Finisterre lighthouse. The 1991 to 2020 figures for Santiago are Wikipedia's, from AEMET.
- **Galicia Press, El Debate and Vive Camino**, for what has been reported about the custom of burning boots, and one travel agency's route page, as a second view of the stages. Each is named where it is used.
- **A standard solar calculation**, for sunrise and sunset at the cape.
- **The CaminoPlus route catalog**, for the road's name, ends and ~90 km. The catalog gives no stage count.

Published lengths differ, sometimes by a few kilometres, and this book says so. If you know a better figure, or a fact that has changed, tell CaminoPlus.

Thanks go to the people who keep the Way: the associations of friends of the Camino, the hosts who open the hostels, and the people in the towns and villages who point the way and say *Buen Camino* to a stranger.

## A short glossary

- **Albergue**: a pilgrims' hostel with shared rooms.
- **Buen Camino**: "good Way", the greeting between pilgrims.
- **Camiño**: Galician for a path or way.
- **Concello**: a Galician town council.
- **Credencial**: the pilgrim's passport.
- **Cruceiro**: a Galician stone cross.
- **Fisterrana** and **Muxiana**: the certificates of Fisterra and Muxía.
- **Hórreo**: a granary on stone legs.
- **Hospitalero**: the host of a pilgrims' hostel.
- **Menú del peregrino**: a set meal for pilgrims.
- **Pazo**: a Galician manor house.
- **Ría**: a long sea inlet.
- **Sello**: a stamp in the credencial.
- **Xunta**: the regional government of Galicia.
