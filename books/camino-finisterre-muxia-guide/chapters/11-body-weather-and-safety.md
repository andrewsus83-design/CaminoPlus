# Body, weather and safety

This chapter is plain on purpose. Nothing here is a reason to be afraid of this road. It is a list of the things worth taking seriously on a short, exposed one.

## Feet and legs

Your feet decide how the walk goes. Stop at the first hot spot and dress it before it becomes a blister. The Xunta's advice is to keep feet clean and dry, change damp socks, and never start with new shoes. Rest a few minutes each hour, take your pack off, and eat and drink before you are hungry or thirsty. If you feel a sharp or growing pain in a knee, a shin or the top of a foot, stop: a day lost to rest is better than a week lost to an injury. For anything that worries you, see a doctor or a pharmacist.

## The exposed coast

The last three days are on the edge of the Atlantic. The Xunta describes a very rough sea off this shore, with hundreds of stories of shipwrecks. Plan for wind even on a fine day.

- **At the cape and at Muxía,** stay on the paths and behind the fences. The lighthouse stands ~120 m above the sea. Do not step onto wet rock for a photograph, at either place. The stones of the sanctuary are beside the water.
- **In the sea,** be careful. Spain's official tourism website calls Langosteira a good place for a swim, weather permitting. Weather permitting is the point. Swim only where and when it is clearly safe, never alone, never at dusk, never in rough water. If a flag, a sign or a local says no, the answer is no.
- **On the beach,** look at the tide before you choose it over the path in the dunes. The Concello de Fisterra publishes a tide page.
- **In fog,** slow down and keep to the marked way.

## Rain, cold and sun

The Xunta advises a waterproof garment on the Way at any time of year, and a cap against both sun and cold. Wet clothes cool you fast, even in mild weather. Carry a waterproof jacket, keep one dry layer in a bag that cannot get wet, and add a windproof layer for the cape. If you begin to shiver, stop somewhere warm before you try to go on. Do not start a long day in the dark; the chapter on when to go has the daylight figures. On a bright day, wear sunscreen and a hat, as the Xunta says.

## Roads, paths and fires

Most of the way is on paths and quiet roads. The Xunta describes stretches where asphalt and country roads alternate, and narrow quiet roads on the way to Muxía. Where you must walk on a road, face the oncoming traffic, keep to the edge and wear something bright. Never walk on a road in the dark.

Do not light fires, at the cape or anywhere else on the road. The Concello de Fisterra says burning has started forest fires, and the wind on the cape is strong. In a dry summer, obey any fire warning you see.

## When to stop

Tell someone where you will sleep each night, keep your phone charged and your map offline, and know the name of the last village you passed. If you are tired, ill or frightened by the weather, stop. The road will be there tomorrow, and so will the sea.

**In an emergency in Spain, call 112,** the European emergency number.
