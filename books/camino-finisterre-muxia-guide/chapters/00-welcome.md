# Welcome

Every other Camino ends in Santiago. This one begins there.

Leave the Praza do Obradoiro with the cathedral at your back and the road runs west to the Atlantic. The Xunta de Galicia, the regional government, says Cape Finisterre (in Galician, Fisterra) is ~89 km from Santiago and the sanctuary of the Virxe da Barca at Muxía ~87 km. That is three to five walking days, depending on how you cut them and which source you ask. It is the shortest road in this series, and in its way the strangest: you walk away from the goal everyone else has walked towards.

**The promise of this book is simple.** By the last page you will know what this road is, how to cut it into days, what the sea and the wind ask of you, what the official sources say about the certificates of the coast, and why the old custom of burning your boots at the cape is one to leave alone.

## How to use it

If you are a month or more away, read the next four chapters in order: the Way in brief, whether it suits you, when to go, and what to do before you go. Then read the days, and the chapter on ways to split them, which matters more here than on most routes because the sources do not agree on the number of days.

If you are on the road tonight, go to your stage. If you are only dreaming, read "Is this Camino for you?" and "The inner Way".

## What this book is not

It is not a live map, a timetable or a booking service. It names no hostels, cafes or shops, and gives no prices, opening hours or bed counts. They change, and a wrong one costs a tired person dearly. For those, use the CaminoPlus app and ask locally.

It is also **not a Compostela book**. The Compostela, the certificate of the Pilgrim's Office in Santiago, belongs to the walk that arrives at the cathedral. This walk begins where that one ends.

Every distance carries a tilde, **~**, because it is approximate. The official sources give different figures for the same stage, and the book says so where it matters. Where something depends on the season (a closure, a fire warning, the state of the sea), it says *check before you go*, and means it.

## A few words you will meet

*Buen Camino*, "good Way", is what pilgrims and locals say as you pass. An *albergue* is a pilgrims' hostel. The *credencial* is your pilgrim's passport, a card stamped along the way; each stamp is a *sello*. A *ría* is a long sea inlet, and an *hórreo* a Galician granary on stone legs. The glossary at the end has the rest.

Pack lightly, bring something windproof, and be on the cape in time for the light. *Buen Camino.*
