# Arriving, and the certificates

## What this walk is not

Start with what the official pages do not say. The Pilgrim's Office in Santiago issues the Compostela to those who come to the tomb of the Apostle for religious and/or spiritual reasons and follow the routes of the Way on foot, by bicycle or on horseback. On foot or on horseback, the Office asks for at least a hundred continuous kilometres on the same route to Santiago that it recognises, and says that the final stage must include at least the last one leading into the Cathedral. Your credencial, stamped along the way, is the proof. That is a walk that arrives at Santiago. This one begins there, and leaves.

The Office's pages read for this book say nothing about Fisterra or Muxía. This book therefore says nothing about what the Office does or does not record for this walk. If you want to know whether it would note your journey, in any form, ask it directly.

## The Fisterrana

The town of Fisterra has its own certificate. The Concello de Fisterra, the town council, writes on its page for the end of the Way that the *Fisterrana* is an official certification obtained at the municipal pilgrims' hostel and at the tourist office, certifying that you have reached "the end of the world". The route you have walked is shown by your credencial, with its stamps or signatures from parishes, hostels and other places on the road. For groups of more than ten people, the council asks you to contact the tourist office or the hostel first. The page gives no opening hours, and this book names none. Ask when you arrive, and have your credencial in your hand.

## The Muxiana

Muxía has its own as well, the *Muxiana*. Two official sources place it at the tourist office. The Xunta's stage text for the road into Muxía says that the Muxiana can be obtained at the tourist office, calls it an end-of-Camino credential, and adds that Fisterra provides the equivalent, the Fisterrana, which can be stamped at the municipal hostel. Spain's official tourism website says the same, and calls the Muxiana the Muxía pilgrim's certificate. No page read for this book says whether you need to have walked from Santiago, in which order you must take the two capes, or how many stamps you need. Ask at the tourist office.

## At the cape, and at Muxía

The cape is not the town: the Xunta's stage ends at the lighthouse, which it calls kilometre zero, and the way up leads past the church of Santa María das Areas. Give yourself the evening, and if you can, the next morning in no hurry. At Muxía, the Concello says that arriving at the sanctuary is the expected reward for the effort of reaching the end of the Way. Walk round the stones slowly. Many people simply stand and look.

## The way back

Do not leave getting back to chance. The Xunta's bus site, bus.gal, is where to look for the times of public buses, and your hostel or the tourist office can tell you what runs this week. Do not plan a flight or a train home for the afternoon of your last walking day. If you have a day to spare, spend it by the sea. The walk will be over soon enough.
