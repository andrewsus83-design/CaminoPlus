# Day 7. A light for the night

*How much of the road do you need to see?*

## The passage

> Our faith is a light by nature coming of our endless Day, that is our Father, God. In which light our Mother, Christ, and our good Lord, the Holy Ghost, leadeth us in this passing life. This light is measured discreetly, needfully standing to us in the night. … For we, with mercy and grace, steadfastly know and believe our light, going therein wisely and mightily. And at the end of woe, suddenly our eyes shall be opened, and in clearness of light our sight shall be full: which light is God, our Maker and Holy Ghost, in Christ Jesus our Saviour. Thus I saw and understood that our faith is our light in our night: which light is God, our endless Day.
>
> — Julian of Norwich, *Revelations of Divine Love*, chapter 83 (ed. Grace Warrack, 1901)

## Reflection

If you ever set out in the dark with a head torch, you know the small round room of light it makes. A few paces of path, a patch of grass, a stone that you would otherwise have turned your ankle on. Beyond it, nothing. Behind it, nothing. Yet you walk, and the room walks with you, and the path turns out to be there, one lit stretch after another.

It is a strange way to cross a country, and it is how most of a long road is crossed. You are never shown the whole of it. You are shown enough.

Julian reaches for the same picture near the end of her book, though she does it with faith and not with a battery. Our faith, she says, is a light that comes *of our endless Day*: the sun, as it were, from the other side of the hill, throwing a first grey up the sky before you can see it. And this light, she adds, is *measured discreetly, needfully standing to us in the night*. Measured: given out in the right amount. Discreetly: with judgement, not wastefully, not stingily. Needfully standing to us: there as much as is needed, like a lamp on a table in a dark house.

In the next chapter she is more exact still. *Neither is the light so large that we may see our blissful Day, nor is it shut from us.* That sentence is the one to carry. The light is not so large that you can see the end. Nor is it shut: you are not left in the dark. What you get is neither the whole view nor nothing. It is a lamp. Anyone who has walked before dawn knows that the light grows on the path ahead of you a stretch at a time, and never in one piece.

That is a hard thing to be told on the seventh day of a walk. The novelty has gone. The finish is not in sight. You may have a long middle to walk, with the road looking much as it did yesterday and the day before. This is where patience comes in. Julian knew about long patience. The showings that came to her took a few hours of one morning and a night, and she spent years trying to understand them. She says herself that *fifteen years after, and more* she was given the meaning. In chapter 64, where she is thinking about the end of life, she writes that a person *lengtheneth his patience over the time of his living*. The word she chooses is "lengtheneth": patience that is stretched, as a road is stretched, over distance.

Now to the hard places. First, a sentence we left out. Between the two parts of the passage, Julian says that the light is the cause of our life, and the night is the cause of our pain and our woe, *in which we earn meed and thanks of God*. "Meed" is an old word for reward. It is her medieval way of saying, as we read it, that the night is not wasted, and it is hard to hear if the night is only night to you, a grief with no reward in sight. We left it out of the passage, between the two parts, but it is there in the chapter for anyone who wants it. Nothing in the rest of today's passage depends on it.

The second is the word "faith". If you are walking without it, the light in this passage may seem to describe something you do not have. Take whatever light you do have, the torch, the map, the next arrow, the person ahead of you on the path, and read the picture with that. The shape of it still holds. You are given enough for the next stretch, and no more.

The third is the nights when the light seems too small even for that. Julian does not hide these. In chapter 15 she writes of a time when *there was no comfort nor none ease to me but faith, hope, and charity; and these I had in truth, but little in feeling.* A lamp that is lit but hardly felt is still a lamp, though it does not feel like one.

What does it ask of an ordinary day? A particular kind of patience: to take the road in the lengths the light allows. Choose a post, a tree, a bend ahead, and walk to it as if it were the whole journey. When you reach it, choose another. This is no trick to get you to the end faster. It is a way of being where you are, in the one room of light that you have, with the whole of the path ahead still hidden and quite likely to be there.

## For the walk

- How much of the road do I actually need to see to take the next step?
- For ten minutes, walk only to the next thing you can see, a post, a wall, a bend. When you reach it, pick another and let the rest of the road go dark. If your mind runs ahead to the end, bring it back to the lit patch under your feet. If it is already light, picture the head torch. The circle it makes is the same size.

## At the end of the day

When dusk comes down in a village, the windows come on one at a time, each with its own small yellow room of light. You can tell a good deal about a place from them: where there is a kitchen, where there is a television, where somebody is up late. Each lit window is not much. Together they make a street you could find your way along.

Think of where today's patience held and where it ran out. There is usually a point, mid-afternoon or thereabouts, when it frays: the hill that is longer than the sign said, the path that goes on straight for an hour, the clock that does not move. What did you do when that point came? Did you pick the next marker, or push through it with your jaw set, or sit down? None of these is wrong. Notice only which came naturally.

Notice, too, whether you were comparing: with the number you meant to have walked by now, with the pace of someone who passed you, with the person you were on the first day. A measured light does not measure you against anybody. It shows the next stretch of your own path, and that is all it was ever for.

Think about what you were trying to see the end of. It might be the day. It might be the walk. It might be something quite different, a decision waiting at home, a grief you hoped would be lighter by now, a question about where your life goes next. The light in the passage is measured for the next step, and it does not tell you what the end looks like. That may be a relief or a frustration. Tonight, both are allowed.

On a quiet road the light can seem smaller, because there are fewer other lamps: nobody ahead on the path, nobody behind. If you walked alone today, the lamp was yours alone, and you carried it all day. That is something.

If the light today seemed too small, say that without apology. Julian did. A walker whose faith is there but little felt is in good company.

Before you sleep, do the kind of small thing that a lit window stands for. Put the torch, or the phone, where your hand will find it in the dark. Lay out tomorrow's first layer. Fill the bottle. None of it is a plan for the whole road. It is a lamp set ready on the table, and it makes the morning a good deal kinder.

You may be tired in the particular way of the seventh day, when the body has settled into its rhythm and the mind has run out of novelty. Let the evening be small. A lit window, a warm drink, a bed.

- Where did I want more light today than I was given, and what did the light I had actually show me?
- When did my patience run out, and what did it need at that moment?
- What am I trying to see the end of, and can I leave it dark for tonight?

## A short prayer

*Give me enough light for the next step. Let me not ask for the whole road tonight. Be patient with me where my patience is thin. Hold the dark ahead, and keep the lamp lit by the bed. In the morning, let there be a little more.*
