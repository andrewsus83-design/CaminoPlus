# Carry it with you

You have come to the end of the eight days, or you are stopping early, or you mean to go back to a day that you skipped. Any of these is fine. The book is not a course, and nobody is marking it. It was made to be a little company on a quiet road, and company does not have to stay once the road is done.

## Eight small things

You cannot take the whole book with you, and you should not try. But eight small things fit in a pocket, and each of them is something you have already held once.

- The hazelnut: made, loved, kept.
- The layers: cloth, skin, flesh, bone, heart, and something around all of them that does not wear out.
- The open door: homely and courteous together, being welcomed and welcoming.
- The question without an answer, which is allowed to walk beside you.
- The promise without a plan: that nothing, down to the least thing, is forgotten.
- The prayer that tastes of nothing, and counts.
- The lamp that shows the next step, and no more.
- The beginning that came before the first step.

Pick one. Not the best one, the one that cost you least and gave you most. You can do without the others for a while.

## Going on

If you would like more of Julian, she is easy to find. The edition we have used, from 1901, is free at Project Gutenberg as ebook number 52958, and it is the same text every passage here was checked against. Her chapters are short, and the book can be read a chapter at a time, as it was once read by people with a long day ahead of them. You will find our eight days in chapters 5 to 7 and 77, 27 to 32, 41 and 83 to 86. The editor's long introduction is not needed, and you can skip it.

A warning is fair. Much of the book is about the suffering of Christ, seen in visions she describes without softening. We have left those chapters aside. They are hers, and they will mean most to readers who share her faith. If you read on, read what helps, and pass quietly over what does not.

Be honest, too, about what did not work. A day that left you cold on the road may speak to you in a kitchen in March. A passage that asked for more belief than you had is not a test you failed. The book is not a test. If something hardly touched you, nothing has gone wrong.

## A last word

Julian spent her life, as far as we know, in a small room beside a church, with a window onto the services and a window onto the street. It is hard to think of a life with less road in it. Yet in chapter 81 she writes that God shewed Himself in the earth *thus as it were in pilgrimage*, here with us, leading us, until we are brought to the end. She did not need to walk to know what a walker knows: that the going is the thing, and that someone is in it.

If you remember only one sentence, the one she was given is short enough. It is all right to say it quietly, to nobody in particular, on an ordinary afternoon with the road in front of you and no sight of the end: *all manner of thing shall be well.*

Walk again, when you can. Or tomorrow, round the block, with your phone left behind and your hands open.

*Buen Camino.*
