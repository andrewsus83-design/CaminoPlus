# Carry it with you

You have come to the end of the eight days, or you are stopping early, or you mean to go back to a day that you skipped. Any of these is fine. The book is not a course, and nobody is marking it. It was made to be a little company on a quiet road, and company does not have to stay once the road is done.

## Eight small things

Eight small things fit in a pocket, and each of them is something you have already held once.

- The hazelnut: made, loved, kept.
- The layers: cloth, skin, flesh, bone, heart, and something around all of them that does not wear out.
- The open door: homely and courteous together, being welcomed and welcoming.
- The question without an answer, which is allowed to walk beside you.
- The promise without a plan: that nothing, down to the least thing, is forgotten.
- The prayer that tastes of nothing, and counts.
- The lamp that shows the next step, and no more.
- The beginning that came before the first step.

Pick one, and let the others go for now. Choose the one you can remember without looking. It will do more for you than all eight kept in a drawer.

## Going on

If you would like more of Julian, she is easy to find. The edition we have used, from 1901, is on Project Gutenberg as ebook number 52958, and it is the same text every passage here was checked against. Her chapters are short, and the book can be read a chapter at a time. Our eight days come from chapters 5, 6, 7, 27, 29, 31, 32, 41, 77, 83 and 86. The editor's long introduction is not needed, and you can skip it.

A warning is fair. Much of the book is about the suffering of Christ, seen in visions she describes without softening. We have left those chapters aside. They are hers, and they will mean most to readers who share her faith. If you read on, read what helps, and pass quietly over what does not.

Some of it will not have worked, and that is fair. A day that left you cold on the road may speak to you in a kitchen in March. A passage that asked for more belief than you had was not a test you failed. If something hardly touched you, put it down. Nothing has gone wrong.

## A last word

Julian wrote from a small room beside a church, with, as the edition's introduction describes such rooms, a window onto the services and another onto the world. It is hard to think of a life with less road in it. Yet in chapter 81 she writes that God shewed Himself in the earth *thus as it were in pilgrimage*, here with us, leading us, until we are brought to the end. It is a walker's picture, from someone who sat still.

If you remember only one sentence, the one she was given is short enough. It is all right to say it quietly, to nobody in particular, on an ordinary afternoon with the road in front of you and no sight of the end: *all manner of thing shall be well.*

Walk again, when you can. Or tomorrow, to the end of your own street and back, slowly, with nothing to prove.

*Buen Camino.*
