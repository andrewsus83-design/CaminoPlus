# How to use this book

This is a small, quiet book for eight days. It is made to go with the Camino de Invierno, the Winter Way, which the app lists as running from Ponferrada to Santiago de Compostela, ~260 km. *Invierno* is the Spanish word for winter. The name belongs to the road. You can read the book in any season, on the road or at home.

The app's own route notes call it a road with few pilgrims, and advise planning where you will sleep ahead. That is worth knowing here. A quiet road gives you long hours alone, and some evenings in a village where you may be the only walker at the table. This book is made for those hours. It is quiet and consoling, and keeps you company without talking much.

## A day

Each day has a morning reading of about five minutes and an evening reflection of about three.

**The morning** comes in three parts.

- **The passage.** A few lines from *Revelations of Divine Love*, written in English in the fourteenth century by Julian of Norwich. The words are exactly as they stand in the 1901 edition we use, old forms and all. Read them twice, the second time aloud if you can, or under your breath. The old English slows you down, and that is a large part of what it is for.
- **The reflection.** This part is ours, not Julian's. It starts from something on the road, a coat, a head torch, a door, and lets the passage open it. It says plainly what the passage says. It says where the passage is hard, and it is allowed to doubt.
- **For the walk.** A question to carry for an hour, and one small practice that needs nothing but attention. No notebook is needed. Most of them are better done on your feet.

**The evening** has two parts.

- **At the end of the day.** A look at the day just gone, with the morning's passage still in mind. It names what may have been hard and what may have been good, and it ends with two or three gentle questions. You do not have to answer them in writing. They are for lying down with.
- **A short prayer.** Original, and short. If you pray, pray it. If you pray differently, change the words. If you do not pray, say it as a wish, to nobody in particular.

## A Christian book, and who it is for

Julian was a Christian of the fourteenth century, and she writes as one. She speaks of God, of Christ, of the Church and of sin in the terms of her own time, and she hopes her readers will take them as she does. We say so now so that nobody is surprised. People walk the Camino for many reasons: faith, sport, grief, curiosity, a rest from work. All of them are welcome here. You do not need her belief to recognise what she noticed about being small, being held, being afraid and being tired. Where she is hard to follow, we say so.

## The shape of the eight days

The days follow a walk. They do not follow the order of her book.

- **Day 1.** The smallness of things.
- **Day 2.** Being held.
- **Day 3.** The courtesy of God.
- **Day 4.** Sin and suffering, told honestly.
- **Day 5.** "All shall be well".
- **Day 6.** The way of prayer.
- **Day 7.** The long patience.
- **Day 8.** The end, and the beginning.

At 20 to 25 km a day, ~260 km takes somewhere between ten and thirteen days. Eight days will not match every day of such a walk, and they do not need to. Pick up a day when it suits you: a rest day, a day of rain, a day when the road is hard. Repeat one that helped. Read two together if you like. If you are worn out, read the passage and the prayer and leave the rest.

## Where to read it

The morning part suits a bench, a bunk, or a café table while the soup comes. Then put the phone away and carry the question with you for an hour. Keep the evening part for last, when the boots are off. At home it works the same way, with tea in the morning and a lamp at night.

Two words you may meet. An *albergue* is a simple pilgrim hostel, often with bunks and sometimes a shared kitchen. *Buen Camino* is the greeting between walkers on the road, roughly, may your Way go well. Julian's own words are explained as they come up. She says *shewing* for what we would call a vision, and *homely* for something close to "at home".

Read slowly.
