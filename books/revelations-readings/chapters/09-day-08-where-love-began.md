# Day 8. Where love began

*What was begun in you before you took the first step?*

## The passage

> *Learn it well: Love was His meaning. Who shewed it thee? Love. What shewed He thee? Love. Wherefore shewed it He? For Love. Hold thee therein and thou shalt learn and know more in the same. But thou shalt never know nor learn therein other thing without end.* … And I saw full surely that ere God made us He loved us; which love was never slacked, nor ever shall be. And in this love He hath done all His works; and in this love He hath made all things profitable to us; and in this love our life is everlasting. In our making we had beginning; but the love wherein He made us was in Him from without beginning: in which love we have our beginning. And all this shall we see in God, without end.
>
> — Julian of Norwich, *Revelations of Divine Love*, chapter 86 (ed. Grace Warrack, 1901)

## Reflection

At some point on every long walk there is a morning when you notice that you are close to an ending. It may be today. It may be some days off, with the number of kilometres now small enough to count on the fingers of a few hands. Either way, the feeling starts to turn. For a week the road was a thing you were in. Now it is becoming a thing you will have done.

Julian was at that point when she wrote the chapter we have been reading from. It is the last chapter of her book. And the first thing she says in it is not "this is done" but the opposite: *This book is begun by God's gift and His grace, but it is not yet performed, as to my sight.* The book finishes by saying that it is not finished. It is worth reading that again slowly, standing up, perhaps with your hand on a gate.

Then she goes back to the question she had been carrying for fifteen years and more. What was it for? What did it all mean? And she is answered, as she says, in her understanding, with a little catechism of five lines. What did he mean? Love. Who showed it to you? Love. What did he show you? Love. Why did he show it? For love. And then the instruction: hold yourself in that, and you shall learn and know more in the same. But you shall never learn anything else, in it, without end.

You can hear how plain it is. After all the visions, the sights of suffering and glory, the long wrestling with sin and pain, the whole thing comes down to a sentence a child could say. And she says it took fifteen years and more to be given it. It is not an answer to be hurried to.

The second half of the passage turns to beginnings. Before God made us, she writes, He loved us, and that love was never slackened and never will be. In our making we had beginning; but the love in which we were made was in Him from without beginning. And *in which love we have our beginning*.

Think what that does to the idea of an ending. If the love you were made in came before your beginning, then there was no first step, in the deepest sense. You did not start this walk from nothing. Whatever it was that turned you towards the road, a grief, a restlessness, a curiosity, a wish for quiet, had already begun in you, and had a history longer than your wish to walk. You did not start the Camino. It found you in the middle of something that was already going on.

There are hard places in the passage, and the end of a book is a fair place to say them. The first is *never know nor learn therein other thing without end*. To a seeker, that can sound like a door closing. Is there to be no more explanation? She does not apologise. She is not saying that nothing else can be learned. She is saying that nothing deeper can be, and that is a different claim, though it is hard to feel on a day when you want to understand why a particular thing happened.

The second is that love as the meaning of everything does not explain the hurt. It is not an argument. It does not account for plague, or a death, or the weather of a bad year. Read after the other days, it is not trying to. It comes at the end of a book that has looked at the hurt without flinching, and it comes after fifteen years, not fifteen minutes. If it sounds too easy, it does not come from ease.

A third is more personal. You may not have found any such meaning in your own walk. It may have been plain walking: feet, weather, sleep. That is a respectable thing to have found. The five questions are Julian's, about Julian's showings. You do not have to answer them with her answer. You may answer *I don't know yet*, and she would understand: she waited fifteen years.

What does it ask of an ordinary day? Perhaps just a different relation to the ending. Not "I have finished", but "I have been begun". A road is not used up when you reach the end of it. In another place she writes that God shewed Himself in the earth *thus as it were in pilgrimage*, here with us, leading us. If that is so, then the leading does not stop at the last yellow arrow.

## For the walk

- If a child asked me why I am here, who or what set me on this road, what has it shown me, and why did I come, what would I say in one word each?
- Take the three questions, Who, What, Wherefore, and put them to the road. Let each answer be a single word, and do not look for a clever one. Say them to the next three markers you pass, one word each, and see if the words keep.

## At the end of the day

This is the last evening of the book. It may or may not be the last evening of your walk. Either way, a certain tenderness is permitted. Eight days ago you opened a small thing, and it may have grown a little, in the way that a good companion does, without ever being much trouble.

Look back not just over today but over the eight days. The first day was a nut in a palm. Then a layer of cloth, a door that opened, a hurt that did not have an answer, a sentence on a mug that turned out to have a history. Then a dry prayer, and a little light for a dark morning. If you were to pick one of them to keep, which would it be? It need not be the one you expected. A person can walk a long way for the sake of one image.

Be honest about what did not land. Some days will have been flat. Some passages will have asked for a belief that you do not hold, or a trust that you did not have to give. That is not a failure of the road or the book. Take what helped and leave the rest in the bunk.

And look at the question of the beginning once more. When did this start? Not the day you booked, or the day you set out, but the first moment you wanted something like this. Where was it, and what was it wanting? You might find that the walk is the reply to something older than the walk.

You are allowed to be tired and to be done. An ending is a little like going out of a lit room into a dark yard. There is a moment of nothing, and then the eyes adjust and there are stars.

- What from these eight days do I want to keep?
- What was already begun in me before I set out?
- Where will I begin again tomorrow?

## A short prayer

*Thank you for the road, and for what began before it. Let me carry the little I have understood, and not be afraid of the rest. Let the ending be a beginning. Keep what I have loved. Be with me tomorrow, wherever the first step falls.*
