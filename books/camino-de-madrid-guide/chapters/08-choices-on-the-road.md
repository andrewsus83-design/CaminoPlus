# Choices on the road

## How many days?

The sources do not agree. The Confraternity of St James says about two weeks without rest days. The Comunidad de Madrid's tourism pages say the road is completed in ten stages to Sahagún, four of them from Madrid to Segovia, which means days of ~32 km on average, by simple division. The Junta's guide recommends an average of ~25 to ~30 km. This book uses thirteen, between towns about a day apart. Neither printed guide divides the road into stages, so no division is official, and many pilgrims cut it differently.

## Halfway points on the long days

These are places on the road, not promises of a bed. A split is only useful if you can sleep at the end of it, so ask the app and the Association's hostel notes before you decide. Distances come from the Junta's table. The Association's figures agree within a kilometre or two, except for Moral de la Reina, which it puts ~14 km from Medina de Rioseco.

- **Day 2, Tres Cantos to Manzanares el Real:** Colmenar Viejo, ~12 km from the start and ~16 km before the end.
- **Day 4, Cercedilla to Segovia:** on the direct way there is no village. The detour east from the Cruz de la Gallega to Valsaín and La Granja, which the Junta's table follows, puts La Granja ~18 km from Cercedilla and ~13 km from Segovia.
- **Day 5, Segovia to Santa María la Real de Nieva:** Añe, ~21 km from Segovia and ~11 km from the end.
- **Day 8, Alcazarén to Puente Duero:** Valdestillas, ~16 km and ~8 km.
- **Day 11, Medina de Rioseco to Cuenca de Campos:** Moral de la Reina, ~19 km and ~8 km on the Junta's table.

## If the sierra is closed to you

The Association's guide says that when it snows the Puerto de la Fuenfría can be impassable. In that case it advises using the road and crossing the range by the nearby Navacerrada pass, which, it says, has snowploughs all year. That is a road, with traffic, not a path. The Confraternity says you can instead take the train from Cercedilla to Segovia. Decide in Cercedilla, with the forecast and the local news, not on the pass. The Confraternity's rule is simple: avoid the sierra in snow or bad weather.

## Simancas or Valladolid

At Puente Duero the Junta's guide gives two ways. The main one goes ~6 km to Simancas. The other goes ~12 km into Valladolid, the regional capital, then ~14 km from the city to Simancas, by a local road or by tracks. That makes the Valladolid way ~20 km longer. The Association's guide also offers a track from the city straight to Ciguñuela, with a steep climb at the start.

Valladolid is the seat of the regional government, since 1983 according to the Association's guide, and its cathedral dates from the late sixteenth century, the Junta says.

## Side trips and joining roads

- **La Santa Espina**, the monastery founded in 1147, is ~6 km from Castromonte on day 10, the Junta's guide says.
- **The Camino Mendocino** begins in the province of Guadalajara and joins the Camino de Madrid at Manzanares el Real, the Comunidad de Madrid says. From there its walkers share the road with you.
- **Sahagún and beyond.** The Camino de Madrid ends where it meets the Camino Francés. You may go home from Sahagún, or walk on. *The French Way* in this series covers the road from there.
