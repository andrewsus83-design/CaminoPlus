# The inner Way

People walk for many reasons: faith, sport, grief, curiosity, a rest from work, a promise, a milestone birthday. None of them is the wrong one, and you do not have to explain yours to anyone on the road.

## A road of solitude

This road asks something different from a crowded Camino. The Confraternity of St James says it appeals to people who want solitude and the wide horizons of the *meseta*. Very few pilgrims walk it, so the company you keep is mostly your own, the sky, and the people of the villages you pass.

That can be a gift or a weight. Some days the plain will feel like room to think, and some days like a long empty hour. Both are normal, and both pass.

## Leaving from home

Most Caminos begin with a journey: a plane, a train, a first night in a strange town. This one can begin at your own front door. The Association's guide says the road was recovered so that a pilgrim from Madrid could set out for Compostela from the *puerta de casa*, the door of home. If you live in Madrid, you may find that leaving the place you know best asks more of you than leaving one you do not know at all.

## Those who walked it before

Shepherds and harvesters used this road long before pilgrims did. For almost five hundred years, until the railway came, Galician reapers walked these tracks to cut the grain of Castile, and the Association's guide calls them the nearest ancestors of today's pilgrims here. They were not looking for a certificate. They walked for work, a long way from home.

You may find it a good company to keep when the path is hard. People crossed this plain on foot for much plainer reasons than yours, season after season.

The Association says it marked the road in a pilgrim spirit and not as a hiker's route, and adds that nobody minds if you begin as a walker. You may end as something else, or you may not.

## Questions for the walk

Choose one a day. Do not answer it before lunch.

- What are you carrying that you could leave behind?
- What does the empty plain ask you to stop doing?
- Who would you like to walk this stretch with, and what would you say to them?
- What are you walking towards, and what are you walking away from?
- What do you notice when no one is watching?
- When did you last do something slowly, and what happened?

## One small practice

On a day of long straight track, walk an hour without your phone or music. Notice what you hear: your own feet, wind in the crops, a tractor far off. At the end of the day, write down three lines about it. They do not have to be good.
