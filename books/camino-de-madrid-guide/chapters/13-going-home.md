# Going home

If you walked on from Sahagún, *The French Way* has taken you the rest of the way, and this chapter belongs to a later day. If you stopped in Sahagún, you have walked ~325 km on the Junta's figures, and that is a Camino whatever the paperwork says.

## The first week back

Expect the quiet to feel odd. After days when the only schedule was your own legs, a calendar full of meetings can feel loud. Give yourself a slow first day, if you can. Sleep. Unpack your pack on the floor, as the hostels taught you.

Look at your credencial. The stamps from Madrid, Segovia and the small villages in between are a map of where you were, and many walkers keep them for years.

## Keeping the road alive

- **Write it down** while the days are still sharp: the long gaps, the pass, the plain. A few lines each evening and a longer note on your first day at home.
- **Stay in touch** with the people you met, in the hostels or on the way.
- **Join a local association.** The Madrid association says it organises a weekend walk on the Caminos of Spain, Portugal and France in most months from September to June, and that many of its members serve as volunteer hosts in hostels that run on donations. Its website has the details, and other cities have associations of their own.
- **Walk again.** Sahagún is a good place to have stopped, because the next day is waiting there: the Camino Francés carries on towards Santiago whenever you are ready.

The road will still be there. The Association says its volunteers go over the yellow arrows every spring.
