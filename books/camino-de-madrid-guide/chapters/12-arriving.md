# Arriving

## The end of this road

This road ends in Sahagún, not in Santiago. You arrive on the thirteenth day, past the sanctuary of the Virgen Peregrina, into a small town of about 2,400 inhabitants, Wikipedia says, between two rivers. The Association's guide says that it grew around a monastery of the martyrs Facundus and Primitivus, of which little remains. The Junta's guide calls Sahagún a major milestone on the Pilgrims' Route, the end of the seventh stage in the Codex Calixtinus. Wikipedia says it holds some of the earliest examples of Mudéjar architecture, a Spanish style that joins Islamic decoration to Christian buildings.

You leave it, the Association's guide says, over the Puente de Cantos on the river Cea. That is where the Camino de Madrid becomes the Camino Francés. From there Santiago is ~355 km on the Association's count and ~364 km on the Junta's, which is ~12 to ~15 days at the Junta's pace. *The French Way*, in this series, takes the road from here.

## The Compostela

The Compostela is the certificate the Pilgrim's Office in Santiago gives to those who meet its rules. The Office owns the rules, and they can change. This book says what the Office says, and sends you to the Office in any doubt.

- The Compostela is for those who come to the tomb of the Apostle for religious and/or spiritual reasons and follow the routes of the Way of St James on foot, by bicycle or on horseback.
- It asks for at least the last hundred kilometres on foot or on horseback, or the last two hundred kilometres by bicycle, shown by the credencial, stamped along the route.
- On foot or on horseback, that means at least a hundred continuous kilometres on the same route to Santiago that the Office recognises. The final stage must include at least the last one leading into the cathedral.
- You may walk the Way in stages, if they follow in chronological and geographical order. A pilgrim who walks only the minimum distance must get the credencial stamped at the start and at the end of each stage, with the date.
- A pilgrim who has already walked part of a recognised route outside Spain needs no less than seventy kilometres in Spain.
- The Cabildo, the cathedral's governing council, reserves the right to grant the Compostela in special and exceptional cases.

The Office awards the Compostela to those who walk for religious or spiritual reasons, and it describes the pilgrimage it has in mind as one made "with a Christian sentiment, even if it is only with an attitude of search". If that does not describe your reason for walking, ask the Pilgrim's Office what it can give you: it also offers a Certificate of Distance, which records the kilometres you walked from wherever you started.

## What this means from Madrid

From Madrid you have ~320 km to Sahagún and then ~355 to ~365 km to Santiago, so ~675 to ~690 km in all. That is far more than the last hundred kilometres the Office asks for. Those last hundred kilometres lie on the Camino Francés, beyond Sahagún, and *The French Way* covers them.

The Office issues the Compostela in Santiago, and its rule asks for the final stage to include the last steps into the cathedral. If you walk only the Camino de Madrid, you finish in Sahagún, short of it. The Office's pages on the Compostela, the credencial and the Certificate of Distance, read for this book, speak of "any of the routes recognized by the International Pilgrim's Reception Office system", and they do not name the Camino de Madrid. If you plan to walk in stages, or you want to know how the Office treats a walk that began in Madrid, ask the Pilgrim's Office before you go. This book does not say whether any other start counts.

Keep your credencial from Madrid with you the whole way, and have it stamped as you go. The Office asks for it stamped along the route.

## At the Pilgrim's Office

The Office issues the Compostela, a document in Latin. Procedures and hours change, so look at the Office's own website before you arrive in Santiago, and do not plan a tight connection for the same day.

Whatever the Compostela means to you, it is a record of a road you walked, not a measure of it.

## Your day in Sahagún

Do not leave on the morning after you arrive.

- **Rest.** You have walked ~325 km on the Junta's figures.
- **Look around.** The Association's guide names the arch of San Benito and the chapel of San Mando, both remains of the old monastery, and several Mudéjar churches.
- **Decide.** If you walk on, the next day is the Camino Francés. If you go home, plan your way with the timetables in front of you.
