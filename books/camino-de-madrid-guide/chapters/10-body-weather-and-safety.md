# Body, weather and safety

This chapter is plain on purpose. On a quiet road you are your own first help, so read it before the first day, and again before the pass.

## The sierra

The climb to the Puerto de la Fuenfría is ~650 m in ~8 km, on a Roman road, and the day is ~31 km long. The Confraternity of St James says the sierra has mountain weather, that snow can lie there until May, and that there are no farms, villages or facilities on it. Its advice is to avoid the sierra in snow or bad weather. The Association's guide adds that fog is a hazard, here and on the plains, and that the pass can be impassable in snow.

- Decide in Cercedilla, not on the pass. Look at the forecast and at the sky.
- Start early, so the crossing is in daylight with time in hand.
- Carry water, food, a waterproof and a warm layer, even in summer.
- Know the alternatives (the choices chapter): the road over the Navacerrada pass, or the train from Cercedilla to Segovia.
- The stretch from the upper Fuenfría valley to the edge of Segovia lies inside the Sierra de Guadarrama National Park, the Association's guide says, so follow the park's rules, and light no fire.

The descent is long and gentle, but it is long. Walk it slowly, for your knees.

## Heat and sun

The Confraternity says July to early September is extremely hot, with long stretches of the road having absolutely no shade. That is the main danger of this road, more than the hills.

- Start at first light and aim to finish before the afternoon.
- Carry more water than you think you need, and fill up at the last village before a long gap: the sierra, and the ~18 km before Alcazarén.
- Wear a hat and sun cream. The Junta's guide advises against walking in only a T-shirt and shorts at the start.
- Do not plan your longest days for the hottest week. Split them, or rest.
- If you feel dizzy, sick or confused, stop in shade, drink, and ask for help. If it does not pass, call 112, the European emergency number.

## Cold, rain and fog

In winter the Association's guide says fog can be intense and persistent in the sierra and in Castile, and that you should carry a means of orientation, or use the nearest road, with the usual care. A waterproof that covers your pack, the leaflets say, is part of the basic kit. Daylight is short, about 9 hours at midwinter, so a long day needs an early start.

## Sand

In the pine woods the sandy ground slows you. The Association's guide warns that forcing the pace on it risks injury, whether you walk, cycle or ride. Shorten your stride, let the pace fall, and plan fewer kilometres on the days with the most woodland.

## Roads and streams

Only a small part of the way is on tarmac, but it is not nothing: the Association counts ~42 km of town streets, ~11 km of them in Madrid. The Junta's guide reminds you that on roads you are the most vulnerable, and that drivers may not see a person on foot.

- Walk facing the traffic, keep to the edge, and wear something bright.
- Near the boundary between Medina de Rioseco and Berrueces the Association's guide has a few hundred metres on the verge of the N-601. Take care there.
- On day 2 the path crosses the Tejada stream on stepping blocks. In rain the water can cover them, and the guide says to take your shoes off to cross.

## Fire

The Confraternity says wild camping in the pine woods is not recommended, because of the risk of fire. Treat that as a rule: do not light a fire, and do not smoke where the ground is dry.

## On your own

Very few pilgrims walk this road, so you may be alone for hours.

- Tell someone at home your plan, as the Junta's guide advises, and send a message each evening.
- Charge your phone, save your maps, and do not count on a signal everywhere.
- Look after your feet. The Association's leaflets advise petroleum jelly and technical socks, and stopping at the first rubbing to look at it.
- Stop when you need to. A short day is never a failure, and a rest day is part of the plan.
- See a doctor about anything that worries you, before you leave and on the road. Carry your health card, or your European health card if you come from abroad.
