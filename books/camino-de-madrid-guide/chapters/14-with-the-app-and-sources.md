# With the app, and sources

## With the CaminoPlus app

**CaminoPlus** is the offline-first companion app for the Camino de Santiago, with maps that work without signal, stays and cafes, a pilgrim passport with stamps, a journal and friends. The paid plan is called **Pilgrim Plus**. The app is still in development. The Camino de Madrid is on its list of routes, from Madrid to Sahagún, but this book makes no claim about what the app holds for it today, so look in the app, and use it, not this book, for beds, cafes, closures and weather.

A reminder about the passport: the Pilgrim's Office asks for the stamped credencial. A passport in CaminoPlus, or in any app other than the Office's own digital credential, is a keepsake until the Office says otherwise.

## Sources and thanks

This book rests on public sources, and its fact ledger lists every fact against the page it came from. The chief ones are:

- **The Junta de Castilla y León** (turismocastillayleon.com): its tourism pages on the Camino de Santiago de Madrid and on the Madrid cycling route, read in Spanish, and its pilgrim guide *The Madrid Route: A Practical Guide for Pilgrims* (Spanish original *Guía del Peregrino del Camino de Madrid*), published by the Fundación Siglo para el Turismo y las Artes de Castilla y León, read in the English edition of June 2020.
- **The Asociación de Amigos de los Caminos de Santiago de Madrid** (demadridalcamino.com): its guide *Camino de Madrid a Santiago de Compostela: Cartografía e itinerario Madrid-Sahagún* (first edition, July 2014, in Spanish), its pages on the credencial and on who it is, its seasonal leaflets of 2021, and the title and date of its hostel-status notice of 1 October 2026.
- **The Comunidad de Madrid**, on its tourism site (visitmadrid.es): the page on the Camino de Santiago in the Comunidad, in Spanish.
- **The Confraternity of St James** (csj.org.uk): its page on the Camino de Madrid, published in 2020 and updated in 2025.
- **The Pilgrim's Reception Office of the Cathedral of Santiago** (oficinadelperegrino.com): its pages on the Compostela, the credencial and the Certificate of Distance.
- **Wikipedia**, for climate tables (Valladolid, Segovia, Madrid), for the articles on Sahagún, Madrid and Madrid's airport, and for the infobox of the Spanish article on the Camino de Santiago de Madrid.
- **A standard solar calculation**, for the hours of daylight.
- **The CaminoPlus route catalog and route notes**, for the route's start, end, ~320 km, difficulty rating and best months.

Published figures differ between these sources, so every distance in this book is marked ~. Where the sources say nothing, for example on closing dates, the book says nothing and tells you to check. If you know a better figure, or a fact that has changed, tell CaminoPlus.

Thanks go to the people who keep the Way: the volunteers who paint the yellow arrows each spring, the hosts who open the hostels, and the people of the villages who point the way and say *Buen Camino* to a stranger.

## A short glossary

- **Albergue**: a pilgrims' hostel with shared rooms.
- **Buen Camino**: "good Way", the greeting between pilgrims.
- **Cabildo**: the governing council of the cathedral.
- **Cañada**: a historic drove road for livestock.
- **Compostela**: the Latin certificate the Pilgrim's Office issues.
- **Credencial**: the pilgrim's passport.
- **Donativo**: a hostel that asks a donation instead of a fixed charge.
- **Hospitalero**: the host of a pilgrims' hostel.
- **Menú del peregrino**: a set meal for pilgrims.
- **Meseta**: the high plain of central Spain.
- **Mudéjar**: a Spanish style that joins Islamic decoration to Christian buildings.
- **Puerto**: a mountain pass.
- **Sello**: a stamp in the credencial.
