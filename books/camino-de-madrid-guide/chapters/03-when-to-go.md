# When to go

Patterns, not forecasts. Weather on a high plain and on a mountain pass can change your plan in an hour, so use this chapter to choose a season and the app and the local forecast to choose a day.

## The short answer

The app's route notes give April to June and September to October as the best months. The Confraternity of St James agrees in spirit: the road is walkable all year, it says, but spring and autumn are best, though they may be wet. It describes July to early September as extremely hot, with long stretches that have absolutely no shade, and winter as severe, with short days. It adds that there is snow on the Sierra de Guadarrama until May.

## Spring

Long days and moderate heat are the case for April to June. The catch is the sierra. The Confraternity says snow can lie on the Guadarrama into May, and the Madrid association's guide warns that when it snows the Puerto de la Fuenfría can be impassable. If you cross early in the season, check conditions on the pass the day before, and read the choices chapter for the alternatives. Rain is possible in spring, as the Confraternity notes. Tierra de Campos is cereal country, so you may find the fields green in spring and cut and pale by the end of June.

## Summer

July to early September is the hard season. For a sense of scale, Wikipedia's climate table for Valladolid, 1991 to 2020, gives a mean daily maximum of ~31 °C and a mean daily minimum of ~14 °C in July. For Segovia, at ~1,000 m, its table (1988 to 2010) gives ~30 °C and ~15 °C. For Madrid it says July daytime highs are ~32 to ~34 °C, often higher. Means hide the hot days. The road is open, much of it is without shade, and some stretches run for hours between villages. The Madrid association's summer leaflet asks for sun cream and a hat.

If you walk in summer, start at first light, finish before the afternoon heat, and treat the day's water as part of the plan. The next chapters come back to this.

The pine woods have one more risk. The Confraternity says wild camping in the pine forests is not recommended, because of the risk of fire.

## Autumn

September and October are the second good window. Days are shorter than in spring, and rain returns, according to the Confraternity. The heat of August eases. Check which hostels are still open as the season ends: the Association's 2021 leaflets advise asking, even in summer, which ones are open for the next stretch.

## Winter

Short days, cold, and fog. Wikipedia gives Valladolid a January mean daily maximum of ~8 °C and a minimum of about 1 °C, and says that morning fog is very typical in winter. The Association's guide warns that fog can be intense and persistent both in the sierra and in Castile, and advises carrying a means of orientation, or using the nearest road with care. Snow on the pass can shut it. Many people would not choose these months for a first Camino. If you do, the Confraternity's advice is to avoid the sierra in snow or bad weather, since there are no farms, villages or facilities up there.

## Daylight

A standard solar calculation for the latitudes of this road gives about 9 hours of daylight around the winter solstice, about 12 hours at the equinoxes, and about 15 hours around midsummer. In midwinter a ~31 km day leaves little spare light, so plan a split.

## Rain

Wikipedia gives Valladolid ~430 mm of rain a year and Segovia ~470 mm. Rain does come in spring and autumn, so carry a waterproof.

## Crowds

You may have the road mostly to yourself in any month. The Confraternity says the pressures from tourism are few, perhaps except in Madrid itself.

## Check before you go

Snow on the Fuenfría, heat warnings, fire restrictions in the pine woods, and which hostels are open. These change from week to week. The CaminoPlus app and the Association's website are the places to look.
