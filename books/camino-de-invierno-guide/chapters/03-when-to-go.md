# When to go

This chapter gives patterns, not forecasts. The road crosses three kinds of country: the drier Bierzo, ringed by mountains, at the start, the deep valleys of the Sil and the Miño in the middle, and the wetter country near Santiago. The figures below come from Wikipedia's climate tables, which cite AEMET, the Spanish state weather agency, and they are averages. Any week can differ.

## The short answer

The Confraternity of St James says the road is best walked between April and November. The CaminoPlus route guide says April to October. They differ by a month, and both leave out the cold months. The Confraternity adds a warning that fits the name badly: despite being called the Winter Way, the road can see the same snow, sleet, fog and high winds as the mountains of the Camino Francés, and you should expect mud, cold and wet at any time of year.

## Spring: April to June

Spring is mild. In Ponferrada the average afternoon reaches ~19 °C in April, ~22 °C in May and ~27 °C in June, with nights of ~6 °C, ~9 °C and ~12 °C. In Santiago the afternoons are a little cooler, ~17 °C, ~20 °C and ~23 °C, and May still brings ~109 mm of rain. Ponferrada gets ~640 mm of rain in a year, and Santiago more than twice as much, so the end of the road is its wettest part. Carry a waterproof.

## Summer: July and August

Summer is hot in the valleys. In Ponferrada the average July afternoon reaches ~30 °C, and Wikipedia says that temperatures above ~35 °C are not uncommon in heatwaves. The record is ~41.5 °C, on 17 July 2022. Wikipedia describes the Ribeira Sacra as more continental than Atlantic, with long hot summers, and the Sil valley is the more continental part. The road runs in those valleys for many days, and a long day in valley heat is the main summer hazard. In Santiago, by contrast, the average July afternoon is ~25 °C, and the month is dry, with ~31 mm of rain.

## Autumn: September to November

Autumn falls inside both sources' season. September afternoons average ~26 °C in Ponferrada and ~23 °C in Santiago, and the nights are cool. Then the rain arrives: in Santiago October averages ~214 mm, the wettest month of the year, and November ~194 mm. In Ponferrada the wet months are October to December, at ~78 to ~84 mm. The Xunta warns that at the start of the stage from Monforte to Chantada, flooding is frequent in the rainy season, on completely flat stretches. Daylight shrinks: on 1 November the day lasts ~10 hours, by a standard solar calculation.

## Winter: December to March

The name does not make this the easy season. In Ponferrada the average January afternoon is ~10 °C and the average night ~1 °C, nights below freezing are very common in winter, and snow falls on ~4 days a year on average. Wikipedia's record low for the town is about −10 °C.

On the road itself, the Xunta offers a shorter alternative to the climb of Monte Faro, from Penasillás, that in its words avoids the snow in winter. In Santiago the average January afternoon is ~12 °C and January brings ~196 mm of rain.

Days are short. By a standard solar calculation, daylight at Ponferrada and Santiago is ~9 hours at the December solstice, with sunrise near nine in the morning and sunset near six in the evening, Spanish time. At the June solstice it is ~15 hours, with sunset after ten at night. A winter walker has little time to waste, and a road with few services leaves little room for a late start. Places that serve pilgrims may be shut in the cold months, so ask before you rely on one.

## Crowds

This is a quiet road. In February 2020 the Xunta said that more than a thousand people had walked it in the previous year. The number has probably changed since, but the shape is clear: you will meet few pilgrims, and in some seasons none. That helps with peace and hurts with company, and it is another reason to plan each night ahead.

## Check before you go

Look at the forecast for the days you will walk, and ask locally about snow on Monte Faro, flooded fields near Monforte, and, in dry summers, fires and closed paths.
