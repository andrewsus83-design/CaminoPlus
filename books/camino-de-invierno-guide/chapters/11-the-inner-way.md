# The inner Way

## A quiet road for a quiet reason

People walk to Santiago for many reasons: faith, a promise, grief, a birthday that means something, a break from work, curiosity, sport, or no reason they could name. All of them are reasons to walk, and this book is for every one. The road asks only that you walk it honestly.

The Xunta de Galicia has a word for what this road offers. It speaks of the *sosiego*, the calm, that presides over this alternative route, which few people use, and calls that calm one of its main attractions. Whatever brings you here, you may find that the quiet is the point.

## What the long days do

On a busy road, company fills the hours. On this one, the hours are yours. For some walkers that is a gift. A long day beside a river, with no one near, gives thoughts a lot of room. You may find that the first day or two are noisy inside, and that the noise settles.

For others it is hard. A road with few pilgrims can feel lonely, and an old worry may arrive on the third day with nowhere to go. If that happens, you are not doing it wrong. Walk the next hour. Stop at the next village and have something warm. Write to someone, or talk to whoever you meet. The Way has been used by people carrying heavy things for a thousand years.

If the old tradition is right, this is a road that took people around an obstacle: the snow, the floods and the high pass. Not every road is a straight line, and not every good journey is the fastest one.

## Questions for the walk

Take one question a day, and let it go if it does not fit.

- What are you carrying that you could put down?
- What would you like to be quiet enough to hear?
- Who are you walking beside, in memory or in hope?
- What have you stopped noticing, and what do you notice here?
- What does this day ask of you, and what can you leave to tomorrow?
- If you had to say why you came, in one plain sentence, what would it be?

## For whatever you believe

The pilgrimage to Santiago is Christian in its roots, and the cathedral at its end is a place of prayer. The Pilgrim's Office has its own rules for the Compostela, and the chapter on arriving sets them out in the Office's words. Whatever you believe, be respectful in a church, and leave a village as you found it.

At the end of the day, ask yourself two things. Where did you feel most alive today? Where was it hard, and what helped? Then sleep. The next stage will keep.
