# The Sil and the Ribeira Sacra: A Rúa to Chantada

## The character of these days

Three long days take you into the south of Lugo, the Ribeira Sacra. The name means "sacred riverbank", and it belongs to the canyons of the Sil and the Miño, to old monasteries and to vineyards planted on terraces called *socalcos*. In 2003 the Council of Europe included the Ribeira Sacra among its Itineraries of Interest, the Xunta says.

These are the heavy days. The stages are ~27, ~36 and ~31 km, and the Xunta rates them Medium, Medium-High and High. From here the figures are those of the Xunta's web pages. Its leaflet and the Castilla y León guide differ from them by a kilometre or three, and the chapter on long days shows where to split.

### Day 4: A Rúa to Quiroga (~27 km)

The Xunta rates this stage Medium and estimates 6 hours 45 minutes. You leave A Rúa on the old road, along the line of a Roman road, and the first village, Albaredos, is already in the province of Lugo. A dirt track then drops towards the river, and soon you reach Montefurado. The Xunta calls its Roman tunnel, dug through the rock, one of the grandest Roman engineering works in the Iberian Peninsula.

After Montefurado the Sil keeps company with you, with fields of olive, fig and walnut trees. You pass A Venda Vella and A Venda Nova, where the Xunta says the ruins were once inns, and the Capela das Farrapas, a name that refers to the rags (*farrapas*) that walkers had to take off. At O Soldón the Xunta records a smithy of the Order of St John of Jerusalem. At Sequeiros you may climb to the castle of Os Novaes, a residence of the Hospitallers, and come back down. You enter Quiroga by the Rúa Real, after the medieval bridge called Ponte Pedriña.

The Xunta's table gives ~27 km. Its leaflet gives a little less, ~26.5 km, so the sources agree to within half a kilometre.

### Day 5: Quiroga to Monforte de Lemos (~36 km)

This is the longest day on the road. The Xunta rates it Medium-High at ~36.5 km, with an estimate of 7 hours 20 minutes. Its leaflet says ~35 km and the Castilla y León guide ~33 km, so sources differ by up to ~3 km, and none of them makes it short.

You leave Quiroga on the right bank of the Sil and follow roads, including stretches of the provincial road, through Espandariz and Nocedo. The Xunta mentions that you may also go by San Clodio and Ribas de Sil, and cross the bridge later to rejoin the route. By the hermitage of Os Remedios you drop to the River Lor, which comes down clear from the Serra do Courel, and cross it by a medieval bridge. Then you enter the municipality of A Pobra do Brollón. On the right, the Xunta says, are the remains of the Roman gold mine of O Medo. In A Pobra itself the Xunta's photographs show the Roman bridge of A Labrada, with its Roman road.

A Pobra is ~24 km from Quiroga. After it comes a sharp ascent to the Alto da Serra and Mount Moncai. You pass Reigada and walk beside the River Seco. You enter Monforte by the Rúa Cruces, with the fortress ahead, crowned by a Benedictine monastery that the Xunta calls a thousand years old, and cross the medieval bridge over the River Cabe.

A Pobra do Brollón is the natural place to break this day: it leaves ~24 km and then ~13 km. Whether you can sleep there is a question for the app and the local sources, and this book does not answer it.

Monforte is the main town of the Terra de Lemos. The Xunta's leaflet lists its tower and walls, the monastery of San Vicente do Pino, and the college of Our Lady of the Antigua, which it says holds works by El Greco. Wikipedia says the town stands in a valley between the Miño and the Sil, and that a railway reached it in 1883. If any day deserves a rest, it is the day you arrive here.

### Day 6: Monforte de Lemos to Chantada (~31 km)

The Xunta rates this stage High, estimating 7 hours 20 minutes. Its leaflet says ~31 km and the Castilla y León guide ~29 km. One warning comes first, and it comes from the Xunta's Spanish text: in the rainy season, flooding is frequent at the start of this stage, on completely flat stretches. *Check before you go.* After heavy rain, ask where you slept.

You leave Monforte by the medieval bridge and the old Rúa Abeledos, alternating stretches of road with tracks, through A Vide, A Lagoa and Pacios, to Moreda. You enter the municipality of Pantón, where the Xunta notes the manor house at O Reguengo and stones at Castrotañe that may come from a Roman road. Under old oaks you go on through O Outeiriño and A Barxa, in O Saviñao, and then Rendal and Diomondi, whose Romanesque church the Xunta singles out.

Next you go down to the bridge of Belesar over the Miño, among the terraced vineyards. At Belesar there was an ancient hospital. Then comes a hard climb of about 3 km to San Pedro de Líncora, which also had a medieval hospital, and fine views of the river. (The Xunta's English page garbles this distance; the Spanish original says about 3 km.) You enter Chantada by the district of A Ponte and the Rúa Dous de Maio.

Two splits are natural: Belesar, ~24 km in and ~6 km short of Chantada, and A Barxa, ~15 km in. A long day, a hard rating and a final climb mean that this is the day to start early.
