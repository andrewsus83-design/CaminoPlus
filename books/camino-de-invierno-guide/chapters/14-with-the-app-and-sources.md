# With the app, and sources

## With the CaminoPlus app

**CaminoPlus** is the offline-first companion app for the Camino de Santiago, with maps that work without signal, stays and cafes, a pilgrim passport with stamps, a journal and friends. The paid plan is called **Pilgrim Plus**. The app is still in development. The Camino de Invierno is on its list of routes, but this book makes no claim about what the app holds for it today, so look in the app, and use it, not this book, for beds, cafes, closures and weather.

A reminder about the passport: the Pilgrim's Office asks for the stamped credencial. Whether it accepts anything else is for the Office to say.

## Sources and thanks

This book rests on public sources, and its fact ledger lists every fact against the page it came from. The chief ones are:

- **The Xunta de Galicia**, on its official Camino de Santiago portal (caminodesantiago.gal): the Winter Way page, in English and Spanish, with its nine stage pages and their sections, lengths, ratings and descriptions; the Xunta's leaflet on the road, a Spanish PDF; and the Xunta's press notes on the road from February 2020 and February 2022.
- **The Junta de Castilla y León**, on its tourism site (turismocastillayleon.com): the chapter on the Camino Real de Invierno in its guide to the traditional Caminos.
- **The Confraternity of St James** (csj.org.uk): its page on the Camino Invierno, updated in June 2025.
- **The Pilgrim's Reception Office of the Cathedral of Santiago** (oficinadelperegrino.com): the pages on the Compostela, the credencial, the Certificate of Distance, planning and the history of the pilgrimage.
- **Wikipedia**, for geography, climate tables and history: the articles on Ponferrada, Las Médulas, Monforte de Lemos, the Ribeira Sacra, Santiago de Compostela, its cathedral and airport, the Jacobean Holy Year and the Camino de Santiago route descriptions; the French Wikipedia's article on the Winter Way, for the height of Monte Faro; and the Spanish Wikipedia's article on the Camino Francés, for its kilometre table.
- **A standard solar calculation**, for the hours of daylight.
- **The CaminoPlus route catalog and route guide**, for the route's start, end, ~260 km, difficulty rating and the app's own note.

The Xunta's English pages are machine-translated from Spanish. Three sentences read differently from the Spanish original: the length of the detour over the Serra da Lastra, the length of the climb after Belesar, and a few place names. Where they differ, this book follows the Spanish.

Published figures differ between these sources, so every distance in this book is marked ~. Where the sources say nothing, the book says nothing and tells you to check. If you know a better figure, or a fact that has changed, tell CaminoPlus.

Thanks go to the people who keep the Way: the associations of friends of the Camino, the hosts who open the hostels, and the people in the villages and towns who point the way and say *Buen Camino* to a stranger.

## A short glossary

- **Albergue**: a pilgrims' hostel with shared rooms.
- **Botafumeiro**: the great censer of Santiago cathedral.
- **Buen Camino**: "good Way", the greeting between pilgrims.
- **Cabildo**: the governing council of the cathedral.
- **Compostela**: the Latin certificate the Pilgrim's Office issues.
- **Credencial**: the pilgrim's passport.
- **Hórreo**: a raised granary, a mark of the Galician countryside.
- **Hospitalero**: the host of a pilgrims' hostel.
- **Menú del peregrino**: a set meal for pilgrims.
- **Peto de ánimas**: a wayside place where walkers leave a small offering for the dead.
- **Sello**: a stamp in the credencial.
- **Socalcos**: terraces for vineyards on the steep slopes of the Ribeira Sacra.
- **Xunta**: the regional government of Galicia.
