# Monte Faro, the Deza and the way in: Chantada to Santiago

## The character of these days

Four days take you over the last high ground and down into Santiago. The first is the climb of Monte Faro, with a view over four provinces. The next two cross the Deza, a country of oak woods, small bridges and farms, where the road meets the Vía de la Plata and shares its last ~50 km. The fourth is a short walk into the city from the south, by the Sar valley and the old gate of Mazarelos.

The figures are the Xunta's web pages again, with the leaflet and the Castilla y León guide as checks. Treat each as good to a kilometre or two.

### Day 7: Chantada to Rodeiro (~26 km)

The Xunta rates this stage High and estimates 6 hours 15 minutes. Its leaflet says ~26 km and the Castilla y León guide ~27 km. You leave Chantada from the Praza de Santa Ana, climb the Rúa Uxío Novoneyra and follow a local road. Granaries (*hórreos*), roadside crosses and the *petos de ánimas*, the places where a walker leaves a small offering for the dead, as the leaflet explains, appear on the way through Centulle and San Xurxo de Asma. Ahead stands the summit of Monte Faro.

At Penasillás the road divides. The way over the mountain is, in the Xunta's words, a hard ascent of ~4.8 km, through Chao de Seixas. A turn to the left, by a way of the cross called the Camiño da Virxe, leads to the hermitage of Our Lady of O Faro, at more than ~1,100 m. The Xunta says the view takes in the four provinces of Galicia. The French Wikipedia gives ~1,187 m for the highest point of the road, on this mountain, and the Xunta's leaflet says there is a pilgrimage festival at the hermitage on 8 September.

The alternative, from Penasillás by Mouricios and San Vicente, is shorter, and the Xunta says that in winter it avoids the snow. If you see cloud on the summit, or if snow is forecast, take it. *Check before you go.*

From the mountain the road goes down, past the manor of Camba, which the leaflet says once gave shelter to pilgrims, and through the valley of A Ermida, to Río and then Mouriz. In Rodeiro the town hall stands on the ruins of a fortress, and opposite it is a stone fountain with four spouts.

### Day 8: Rodeiro to Bendoiro, by Lalín (~28 km)

The Xunta rates this stage Medium and estimates 6 hours 55 minutes. Its leaflet says ~27 km and the Castilla y León guide ~27 km, so the sources sit within about a kilometre of each other. You follow the road towards Lalín and cross the River Arnego several times. The first bridge is A Ponte do Hospital, a name that, the Xunta says, recalls a pilgrims' hospital.

After Penerbosa you cross the medieval bridge of Pedroso. In the hamlet of A Ponte, also called Mesón, "the inn", the Xunta says that many ground floors still keep the ledges that served as counters for travellers. You climb the Coto da Mamuela, a name that refers to a field of burial mounds, and pass among chestnut trees to Palmaz.

Soon after, you enter Lalín. The town is proud of being the "zero kilometre of Galicia", the geographic centre of the land, and holds a famous fair of *cocido*, the local stew, each year. Lalín is ~22 km from Rodeiro by this book's arithmetic, with ~6 km left to A Laxe by the Castilla y León guide, and it is a natural place to stop if you want a shorter day. Beyond it, you walk by the River Pontiñas, through oak woods, beside the national road, to A Laxe, in the parish of Bendoiro. Here, the Xunta says, the Winter Way joins the Vía de la Plata.

### Day 9: Bendoiro to Outeiro (~34 km)

This is the second-longest day. The Xunta rates it Medium and estimates 7 hours 5 minutes. Its leaflet says ~34 km, so the sources agree. The Xunta's last section, A Bandeira to Outeiro, is rated Medium-High.

From here to Santiago you walk the stretch shared with the Camino Sanabrés, so you may meet pilgrims who have come a long way from the south. The first metres run along the national road N-525, past Vilasoa and Prado, where a detour leads to the Marian sanctuary of O Corpiño. You cross the medieval bridge of Taboada over the River Deza, climb to Taboada and Trasfontao, and reach Silleda, ~9 km from the start.

Then a dirt road to the left of the N-525 leads to A Bandeira, ~17 km in, which splits the day almost in half. The road goes on past Santo Estevo de Oca, famous for its manor and for gardens called the "Galician Versailles". You cross the River Ulla at A Ponte Ulla, by a historic bridge, and enter the province of A Coruña, in the municipality of Vedra. Outeiro, where the Xunta's stage ends, is by the town hall of Vedra.

### Day 10: Outeiro to Santiago (~17 km)

The Xunta rates this stage Low and estimates 3 hours 20 minutes. Its leaflet gives ~17 km, the same. Beyond Rubial, in the municipality of Boqueixón, a detour leads to Pico Sacro, which rises, the Xunta says, to more than five hundred metres and is linked to the legend of the translation of the Apostle's body: on its slopes grazed the wild bulls that the disciples tamed so that they would carry the body to its tomb.

You enter Compostela from the south, by the Sar district, and cross the River Sar. Then comes a last effort, the hard climb up the Rúa do Sar and the Rúa do Castrón Douro. At the top, on the right, you pass under the arch of the Porta de Mazarelos, the only gate of the old medieval wall that still stands, according to the Xunta. The last steps are by the Praza da Universidade, the Rúa da Calderería and the Praza das Praterías, to the Praza do Obradoiro. Sit down before you do anything else.
