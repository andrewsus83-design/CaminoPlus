# The Bierzo and Valdeorras: Ponferrada to A Rúa

## The character of these days

Three days take you out of León and into Galicia by the River Sil. The first is a walk from a Templar town through chestnut and slate villages to a landscape the Romans made by washing a mountain away. The second crosses the border into the province of Ourense. The third is short, along a wide river and past the caves where Valdeorras keeps its wine.

The figures come from the Xunta de Galicia. Its stage pages start at Las Médulas, so the first day uses the Xunta's leaflet and the Castilla y León tourism guide. Where these sources differ, the book says so. Treat every distance as good to a kilometre or two, and a little more on the first day.

### Day 1: Ponferrada to Las Médulas (~27 km)

The Xunta's leaflet gives ~27 km for this stretch, and the Castilla y León guide adds its sections up to ~31 km. Expect between the two. The Xunta describes it as a gentle climb out of Ponferrada and a long descent to Las Médulas. Its web pages do not rate this section, so treat it as a first long day.

The Castilla y León guide has you leave from the basilica of Our Lady of La Encina, skirting the Templar castle through the old streets, to the medieval bridge over the River Boeza. The road then runs beside the Boeza and rises gently through Toral de Merayo, Villalibre de la Jurisdicción and Priaranza del Bierzo to Santalla del Bierzo. At Santalla the guide praises the view, and mentions a Templar knight carved in a tree trunk beside the way, with the inscription "Camino Real de Invierno. Vía Real de Peregrinación".

At Villavieja, the guide says, the church holds a twelfth-century image of St James as a pilgrim. The castle of Cornatel, a fortress tied to the Templars, stands on a spur above the stream, and the Xunta's leaflet says two alternatives meet on the descent from it. The climb to its foot is long and steep, so decide at the turning whether to go up. From the Alto de Ferreiros the Xunta describes a continuous descent to Las Médulas, and the Castilla y León guide has you pass through Borrenes.

Las Médulas was the most important gold mine of the Roman Empire, according to Wikipedia, and it is a World Heritage Site, inscribed in 1997. Today its red cliffs stand among chestnut trees. You are at the edge of Galicia, and the next day starts with a gentle climb.

### Day 2: Las Médulas to O Barco de Valdeorras (~26 km)

The Xunta rates this stage Medium and estimates 6 hours 40 minutes. The first section follows the old royal road, the *Camino Real*, to Puente de Domingo Flórez, ~9 km, still in León. Then comes a gentle ascent to the top of Pedrices, with a fine view back over Las Médulas, and a steep descent to the last village of León, where the Xunta promises "pleasant solitude".

From Puente de Domingo Flórez you cross the Sil towards Quereño and follow the right bank to Pumares, which is already in Ourense, and on to Sobradelo. At Éntoma the Xunta singles out a Roman bridge of a single arch built of slate, and then you reach O Barco de Valdeorras.

The Xunta offers an alternative at Quereño: a detour over the Serra da Lastra, an oak wood of Mediterranean character that has been a natural park since 2002. Read this carefully. The Xunta's Spanish page says the detour is ~14 km longer. Its English page garbles the sentence into "an alternative to this 14 kilometre route", which can be read as saying the opposite. Whichever way you go, the two paths meet again at O Barco. The Xunta's table gives the main line as ~26.5 km, and the leaflet's figures imply ~26 km.

### Day 3: O Barco de Valdeorras to A Rúa (~14 km)

After two long days, this is a short one: the Xunta rates it Medium, with an estimate of 3 hours 35 minutes, and its leaflet gives ~13 km. You leave O Barco by the old road, between the national road and the Sil, and cross the railway line by a flyover to Vilamartín de Valdeorras. There you cross the old bridge of A Rodeleira. The Xunta says the town has numerous caves cut into its slopes, many of them left by Roman gold-working and now turned into wine cellars, and the leaflet counts more than 400 of them. Early in August the town holds a festival of the caves, with guided visits.

From Vilamartín the road runs on long straights between the railway and the river, round the Valencia reservoir on the Sil, with a fine view of the Valdeorras valley before you enter A Rúa by the San Roque quarter. The Xunta's leaflet mentions a Roman-origin bridge in A Rúa, part of the Roman road from Braga to Astorga, and a riverside promenade.

The Valdeorras wines have a protected denomination of origin, and the leaflet names two grapes, the white godello and the red mencía. Rest here. Tomorrow is ~27 km, and the road gets hillier from here.
