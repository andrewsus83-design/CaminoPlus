# Welcome

The Winter Way is the quiet road out of Ponferrada. It leaves the Camino Francés where the French Way goes straight on, turns left, and follows the River Sil through Roman gold country and wine country. It climbs a hill with a view over four provinces, and comes down through the Deza to the cathedral of Santiago de Compostela. It is ~260 km in all, and it is a road that asks you to plan.

**The promise of this book is simple.** By the last page you will know whether this quiet road is your Camino, what each of its ten walking days asks of you, and what to check before you leave home.

## How to use it

If you are a month or more away, read the next four chapters in order: the Way in brief, whether it suits you, when to go, and what to do before you go. Then read the days by region, and the chapter on the long days, which shows where the official stages can be split.

If you are on the road tonight, go straight to your stage. If you are only dreaming, start with "Is this Camino for you?" and finish with "The inner Way".

## What this book is not

It is not a live map, a timetable or a booking service. It names no hostels, cafes or shops, and it gives no prices, opening hours or bed counts. They change from season to season, and a wrong one costs a tired person dearly. On a road with few pilgrims that matters more than usual. For those answers, use the CaminoPlus app and ask locally.

This book was written with AI assistance, from public sources, and its author has not walked the road. So it gives you sources instead of memories, and it says plainly when the sources disagree.

Every distance here carries a tilde, **~**, because it is approximate. The official sources measure this road in different ways, and the book says so where it matters. Where something depends on the season (snow, fog, flooded fields, heat, a closed path), it says *check before you go*, and means it.

## A few words you will meet

*Buen Camino*, "good Way", is what pilgrims and locals say to one another as you pass. An *albergue* is a pilgrims' hostel. The *credencial* is your pilgrim's passport, a folded card that you carry and have stamped along the way. Each stamp is a *sello*. The *Compostela* is the certificate the Pilgrim's Office in Santiago gives to those who meet its rules, which the chapter on arriving sets out. The *Xunta* is the regional government of Galicia, and its official Camino portal is one of this book's main sources.

Place names follow the official pages: Spanish in the first days, in León (Las Médulas, Puente de Domingo Flórez), and Galician from the border (O Barco de Valdeorras, A Rúa, Monforte de Lemos).

Carry what you need, plan your nights ahead, and take your time over the long hill. *Buen Camino.*
