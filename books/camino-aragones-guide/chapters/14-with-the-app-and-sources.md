# With the app, and sources

## With the CaminoPlus app

**CaminoPlus** is the offline-first companion app for the Camino de Santiago, with maps that work without signal, stays and cafes, a pilgrim passport with stamps, a journal and friends. The paid plan is called **Pilgrim Plus**. The app is still in development. It lists the Camino Aragonés as a route, from Somport to Puente la Reina, ~160 km, but this book makes no claim about what the app holds for it today, so look in the app, and use it, not this book, for beds, cafes, closures and weather.

A reminder about the passport: the Pilgrim's Office describes a paper credencial and a digital credential of its own. This book says nothing about the passport in any other app, and you should ask the Office whether it counts.

## Sources and thanks

This book rests on public sources, and its fact ledger lists every fact against the page it came from. The chief ones are:

- **The Gobierno de Aragón** (aragon.es): the page on the Directriz del Camino de Santiago, Camino Francés a su paso por Aragón, and its planning study (Tomo I), which describes the path section by section from the Somport to the border with Navarre.
- **Turismo de Aragón**, the regional tourism board: its brochure *El Camino de Santiago por Aragón* (a 2009 print), for the four Aragonese stages and the villages.
- **The Gobierno de Navarra**, through its tourism site (visitnavarra.es): the page on the French-Aragonese route, the printed *Camino de Santiago* guide (fifth edition, November 2023), the leaflet of milestones (2021), and the advice leaflet of the Policía Foral (2026).
- **The Pilgrim's Reception Office of the Cathedral of Santiago** (oficinadelperegrino.com): the rules of the Compostela, the credencial and the Certificate of Distance.
- **The Confraternity of St James** (csj.org.uk): its page on the Chemin d'Arles.
- **The Fundación Eroski** (caminodesantiago.consumer.es): the six stage pages of the French Way "by Somport", which give the day lengths, the places along each day and the practical warnings. This is a private foundation's guide and not an official source, and the book attributes it each time.
- **Wikipedia**, for history, geography and climate: the articles on Somport, the Aragonese Way, the French Way, the Way of St. James, Jaca (with its table of World Meteorological Organization figures), the Yesa Reservoir, Canfranc International railway station and Puente la Reina; and the Spanish Wikipedia's articles on the Camino de Santiago Francés en Aragón and on the Hospital de Santa Cristina de Somport.
- **A standard solar calculation**, for the hours of daylight at Jaca.
- **The CaminoPlus route catalog**, for the road's start, end, ~160 km and difficulty rating, and the CaminoPlus website's list of routes, for its 6 to 7 stages.

Published figures differ between these sources, so every distance in this book is marked ~. Where the sources say nothing, as on the present line of the path beside the reservoir, the book says nothing and tells you to check. If you know a better figure, or a fact that has changed, tell CaminoPlus.

Thanks go to the people who keep the Way: the associations of friends of the Camino, the hosts who open the hostels, and the people in the villages and towns who point the way and say *Buen Camino* to a stranger.

## A short glossary

- **Albergue**: a pilgrims' hostel with shared rooms.
- **Buen Camino**: "good Way", the greeting between pilgrims.
- **Cabañera**: a broad droving track made by flocks.
- **Canal de Berdún**: the broad valley of the River Aragón between Jaca and Navarre.
- **Credencial**: the pilgrim's passport.
- **GR 65.3**: the waymarked long-distance path of the Camino in Spain, with red and white bars.
- **Hospitalero**: the host of a pilgrims' hostel.
- **Javieradas**: the pilgrimages to the castle of Javier each March.
- **Menú del peregrino**: a set meal for pilgrims.
- **Sello**: a stamp in the credencial.
- **Via Tolosana**: the road from Arles by Toulouse, of which this is the Spanish end.
