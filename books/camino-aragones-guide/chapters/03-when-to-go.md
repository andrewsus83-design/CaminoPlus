# When to go

## The short answer

The Confraternity of St James says the Arles road, of which this is the Spanish end, can be walked all year, and that spring (long daylight, flowers) and autumn are best. It calls summer too hot, and it advises against winter journeys over the Somport pass in snow or mist, except for experienced mountain walkers.

The CaminoPlus app's route notes put the best months at May to September. The two views differ, and the difference is worth knowing. May and September fall within both. July and August are where they part.

## What the weather does

This book has climate figures for one place only, Jaca, near the top of the road. Wikipedia gives figures from the World Meteorological Organization and describes a submediterranean climate with cool winters, warm summers, common frost, snow common from late November to early March, and the rainiest seasons in autumn and spring.

- **January:** daytime highs of about 10 °C, and lows around −1 °C.
- **April and May:** highs of about 16 °C and about 21 °C.
- **July:** highs of about 29 °C, with lows of about 14 °C.
- **October:** highs of about 19 °C, and lows of about 7 °C.
- **Rain:** about 770 mm a year, with no real dry season.

These are Jaca's figures, at ~820 m. The pass is much higher, so expect it to be colder. Aragon's planning study notes that temperatures keep rising as the valley opens out below Jaca. The Confraternity warns that Aragón can be chilly and windy even in summer, and that the Somport is snow-covered in winter.

## Daylight

A standard solar calculation for Jaca gives about 9 hours of daylight in late December, about 12 hours in mid-March and mid-September, and about 15 hours at midsummer, when the sun rises around 06:25 and sets around 21:40 on summer time. In mid-October it sets at about 19:20. If you walk in autumn or winter, plan to arrive before dark.

## A season at a time

- **Spring.** Long days, green hills and cool mornings. Snow may still lie at the pass early in the season, so ask before you set off. The Confraternity's pick.
- **Summer.** On a day that the stage guide calls shadeless, the sun is the main thing you walk against. Start at first light, rest in the middle of the day, and carry more water than seems sensible.
- **Autumn.** Shorter days and more rain, but milder air. The Confraternity's other pick.
- **Winter.** Short days and snow at the pass. The Confraternity does not advise the crossing, and this book agrees.

## Crowds

Few pilgrims walk this road. Spanish Wikipedia, citing the Pilgrim's Office, counts 404 pilgrims who received a Compostela in 2018 after starting at Somport. You may often have the path to yourself. You will meet far more people once you join the French Way.

## Check before you go

- **Snow and mist at the pass.** Ask in Jaca or Canfranc, and check the forecast, before you set off from Somport.
- **Heat.** In summer, look at the forecast for the Canal de Berdún before the two long days.
- **Opening seasons.** Some small albergues and the campsites keep only a season. One stage guide gives March to October for volunteer hosts in one village. Ask ahead.
- **The reservoir.** The Aragonese government's 2018 planning study says that works to enlarge the Yesa reservoir would affect two stretches of the path near Artieda and Ruesta. Ask locally which line is open.
