# Going home, or going on

## If you stop here

A short road ends quickly, and the quiet can arrive all at once. Give yourself a day before the journey home. Sit somewhere with a view of the bridge, wash your clothes, and write down what you remember before the details soften: the cold at the pass, the shadeless plain, the ruined village, the first sight of Eunate. If you carry your credencial home, the stamps are a record of where you slept, and they will mean more in a year than they do tonight.

Do not plan a tight connection for the day you finish. From Puente la Reina, Pamplona is ~24 km back along the French Way, and you will want your onward travel sorted before the last stage.

## If you go on

The Camino does not end at Puente la Reina. You can carry on at once, along the French Way, with a rest day first if you can spare it. The road you have just walked is a good preparation: you have six days of legs, your feet have told you what they think of your shoes, and you know how much water you need.

## Keeping the Way alive

- **Walk again.** You can come back another year and pick up at Puente la Reina.
- **Read the other books in this series,** if you want to know what lies ahead. *The French Way* has the road from here.
- **Pass it on.** Say *Buen Camino* to the next walker you meet, wherever you are.

When the days at home feel ordinary, remember that you carried everything you needed on your back for a week, and that you walked from a mountain pass to the place where the roads become one. *Buen Camino.*
