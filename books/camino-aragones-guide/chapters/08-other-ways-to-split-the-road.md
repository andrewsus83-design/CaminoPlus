# Other ways to split the road

## Six days, seven days

This book follows six days, the plan of the consumer foundation's stage guide. Aragon's own tourism brochure divides its part of the road into four stages: Somport to Jaca, Jaca to Arrés, Arrés to Artieda, and Artieda to Undués de Lerda. Add Undués to Sangüesa, which the brochure counts at ~11.5 km, and the two Navarre days, and you have seven. The CaminoPlus website's list of routes gives 6 to 7 stages, its own editorial range. Both plans work.

The seven-day plan, with lengths rounded from the same guide, looks like this:

1. Somport to Jaca (~31 km).
2. Jaca to Arrés (~25 km).
3. Arrés to Artieda (~18 km).
4. Artieda to Undués de Lerda (~22 km).
5. Undués de Lerda to Sangüesa (~11 km).
6. Sangüesa to Monreal (~27 km).
7. Monreal to Puente la Reina (~31 km).

The sums come to ~165 km. Days 3 to 5 are shorter. They also split the long day from Arrés to Ruesta, so that you do not carry a whole day's food across the shadeless plain.

You can also cut Day 2 at Santa Cilia, ~15 km from Jaca, and Day 1 at Canfranc or Villanúa. The road has few villages, so ask ahead before you shorten a day.

## The northern line

After Puente la Reina de Jaca the Aragonese government's study shows two lines. The southern one, by Arrés, Mianos and Artieda, is the one in this book. The northern one runs beside the N-240 by Berdún, Sigüés, Escó and Tiermas, and Spanish Wikipedia's table takes it on by Yesa and Javier to Sangüesa. The Aragonese study found it unsigned along much of its length in 2018, and says that part of its old line lies under the reservoir. The study calls the southern line the main one, and so does this book.

## Detours

- **Santa Cruz de la Serós and San Juan de la Peña.** See the chapter on Jaca to Ruesta. It adds about 14 km: the stage guide counts ~29 km from Jaca to Santa Cilia by the monastery, against ~15 km direct.
- **The Foz de Lumbier.** The second way from Sangüesa to Monreal, ~5 km longer.
- **Leyre.** Navarre's tourism page puts the monastery ~12 km from Sangüesa. It is a Romanesque monastery with an eleventh-century crypt and a lavishly decorated doorway, and Navarre's brochure says that pilgrims on foot do not pass it. Navarre's page also mentions Gregorian chant.
- **Javier.** The castle, birthplace of St Francis Xavier, patron of Navarre, lies ~8.5 km off the Camino from Sangüesa, and the page says thousands of Navarrese come in March on the *Javieradas*, a pilgrimage in his honour.

Decide about Leyre and Javier before you leave Sangüesa, not after.

## Where to rest

Jaca is the obvious place for a rest day, at the end of the long first day and before the quiet middle. Sangüesa is the other. Navarre's page says its streets hold palaces, churches and convents, and the stage guide calls it the second-largest town on the road after Jaca.
