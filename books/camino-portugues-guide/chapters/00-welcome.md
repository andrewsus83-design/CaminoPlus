# Welcome

The Portuguese Way runs from the cathedral of Porto, on the Douro, to the cathedral of Santiago de Compostela. On the way it crosses a river that is also a border, the Minho, and passes from one language into two. This book follows the Central route, ~240 km in the CaminoPlus catalog, in eleven walking days.

**The promise of this book is simple.** By the last page you will know whether this is your Camino, what each of the eleven days asks of you, and what to check before you leave home.

## How to use it

If you are a month or more away, read the next four chapters in order: the Way in brief, whether it suits you, when to go, and what to do before you go. Then read the days, which are grouped by region, and the chapter on other ways to split them, which also gives the rule for the Compostela.

If you are on the road tonight, go straight to your stage. Each day says what the walking is like, what is worth a pause, and where to take care.

If you are only dreaming, start with "Is this Camino for you?" and finish with "The inner Way". You can decide the rest later.

## What this book is not

It is not a live map, a timetable or a booking service. It names no hostels, cafes or shops, and it gives no prices, opening hours or bed counts. They change from season to season, and a wrong one costs a tired person dearly. For those, use the CaminoPlus app and ask locally.

It is also not a walker's memoir. The book was written with AI assistance, from public sources, and its author has not walked the road. So it gives you sources instead of memories, and it says plainly when the sources disagree, which on this road they often do.

Every distance carries a tilde, **~**, because it is approximate. Where something depends on the season (a diversion, heat, a river after rain), the book says *check before you go*, and means it.

## A few words you will meet

*Caminho* is the Portuguese word for "way" or "road", and *Camino* the Spanish; the route has both names. The greeting between pilgrims is *Buen Camino* in Spanish and *bom caminho* in Portuguese. A *peregrino* (Spanish and Portuguese alike) is a pilgrim. An *albergue* is a pilgrims' hostel. The *credencial* is your pilgrim's passport, a folded card you carry and have stamped along the way; a stamp is a *sello* in Spanish and a *carimbo* in Portuguese. The *Compostela* is the certificate the Pilgrim's Office in Santiago gives to those who meet its rules.

They are explained again when they first matter, and used plainly after that.

Pack lightly, walk gently, and take your time over the long hill. *Bom caminho.*
