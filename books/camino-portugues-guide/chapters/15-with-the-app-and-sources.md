# With the app, and sources

## With the CaminoPlus app

**CaminoPlus** is the offline-first companion app for the Camino de Santiago, with maps that work without signal, stays and cafes, a pilgrim passport with stamps, a journal and friends. The paid plan is called **Pilgrim Plus**. The app is still in development, so look in the app for what is available on the Camino Portugués today, and use it, not this book, for beds, cafes, closures and weather.

A reminder about the passport: the Pilgrim's Office asks for the stamped credencial. A passport in any app is a keepsake until the Office says otherwise.

## Sources and thanks

This book rests on public sources, and the fact ledger that goes with it lists every fact against the page it came from. The chief ones are:

- **The Xunta de Galicia**, the regional government, on its official Camino de Santiago portal (caminodesantiago.gal): the page on the Portuguese Way, its six stages from Tui to Santiago with their lengths and descriptions, and the page on the Mar de Arousa and Río Ulla Route.
- **The Pilgrim's Reception Office of the Cathedral of Santiago** (oficinadelperegrino.com): the rules of the Compostela, the credencial, the certificate of distance and the history of the pilgrimage.
- **The Confraternity of St James** (csj.org.uk): its page on the Camino Portugués, for the route, the waymarking, the weather and the way out of Porto.
- **Wikipedia**, for history, geography and climate: the articles on the Portuguese Way, Porto, its cathedral and its airport, Barcelos, Ponte de Lima, Valença, the monastery church of Rates, the bridge of Rubiães, Tui, Peter González, Redondela, Pontevedra, Caldas de Reis, Padrón, Santiago de Compostela, its cathedral and its airport, the Jacobean Holy Year, Galicia, Portugal and time in Portugal and Spain. It is also where this book found the Pilgrim's Office statistics for 2025, and the climate averages of the Portuguese and Spanish weather services.
- **Published day-length tables** for Porto, checked against a standard solar calculation, for the hours of daylight.
- **The CaminoPlus route catalog**, for the names and order of the eleven stages and, for the Portuguese days, lengths measured along the OpenStreetMap route (data by OpenStreetMap contributors) and compared with published guides.
- **The Associação dos Amigos do Caminho Português de Santiago**, which the Confraternity credits for the yellow arrows between Porto and the border.

Stage lengths differ from one source to the next, and a few pages disagree with themselves, so every distance in this book is marked ~. This book found no official stage table for Portugal that it could read; the Portuguese figures are therefore the least certain. If you know a better figure, or a fact that has changed, tell CaminoPlus.

Thanks go to the people who keep the Way: the associations of friends of the Camino, the hosts who open the hostels, and the people in the towns and villages who point the way and say *bom caminho* to a stranger.

## A short glossary

- **Albergue**: a pilgrims' hostel with shared rooms.
- **Botafumeiro**: the great censer of Santiago cathedral.
- **Bom caminho, Buen Camino**: "good Way", the greeting between pilgrims, in Portuguese and in Spanish.
- **Caminho, Camino**: "way" or "road", in Portuguese and in Spanish.
- **Carballeira**: an oak wood, in Galician.
- **Carimbo, sello**: a stamp in the credencial, in Portuguese and in Spanish.
- **Compostela**: the Latin certificate the Pilgrim's Office issues.
- **Credencial**: the pilgrim's passport.
- **Hospitalero**: the host of a pilgrims' hostel.
- **Humilladoiro**: the place where pilgrims knelt on first seeing the cathedral, in Galician.
- **Menú del peregrino**: a set meal for pilgrims.
- **Peregrino**: a pilgrim.
- **Pedrón**: the big stone of Padrón.
- **Ría**: a long sea inlet, as at Vigo and Pontevedra.
- **Sé**: the cathedral, in Portuguese.
- **Xunta**: the regional government of Galicia.
