# When to go

This is a green road, and it is green because it rains. The Confraternity of St James describes northern Portugal and Galicia as a changeable maritime climate, where westerly winds bring generous rain, hence "Green Portugal" and "Green Spain". Everything below is a pattern, not a forecast.

## The seasons

**Spring.** The Confraternity calls spring a rewarding time, cool, with fresh growth. It is also still wet: at Porto airport the rainfall is ~100 mm in March and in April, and at Santiago ~140 mm and ~130 mm (averages for 1991 to 2020). Carry a proper rain jacket and a plan for wet feet.

**Summer.** Wikipedia says most pilgrims finish this Camino between May and September, and the Confraternity warns that summer can be hot and humid, with the peak holiday months crowded. The averages are mild: in July and August the mean daytime high is ~24 to ~25 °C at both Porto airport and Santiago. The averages hide the extremes. The record highs are ~38.6 °C at Porto airport and ~39.4 °C at Santiago airport, and rain is scarce: ~17 mm in July at Porto, ~31 mm at Santiago. A heatwave on a long day, such as Day 3, is a safety matter, not a discomfort.

**Autumn.** The Confraternity says early autumn is regarded as fairly dry and settled. By October the rain has returned: Porto averages ~149 mm and Santiago ~214 mm, the wettest month of the year at Santiago, and November is about as wet.

**Winter.** Mild and damp. The mean daytime high in January is ~14 °C at Porto and ~12 °C at Santiago, with mean lows of ~6 °C and ~5 °C. Wikipedia says frost at Santiago is common only from December to February, about thirteen days a year, and that snow is uncommon, two or three days a year. The trouble in winter is not cold but rain and short days. The rainfall in January is ~150 mm at Porto and ~196 mm at Santiago.

The Confraternity adds that during periods of low pressure the rain can be heavy and prolonged, and that walkers should be adequately prepared. Wikipedia's infobox gives the walking season as March to October. Winter walks happen; they ask for a lighter plan and more patience.

## Daylight

At the winter solstice there are about nine hours of daylight in Porto, and about the same in Santiago. At the summer solstice there are about fifteen. The clocks add a twist: Portugal keeps Western European Time and Galicia Central European Time, so the clock jumps forward one hour when you cross the border. Computed for each city, the winter sun rises at about eight o'clock in Porto and about nine o'clock in Santiago, and in June sets at about ten past nine in Porto and a quarter past ten in Santiago, by the local clock. In December, plan to finish before the light goes, at about five o'clock in Porto and about six in Santiago.

## A month, in a line each

- **March and April:** cool and fresh, and wet.
- **May and June:** mild, with daytime highs of ~20 to ~23 °C at both ends, and the days at their longest.
- **July and August:** hot and dry, and crowded in the peak holiday months, as the Confraternity says; start early.
- **September:** warm, with the rain beginning to return: ~68 mm at Porto, ~89 mm at Santiago.
- **October and November:** wet, with the days shortening.
- **December to February:** the coldest and shortest days, with rain. If the statistics are a guide, few pilgrims finish then.

## Check before you go

Before any date, look for diversions, closures and, in summer, heat and fire warnings. The Confraternity says road works are still met on the route and are usually marked with a diversion. A path can change between this book's date and your walk.
