# Other ways to split the days, other roads, and the Compostela rule

## Other ways to split the days

The eleven days in this book are the CaminoPlus stage list. They are not the only plan: the CaminoPlus route list gives twelve stages and its stage list eleven, because published plans do not agree. Here are the commonest changes.

- **Start in Galicia.** The Xunta de Galicia describes six stages from Tui to Santiago, which are Days 6 to 11 of this book. They total ~119 km by the Xunta's figures, and they make a walk of six days.
- **End Day 5 in Valença.** Published guides stop at Valença, ~4 km before Tui, and start the next day there. They put Rubiães to Valença at ~16 to ~17 km, and Valença to O Porriño at ~20 km.
- **Three days to Barcelos.** One published plan divides Porto to Barcelos into three shorter days instead of two. If the first day's ~27 km worries you, this is the plan to look for.
- **Split the long Day 3.** Published guides divide Barcelos to Ponte de Lima around the middle of the day, near Vitorino dos Piães, and the app and local advice will tell you what is open for the night. A split makes it roughly two days of ~17 km.
- **Add a rest day.** Pontevedra is the natural place, as its entry says. Ponte de Lima and Padrón are others.

Whatever you choose, the arithmetic is the same: eleven days, ~246 km on this book's figures, so ~22 km a day on average, less on the days that are short.

## Other roads: the Coastal Way and the Spiritual Variant

This book describes only the Central route. The **Coastal Way** is another road to the same city. The Xunta says that its variant crosses the Minho at A Guarda and rejoins the inland route at Redondela, and the Confraternity says it leaves the Central route near Porto and follows the shore. It has its own book in this series, *The Coastal Way*, for those who want the sea.

The **Spiritual Variant** is a sea-and-river alternative that leaves the Central route at Pontevedra. The Confraternity describes it as a relatively new variant that goes to Vilanova de Arousa, on the shores of the Sea of Arousa, then takes a boat up the estuary to Pontecesures and rejoins the Central route to Santiago. The Xunta describes a related maritime and river itinerary, the Mar de Arousa and Río Ulla Route, which commemorates the arrival of the body of St James by sea, and says that the walk from Padrón to Santiago is ~25 km. A route that depends on a boat depends on the boat: ask locally before you plan around it.

The Confraternity says that most pilgrims start in Porto but many walk from Lisbon. The catalog lists that route at ~620 km, and it reaches Porto first. It is not in this book.

## The Compostela rule

The Compostela is the certificate the Pilgrim's Office in Santiago gives to those who come to the tomb of the Apostle for religious and/or spiritual reasons and follow the routes of the Way of St James on foot, by bicycle or on horseback. The Office asks for at least the last hundred kilometres on foot or on horseback, or the last two hundred kilometres by bicycle, shown by the credencial, stamped along the route. On foot or on horseback, the Office asks for at least a hundred continuous kilometres on the same route to Santiago that it recognises. The final stage must include at least the last one leading into the Cathedral.

The Way may be done in stages, if they follow in chronological and geographical order. A pilgrim who walks only the minimum distance must get the credencial stamped at the start and the end of each stage, with the date. A pilgrim who has already walked part of a recognised route outside Spain needs no less than seventy kilometres in Spain. The Cabildo, the cathedral's governing council, reserves the right to grant the Compostela in special and exceptional cases.

Here is the arithmetic for this road, with its sources. The Xunta gives the Galician Way from Tui as ~119 km, and a milestone it notes in Tui reads ~115 km to Santiago. Wikipedia gives figures from ~107 to ~116 km in different articles. All of them are more than a hundred kilometres, but by a margin that depends on the source and on where you start counting. If you walk from Porto, the Spanish stretch alone is ~119 km on the Xunta's figures, which is more than the seventy kilometres in Spain that the Office names for those who have walked a recognised route outside Spain.

None of that is a decision. The Office decides, and rules change. If you plan to start in Tui, in Valença or anywhere near the limit, ask the Pilgrim's Office before you go.
