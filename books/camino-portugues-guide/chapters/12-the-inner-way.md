# The inner Way

## Why people walk

People walk this road for faith, for sport, for grief, for curiosity, for a rest from work, for a promise made to someone else. The Pilgrim's Office asks that the Compostela be sought for religious and/or spiritual reasons, and says so plainly; the road itself asks nothing of the kind. Every reason is a good one, and none of them needs to be explained to the people you meet.

The people before you had every kind of reason too. The Xunta names queens and kings, Elizabeth of Portugal and Manuel I among them. It adds that in the nineteenth century, the century of the fewest pilgrims, more than eighty per cent of the foreign pilgrims were Portuguese. This was a road ordinary people took, as well as royalty.

## What the road gives

It gives you hours with no task. For eleven days the feet, the weather and the next town set the agenda, and what you do with the quiet is yours.

It also has places that seem made for thinking. A river that, Wikipedia reports, the ancients said took away memory. A border in the middle of a bridge. A hill, the *humilladoiro*, where the Xunta says pilgrims once knelt on first catching sight of their goal. You need not believe a legend to be moved by the hour in which you stand where people have stood for centuries.

## Questions for the walk

Take one a day, or none. They are better walked than answered.

- What are you carrying that is not in your pack?
- Whom would you like to have beside you on this road, and what would you say to them?
- What do you want to remember from today, and what would you like to forget, as the old story says the Lima could make a pilgrim forget?
- What would "enough" look like today, for your feet and for your heart?
- What are you walking towards, and what are you walking away from? Is the second a reason to turn round, or only a reason to keep going?

You do not have to arrive at an answer in Santiago. A better question is a fair thing to bring home.
