# Galicia, from Tui to Pontevedra: Days 6 to 8

## The character of these days

The next six days are Galicia, and the Xunta de Galicia describes them in six official stages from Tui to Santiago. This chapter has the first three: through the granite country around O Porriño, along the busy N-550 corridor, and down to the head of the Ría de Vigo, a long sea inlet, before the road turns inland to Pontevedra.

The Galician distances come from the Xunta. Its route page gives ~119 km for the whole Galician Way; its stage pages add up to a little more, ~120 km; and the OpenStreetMap route measured in the CaminoPlus catalog notes differs from the Xunta by no more than ~2.5 km on any stage. Treat each figure as a good guide, not as a measurement.

### Day 6: Tui to O Porriño (~18 km)

The Xunta gives this stage as ~18 km and rates it Low, with an estimate of ~3 h 35 min. Its own page disagrees with itself: it marks each of the three sections of the stage Medium-High. Take the first full day in Spain gently.

You leave Tui through its historic quarter to San Bartolomé Park, where a pre-Romanesque church stands. Then comes country. The way passes the chapel of A Virxe do Camiño, the Virgin of the Way, and the bridge the Xunta calls the San Telmo bridge, also known as As Febres, then runs through a dense wood called A Ribeira to the small village of A Madanela. The Louro, the little river that divides the municipalities of Tui and O Porriño, runs beside the path. At Orbenlle the Xunta says you can continue by Os Eidos or by As Gándaras.

In the distance you can see the granite quarries that have made this place famous. The Xunta says the stone is known as pink granite and is exported to places as far away as the United States and Japan.

O Porriño is an industrial town, as the Xunta says, with one of the highest rates of population growth in the province of Pontevedra. Its look is the work of the architect Antonio Palacios, who was born there in 1874; the Town Hall, one of his greatest works, dates from 1924. The Xunta also points to the Templete de la Red de San Luis, which was once the entrance to a Madrid underground station, and to the fountain of O Cristo, dated 1907. It is a working town more than a postcard. Rest your feet and prepare for tomorrow.

### Day 7: O Porriño to Redondela (~16 km)

The Xunta gives ~16 km and rates the stage Low, with an estimate of ~3 h 10 min. Its three sections add up to ~15 km, so the page differs from itself by about a kilometre. The OpenStreetMap route comes to ~16 km as well.

This day has the road in it, and the Xunta is plain about it. After the chapel of As Angustias the way runs along the N-550, and the Xunta says it is especially important to watch for vehicles; a way that diverts to the right avoids the traffic. The Louro can be heard but not seen. You pass A Rúa, once the municipal capital of Mos, where a stone cross inscribed "Camino de Santiago" marks the street that leads to a cross monument. At O Enxertado there is a slight climb to the small chapel of Santiaguiño de Anta, in an oak wood, the *carballeira*.

Then the old road shows itself. The Xunta says that a Roman milestone, one Roman mile being about 1,480 metres, guides you towards Redondela. It once belonged to Via XIX, the road linking Braga and Astorga. The woods are pine and eucalyptus, and the section ends at a place the Xunta calls legendary, Chan das Pipas. Saxamonde, Quintela and O Muro lead to Redondela, which you enter by the Ponteareas Square.

Redondela is known as the village of the viaducts, Wikipedia says, for its two railway viaducts of the nineteenth century. It is also where another road joins yours. The Xunta says that the coastal variant of the Portuguese Way, which crosses the Minho at A Guarda, meets the inland route here. From Redondela on, those who came by the sea and those who came by the Central route share one path. That coastal road is the subject of *The Coastal Way*.

### Day 8: Redondela to Pontevedra (~18 km)

The Xunta gives ~18 km, rates the stage Medium and estimates ~4 h 35 min. The OpenStreetMap route is longer, ~20 km, and published guides give ~18 to ~19 km. This is the largest gap on the Galician side, so allow for a longer day than the Xunta's.

The first half belongs to the water. After the chapel of As Angustias, in the parish of Cesantes, the Ría de Vigo opens on your left. The Xunta points to the islands of San Simón and San Antón, and says that the remains of the galleons of the Battle of Rande, fought in 1702, lie beneath the water. Rande Bridge, a cable-stayed bridge, rises above the rafts of the mussel farmers. Then comes Arcade, and the way follows its streets down to the medieval Sampaio Bridge over the Verdugo. The municipality of Pontevedra begins at the middle of the bridge.

After Ponte Sampaio the road climbs along ancient stone paths. The Xunta says that these include sections of the Roman Via XIX, such as the climb of the Brea Vella de A Canicouva. Then the way approaches Pontevedra by several small places, among them Santa Comba de Bértola and Tomeza, and enters the city, passing the shrine of the Pilgrim Virgin on the way to the old quarter.

Pontevedra is a place to stay a second night. Wikipedia says its old town is the second most important historic centre in Galicia, after Santiago, and that the city has pedestrianised its centre. The circular church of the Pilgrim Virgin, which Wikipedia dates to the eighteenth century, has a floor plan in the shape of a scallop shell. Wikipedia adds that the carrack Santa María, one of Columbus's ships, originally named La Gallega, was built in Pontevedra. You will cross the medieval Burgo Bridge tomorrow; Wikipedia says there are scallop shells carved in its arches.
