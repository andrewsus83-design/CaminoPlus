# Galicia

## The character of these days

Galicia begins at O Cebreiro, and from there the Xunta de Galicia measures ~155 km to Santiago by the San Xil route, or ~162 km by Samos. The country is greener and wetter than anything behind you, with small hamlets, oak woods and eucalyptus, and place names in Galician, many with the article *O* or *A* in front (O Cebreiro, A Calzada, A Salceda). Santiago receives ~1,500 mm of rain a year, so carry a waterproof whatever the month.

From Sarria on you walk with many other people: about 65 per cent of those who walked the French Way in 2024 started there. Distances in this chapter come from the Xunta's stage pages, which give a difficulty rating and an estimated time for each stage. Some other tables differ by up to ~4 km, and the days say where.

### Day 27: O Cebreiro to Triacastela (~22 km)

The Xunta rates this stage "Medium-High" and gives ~22.2 km. From ~1,300 m the Way passes Liñares, Hospital da Condesa, the Alto do Poio, at ~1,330 m, Fonfría and O Biduedo, and drops to Triacastela, at ~665 m, a descent of ~635 m. It is mostly downhill and still hard on knees, so go slowly.

### Day 28: Triacastela to Sarria (~18 km)

Here the Way divides. The shorter route by San Xil is ~18 km, rated "Medium-Low" by the Xunta, through small hamlets such as A Balsa, Montán, Pintín and Calvor. The route by Samos is ~25 km, also "Medium-Low", by San Cristovo do Real and the Monastery of Samos, which the Xunta calls one of the oldest monasteries in the western world, from the 6th century. King Alfonso II, who backed the first church over the tomb, was brought up there.

Sarria is, according to Wikipedia, ~115 km from the cathedral. It has the 13th-century church of O Salvador and the Convent of A Madalena, founded in the early 13th century as a pilgrims' hospital.

### Day 29: Sarria to Portomarín (~22 km)

The Way leaves Sarria by the bridge of A Áspera and goes towards Barbadelo, ~4 km on, where the church of Santiago can be visited. The Xunta rates the day "Medium-High", and warns that after about a kilometre beside the railway there is the hardest hill of the stage. Then comes oak and meadow country, and the bridge over the Miño into Portomarín.

Portomarín was moved. The Belesar reservoir flooded the old town in the 1960s, and its main buildings, including the church of San Xoán, were rebuilt on higher ground.

### Day 30: Portomarín to Palas de Rei (~25 km)

You cross the reservoir by a footbridge and climb steeply to Gonzar, ~8 km on. The Xunta rates that stretch "Medium-High", and the whole stage "Medium". Then comes Castromaior and Ligonde, whose hospital received the Emperor Charles I and his son Philip II, the best-known pilgrims of the 16th century, and so to Palas de Rei, a name that the Xunta says comes from a supposed royal palace.

### Day 31: Palas de Rei to Arzúa (~29 km)

The longest day of the last week. The Xunta gives ~14.6 km to Melide and ~14.5 km on to Arzúa, while Wikipedia's example itinerary has the whole day as ~25 km. From Palas the Way goes through leafy woods by San Xiao do Camiño and O Leboreiro to Melide, where the Camino Primitivo joins. Melide is known for *polbo á feira*, Galician octopus.

From Melide the path is "easy to walk on", says the Xunta, with gradual climbs and descents, through Boente and Castañeda to Arzúa, where the Camino del Norte joins. Arzúa gives its name to a cheese with protected designation of origin.

### Day 32: Arzúa to O Pedrouzo (~18 km)

A gentle day. The Xunta rates it "Low", and gives ~18.4 km, through meadows and woods of oak and eucalyptus, with hamlets such as A Calzada, A Salceda, A Brea and Santa Irene. The warning is the road: in the first half the Way follows stretches of the N-547 highway and crosses it several times, so watch for vehicles.

O Pedrouzo, also written Arca, is in the municipality of O Pino, the last before Santiago. It is tempting to push on tonight. Stay, and arrive fresh tomorrow.

### Day 33: O Pedrouzo to Santiago de Compostela (~20 km)

The Xunta gives ~20.2 km for the last day, and Wikipedia's example itinerary ~16.5 km. After Santo Antón you climb to the municipality of Santiago, by O Amenal and San Paio, with Lavacolla about halfway.

Monte do Gozo, the Hill of Joy, at ~370 m and ~4.5 km from the centre, is where pilgrims traditionally cheered at the first sight of the cathedral's spires. Then the old town, and the cathedral. The chapter on arriving says what to do next.
