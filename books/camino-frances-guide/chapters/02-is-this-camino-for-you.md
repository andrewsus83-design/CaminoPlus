# Is this Camino for you?

## Long, not technical

The French Way asks for time and stamina more than skill. It is a walking path, and most of the distance is on tracks, field edges and village streets. The Xunta de Galicia rates the Galician section "Medium", and its stages run from "Low" to "Medium-High". The days in this book run from ~18 to ~30 km. What makes the Francés demanding is the number of them: 33 in a row, with a rest day or two on top.

A fair test comes from the Confraternity of St James: aim to be able to walk 20 km or more a day, over varied ground, with your pack on. If you can do that on a training weekend, you are ready to start slowly. The Confraternity adds that you will get fitter by walking the Camino itself.

## The days that ask the most

A few days are harder than the rest, and it is better to know them now. The figures are worked out from altitudes in Wikipedia's route tables, so read them as ~ guides.

- **Day 1, over the Pyrenees.** A net climb of ~1,250 m, from ~175 m to ~1,430 m, before the drop to Roncesvalles.
- **Day 24, down from the Cruz de Ferro.** About ~850 m of descent in ~19 km, hard on knees.
- **Day 26, up to O Cebreiro.** A net climb of ~800 m over ~30 km.
- **The Meseta.** Not steep, but open, and in summer hot. Day 17 passes ~17 to ~18 km without a village.

## Who it suits

- **First-time long-distance walkers** who want a path with a long tradition and plenty of company. It is the most walked of the Caminos: in 2024, 47 per cent of Compostelas went to people on the French Way, against 19 per cent for the Portuguese Way, the next most walked.
- **People with about five weeks** and a wish to see Spain change by the day: Basque hills, vineyards, a plateau, mountains, green Galicia.
- **People who like cities in the middle of a walk.** Pamplona, Logroño, Burgos and León make natural pauses.

## Who might choose another

- **If you have a week or two,** do not squeeze the whole Francés into it. Start later (the next chapter shows where), or choose a shorter Camino. In the app's catalog the Portuguese Way from Porto is ~240 km, the English Way ~120 km and the walk to Finisterre and Muxía ~90 km.
- **If you want a quiet road,** the Francés in high season is not it, especially after Sarria. Choose another season, another Camino, or start where fewer people do. Of the people who walked the Francés in 2024, about 65 per cent started in Sarria.
- **If you want the sea,** look at the Coastal and Northern Ways. The Northern Way is ~820 km, according to the catalog.
- **If heat or a long month away worries you,** read "When to go" and "Body, weather and safety" before you decide. A shorter walk, in a milder month, is a real Camino too.

## Every reason is a good reason

People walk for faith, for sport, for grief, to think, to stop thinking, because a friend did, or because a calendar had a gap. At the end of the walk the Pilgrim's Office asks about your motivation, as the chapter on arriving explains. Its credencial is, in the Office's own words, for those who walk with a Christian sentiment, "even if it is only with an attitude of search". The chapter on the inner Way says more.
