# When to go

The French Way crosses mountains, a high plateau and a wet green coast of hills, so "the weather" is five weathers. No month is perfect along all of it. What follows are patterns, not forecasts. Before any day, look at the local weather.

## How many people, and when

Most pilgrims finish between May and September, says Wikipedia. Its chart of the Pilgrim's Office figures for 2024 shows how steep the seasons are on this road:

- **December to February:** ~1,000 to ~1,500 a month.
- **March:** ~11,000. **April:** ~19,000.
- **May and June:** ~32,500 and ~31,000.
- **July:** ~26,500. **August:** ~32,000.
- **September:** ~30,500. **October:** ~24,000.
- **November:** ~5,000.

The monthly figures add up to less than the year's total of "over 235,000" that the same article gives, so read them as a shape, not a count.

## What the seasons are like

The temperatures below are monthly averages for 1991 to 2020, as listed on Wikipedia (the figures for Burgos and Santiago come from the Spanish weather service). Extremes are far wider than averages.

**Spring (March to June).** Quiet in March, busy by May. In Burgos, at ~865 m, the average June day reaches ~24 °C. Early in the season, ask about conditions on the Pyrenees and the high ground of León and Galicia.

**Summer (July and August).** Hot, and on the Meseta exposed. The average July high is ~28 °C in Pamplona and ~28 °C in Burgos; the records are ~42 °C in Pamplona and ~39 °C in Burgos. Galicia is cooler (the average July high in Santiago is ~25 °C) and much drier than in winter. In Pamplona the San Fermín festival runs from 6 to 14 July, and the city fills.

**Autumn (September and October).** The crowds thin by October and the days shorten. The rain also returns: Santiago averages ~89 mm in September and ~214 mm in October.

**Winter (November to February).** Few walkers, short days and cold. In Burgos the average January day reaches only ~7 °C and the average night falls to about freezing; the record low is about 22 degrees below freezing. In Santiago the average January day is ~12 °C, and the month brings ~196 mm of rain. The high route over the Pyrenees is closed to walkers in winter; in November 2025 Navarre's government announced a closure from 1 November to 31 March, so check the notice for your year. Many places that serve pilgrims in summer may be shut, so ask before you rely on one.

## Rain, heat and light

Santiago receives ~1,500 mm of rain a year, against ~720 mm in Pamplona and ~550 mm in Burgos. The Xunta de Galicia advises a waterproof garment whatever the season, and a cap against the sun, and against cold in the high places.

Daylight changes more than people expect. Anywhere on the road, the day lasts ~9 hours at the December solstice and ~15 hours at the June solstice, by a standard solar calculation. In December the sun rises between about half past eight in Saint-Jean and nine in Santiago, so a winter walker has little time to waste.

## Holy Years

A Jacobean Holy Year occurs when 25 July, the feast of St James, falls on a Sunday. The next one is 2027, then 2032. The Confraternity of St James says Holy Years bring a huge surge in pilgrims, and that Santiago becomes extremely busy, particularly around Easter and the feast of St James. It recommends booking accommodation as far ahead as you can.

## Choosing

If you want company and warm days, May, June and September suit most people. If you want fewer people, April and October are quieter, and you should carry a coat. If you must walk in July or August, start early every day and plan for the Meseta's heat.

**Check before you go:** the route over the Pyrenees, snow and fog on the high stages, heat warnings, and wildfires.
