# La Rioja

## The character of these days

La Rioja is small, and the Camino crosses it in three days, between Logroño and the border with Castilla y León. It is a wine country. Rioja became in 1991 Spain's first *denominación de origen calificada* (DOCa), a quality designation for wine, according to Wikipedia, and its grapes grow in La Rioja, Navarre and the Basque province of Álava. The country between Logroño and Santo Domingo is low and rolling, with vines and small towns.

### Day 8: Logroño to Nájera (~29 km)

A long day over low hills, from ~385 m at Logroño up to ~640 m near Ventosa and down to Nájera, at ~490 m. Navarrete, ~13 km along, is a natural place to split it.

Nájera is on the Najerilla river. It was once the capital of the Kingdom of Nájera-Pamplona, until Castile conquered it in 1054. The church of Santa María la Real was founded in 1052 by King García Sánchez III and is the burial place of kings of Navarre. Look in the app for when it can be visited.

### Day 9: Nájera to Santo Domingo de la Calzada (~21 km)

A short day by way of Azofra and Cirueña, the highest point at ~755 m, then down to Santo Domingo, at ~640 m. From Azofra a variant leads towards the monastery of San Millán de la Cogolla; check before you go if it interests you.

Santo Domingo de la Calzada is named for Saint Dominic, who died in 1109 and helped pilgrims on the road. In its cathedral a pair of hens is kept at all times in the choir loft, descendants of the chickens of a legend: according to Wikipedia, two cooked, beheaded chickens came back to life to clear a pilgrim who had been wrongly accused of theft.

### Day 10: Santo Domingo de la Calzada to Belorado (~23 km)

The road climbs by way of Grañón, ~725 m, to a high point near ~845 m, and drops towards Belorado, at ~770 m. Near Redecilla del Camino you pass from La Rioja into the province of Burgos, and so into Castilla y León.

Belorado is the first town of a new region, and ~130 m higher than Santo Domingo. The plateau is ahead of you, so fill your bottle.
