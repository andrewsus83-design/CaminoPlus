# Going home

## The second stage

The walk ends in Santiago, and going home is the next stage. The routine you left is waiting where you left it, and you have changed a little, so give yourself some days before everything starts again at full speed.

Write down what you want to keep while it is clear: a few lines about the days that mattered, the people, the questions you carried. If you kept a journal in the CaminoPlus app, read it a week after you are home. It is easier to see a month once it is behind you.

## Keeping the Way alive

Walking is the habit to keep. An hour on foot each day costs little, and it keeps the legs and the quiet that the road gave you. Keep in touch with the people you met, and consider the associations of friends of the Camino, whose founding in the 1950s and 1960s, the Xunta says, began the road's revival.

## Walking again

You may want to go back to the road, to the same Camino or to another. The French Way is one of 14 routes in the CaminoPlus catalog, from the sea to the mountains. The English Way, the Portuguese Way and the walk to Finisterre and Muxía are shorter, and the Northern Way is ~820 km. You do not have to decide yet.

*Buen Camino*, again and always.
