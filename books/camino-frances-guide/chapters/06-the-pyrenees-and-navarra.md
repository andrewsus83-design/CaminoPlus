# The Pyrenees and Navarra

## The character of these days

The first week begins with the biggest climb of the walk and settles into green hills, Pamplona and the rolling country beyond. Navarre is a land of two languages: Roncesvalles is also Orreaga in Basque, and Pamplona is Iruña. It has fine Romanesque churches, at Eunate and Torres del Río among other places.

The distances in this book are rounded. For days 1 to 3 they come from the CaminoPlus stage data, and from day 4 on from public tables, chiefly Wikipedia's example itinerary and the Xunta de Galicia (see "Sources"). Tables differ by a kilometre or two, and the days say so where the gap is bigger. Many pilgrims split days differently; this book follows the usual stage towns.

### Day 1: Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port to Roncesvalles (~24 km)

The hardest day comes first. Saint-Jean sits at ~175 m, the road climbs to the Lepoeder pass at ~1,430 m, and then it drops to Roncesvalles at ~950 m. That is a net climb of ~1,250 m, with the border crossing on the way.

There are two routes. The high one over Lepoeder is closed to walkers in winter (Navarre's government announced a closure from 1 November to 31 March in November 2025). The lower road by Valcarlos and the Ibañeta pass, at ~1,057 m, is the way in bad weather and in winter. Check the notice for your year.

Roncesvalles is a village below the pass where, in 778, the Basques ambushed the rearguard of Charlemagne's army, the battle behind the legend of Roland. Go slowly, carry food and water for the climb, and remember that this day is not the whole walk.

In the app: stage 1.

### Day 2: Roncesvalles to Zubiri (~21 km)

A gentler day, mostly downhill, with a pass or two in the middle. You pass through Burguete and Espinal, over the Mezkiritz pass at ~925 m and the Erro pass at ~800 m, and drop to Zubiri, at ~530 m.

The thing to watch is the end. The last ~6 km fall ~270 m, steep enough to find out how your knees feel. Poles help, and so does taking it slowly.

In the app: stage 2.

### Day 3: Zubiri to Pamplona (~20 km)

The path runs through Larrasoaña, Zuriáin and Zabaldika, then the towns of Villava and Burlada, and into Pamplona, at ~450 m. Sources give this stage as ~20 to ~22 km.

Pamplona's old walls and the Portal de Francia, the gate pilgrims from France entered by, are worth a look as you arrive. The cathedral is 15th-century Gothic, with a 13th- and 14th-century cloister. The San Fermín festival runs from 6 to 14 July, and the city is then very busy. At other times it makes a good first rest day.

In the app: stage 3.

### Day 4: Pamplona to Puente la Reina (~24 km)

The day's climb is the Alto del Perdón, at ~765 m, on the Sierra del Perdón ridge, where a sculpture of iron pilgrims by Vicente Galbete, put up in 1996, stands against the sky. The descent is steep: ~285 m in ~4 km down to Uterga.

If you have energy, a detour of ~2 km from Muruzábal leads to Santa María de Eunate, an octagonal 12th-century Romanesque church standing alone in open country. Puente la Reina, "the Queen's bridge", is where the Aragonese Way joins.

### Day 5: Puente la Reina to Estella (~22 km)

A rolling day between ~345 m and ~500 m, through hill villages: Mañeru, Cirauqui, Lorca and Villatuerta. Estella was founded in 1090, by a charter from King Sancho Ramírez, and became a landmark of the pilgrim road. Its Romanesque buildings include the church of San Pedro de la Rúa and the Palace of the Kings of Navarre.

The Xunta names Estella, in 1963, as one of the two places where the first modern associations of friends of the Camino were founded, the start of the road's revival. The other was Paris, in 1950.

### Day 6: Estella to Los Arcos (~22 km)

You pass Ayegui and the Monastery of Irache, a former Benedictine monastery whose origins go back to the 8th century, though the surviving buildings are later. Then come Azqueta and Villamayor de Monjardín, ~675 m up, with the ruins of the castle of San Esteban de Deyo on the hill above.

After Villamayor, Spanish Wikipedia's table lists only one small place before Los Arcos, ~13 km on, so fill your bottle there.

### Day 7: Los Arcos to Logroño (~28 km)

The longest day of the week. Sansol and Torres del Río face each other across a river, and Torres del Río has an octagonal Romanesque church, linked with the Templars. Viana, ~10 km short of Logroño, is a common place to split the day; Wikipedia says Cesare Borgia is buried there.

Between Viana and Logroño you leave Navarre for La Rioja. Logroño, on the Ebro, is the capital of La Rioja and a centre of the Rioja wine trade.
