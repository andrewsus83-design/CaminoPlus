# Welcome

The French Way is the most walked of the Caminos. It begins in Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port, a small town in the French Pyrenees, and ends at the cathedral of Santiago de Compostela, in Galicia. The CaminoPlus catalog gives it as ~773 km, walked in 33 usual stages. That is about a month of walking, and more with rest days.

**The promise of this book is simple.** By the last page you will know whether this is your Camino, where to start and how long to give it, when to go, what to carry, and what each of the 33 days asks of you.

## How to use it

If you are a month or more away, read the chapters in order up to "Before you go". Then read the five regional chapters, which hold the days, and the chapters that follow them.

If you are on the road tonight, go straight to your stage. Each day says what the walking is like, what is worth a pause and where to take care. Where a day has its own stage in the CaminoPlus app today, its entry ends with a pointer. Not every day does yet.

If you are only dreaming, read "Is this Camino for you?", then "The inner Way". The rest can wait.

## What this book is not

It is not a live map, a timetable or a booking service. It names no hostels, cafes or shops, and it gives no prices, opening hours or bed counts, because they change from season to season and a wrong one costs a tired person dearly. For those, use the CaminoPlus app and ask locally.

Every distance carries a tilde, **~**, because it is approximate. Published stage lengths differ from one source to the next, by a kilometre or two and sometimes more, and the book says so where it matters. Where something depends on the season (a closure, a diversion, snow, heat), it says *check before you go*, and means it.

## A few words you will meet

*Buen Camino*, "good Way", is what pilgrims and locals say to one another as you pass. An *albergue* is a pilgrims' hostel. The *credencial* is your pilgrim's passport, a folded card that you carry and have stamped along the way; each stamp is a *sello*. The *Compostela* is the certificate that the Pilgrim's Office in Santiago gives to those who meet its rules, which you will meet in plain words in the chapter on where to start.

They are explained again when they first matter, and used plainly after that.

Pack lightly, start slowly, and take your time over the long hill. *Buen Camino.*
