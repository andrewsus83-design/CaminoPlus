# The inner Way

## Why people walk

People walk the French Way for faith, for sport, for grief, for a pause between one chapter of life and the next, to see Spain at the speed of a person, or because they were asked to, and some cannot say why. All of those are real reasons, and none needs defending.

The road itself began as a Christian pilgrimage to the tomb of St James, and it still ends at his cathedral. The Pilgrim's Office, which issues the Compostela, says that its credencial is for pilgrims who walk with a Christian sentiment, "even if it is only with an attitude of search". The Xunta de Galicia describes the pilgrimage today as spiritual and ecumenical, and open to knowledge, friendship and mutual understanding. You can walk in that spirit without sharing the faith, and the road asks only that you walk with respect for the people who do.

## What a month of walking is for

The Francés has room built into it. You cross five regions, each with its own landscape. The days are long enough for thoughts to run their course, and the evenings are for company or for quiet. The Xunta writes that, for the medieval mind, the pilgrim was sent from heaven and was to be treated as Christ himself, and that the road's hospitals grew from that idea. You are, in the oldest sense of the word, a guest. Arrive tired, grateful and polite.

At the Cruz de Ferro, ~1,500 m up, pilgrims leave a stone brought from home, a custom with disputed origins, often described as a way of letting go of a worry or a loss. You do not need to carry one. But it is worth asking, before you leave, what you would put down if you could.

## Questions for the walk

Take one a day, or none.

- What are you carrying that you could leave on the road?
- What did you notice today that you would have missed in a car?
- Who walked with you today, in the flesh or in your mind?
- What does your body need that you have been ignoring?
- What will you be glad to have done by the time you reach Santiago?

## Walking alone, walking together

The Francés can be walked alone and in company, sometimes in the same hour. Greet the people you pass. Let a silence be a silence. If you want to be alone, say so kindly, and if someone else does, respect it. A friendship made on the road may last for years or for an afternoon, and both belong to it.
