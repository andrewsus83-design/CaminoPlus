# With the app, and sources

## With the CaminoPlus app

**CaminoPlus** is the offline-first companion app for the Camino de Santiago, with maps that work without signal, stays and cafes, a pilgrim passport with stamps, a journal and friends. The paid plan is called **Pilgrim Plus**. The app is still in development, so look in the app for what is available on the French Way today, and use it, not this book, for beds, cafes, closures and weather.

A reminder about the passport: the Pilgrim's Office asks for the stamped credencial. A passport in any app is a keepsake until the Office says otherwise.

## Sources and thanks

This book was written with AI assistance, from public sources, and not from a walk. That is why it sends you to local information for anything that changes. A fact ledger that goes with it lists every fact against the page it came from. The chief sources are:

- **The Xunta de Galicia**, on its official Camino de Santiago portal (caminodesantiago.gal): the stage pages for Galicia, with their lengths, difficulty ratings and descriptions, the history of the Way, the pages on preparation, packing and public hostels, and the page on the pilgrims' Mass.
- **The Pilgrim's Reception Office of the Cathedral of Santiago** (oficinadelperegrino.com): the rules of the Compostela, the credencial and the certificate of distance.
- **The Confraternity of St James** (csj.org.uk): the Compostela, Holy Years, training and packing.
- **Wikipedia**, in English, Spanish and German, for the example itineraries, the distances to Santiago and altitudes of the route's towns, the history of its places, the 2024 pilgrim statistics from the Pilgrim's Office, and the climate tables. The articles on the French Way, Camino de Santiago, Santiago Cathedral, Codex Calixtinus, the Jacobean Holy Year, the festival of San Fermín and the towns on the road were used.
- **Press and tourism pages**: the Navarre press report on the winter closure of the high route over the Pyrenees, the UNESCO World Heritage Centre for the inscription of the road in 1993, and the tourism pages of Pamplona and Ponferrada for the Portal de Francia and the Templar castle.
- **A standard solar calculation** for the hours of daylight.
- **The CaminoPlus route catalog**, for the names and order of the 33 stages and for the distances of the first three.

The tables disagree with each other by a kilometre or two, and sometimes more, so every distance in this book is marked ~. If you know a better figure, or a fact that has changed, tell CaminoPlus.

Thanks go to the people who keep the Way: the associations of friends of the Camino, the hosts who open the hostels, and the people in the towns and villages who point the way and say *Buen Camino* to a stranger.

## A short glossary

- **Albergue**: a pilgrims' hostel with shared rooms.
- **Botafumeiro**: the great censer of Santiago cathedral.
- **Buen Camino**: "good Way", the greeting between pilgrims.
- **Compostela**: the Latin certificate the Pilgrim's Office issues.
- **Credencial**: the pilgrim's passport.
- **Hospitalero**: the host of a pilgrims' hostel.
- **Menú del peregrino**: a set meal for pilgrims.
- **Meseta**: the high plateau of central Spain.
- **Palloza**: an archaic house type, some of which survive in O Cebreiro.
- **Sello**: a stamp in the credencial.
- **Xacobeo**: the Galician name for a Holy Year.
