# How to use this book

This is a short book for a short road. It is made to go with the Camino Inglés, the English Way, a road of about a week, and it has six days: one to read for each day of walking, or six days at home, before the road or after it. If your walk turns out longer or shorter, nothing breaks. Read a day twice, leave one for later, or take two on a rest day.

Every day has a morning reading of about five minutes and an evening reflection of about three.

**In the morning** there are three parts.

- **The passage.** A few lines from *The Practice of the Presence of God*, a small book of conversations and letters by a seventeenth-century lay brother in Paris. The words are quoted exactly as an old English edition printed them, old spellings and capital letters included. Read the passage twice, once for the sense and once for the sound, aloud if you can. The old wording makes you slow down, which suits it.
- **The reflection.** This part is ours, not Brother Lawrence's. It starts from something on the road, a chore, a wandering mind, a flat afternoon, and lets the passage open it. It tries to say what the passage says, to admit where it is hard, and to ask no more of an ordinary day than a day can give.
- **For the walk.** A question to carry for the next hour and one small practice that needs nothing but attention. Leave the notebook in the pack. These are better done on your feet.

**In the evening** there are two more.

- **At the end of the day.** A look back over the day you have just lived, with the morning's passage in mind. It is honest about tiredness, and it asks two or three gentle questions. You do not have to write anything down. They are questions to take to bed.
- **A short prayer.** Original, and short. If you pray, it is yours to use. If you pray in another way, change the words. If you do not pray, take it as a wish, said into the dark.

## A Christian book, and who it is for

Brother Lawrence was a Christian, a lay brother in a Catholic religious community, and everything he says is said to God and about God. The practice that gives his book its title, keeping himself in God's presence, is a Christian practice. We say so plainly so that nobody is surprised.

People walk the Camino for many reasons: faith, sport, grief, curiosity, a rest from work. All of them are welcome here. You do not need to share Lawrence's faith to keep him company for a week. What anyone can recognise is the shape of what he describes: small turnings of the attention, made often, with no fuss when you forget. If you borrow that shape without his name for God, borrow it knowing that it is a borrowing, and that he means a person, not a technique for calm. Where he is hard to follow, we say so.

## The shape of the six days

The days do not follow the order of the original. They follow a walk.

- **Day 1, setting out.** How little is asked of you.
- **Day 2, the ordinary task.** The errand or chore you would rather not do.
- **Day 3, distraction and return.** The mind that runs off, and the way back.
- **Day 4, dryness.** The day when nothing is felt and the road goes on.
- **Day 5, the small kindnesses.** A straw on the path, a gate, a word.
- **Day 6, going on.** What is worth carrying home.

The days are a shape, not a timetable. The book cannot know which day of your walk will be the flat one. Read the day that fits the day you are having. If you are tired, the passage and the prayer are enough, and the rest will keep until morning.

## On the road and at home

Read the morning part wherever you stop: a bench, a bunk, a café table while the soup comes. Then put the phone away and take the question with you for an hour. Keep the evening part for last, once the boots are off. At home the pattern is the same, with tea in the morning and the evening before bed.

Two words you will meet. An *albergue* is the simple hostel where pilgrims sleep, often in bunks, often with a shared kitchen. *Buen Camino* is what walkers say to one another on the road, roughly, may your Way go well. Other words are explained where they first appear.

Read slowly. These are letters and notes, short on purpose, and they keep better one at a time.
