# Tomar to Coimbra: Days 7 to 10

## The character of these days

Four days, ~92 km on the table's figures, from the Templar town to the university city. The plain ends at Tomar. The way climbs into the limestone hills of the Serra de Sicó, among olive groves, vines and oak woods, and follows old Roman roads. The Confraternity calls the stages between Tomar and Coimbra delightful.

This is where the regional board's account takes over. It lists Tomar, Alvaiázere, Rabaçal and Coimbra as its stage places, and its article on the road names Ansião as well. The table this book follows adds Ansião and Cernache, which shortens the days. Others join Alvaiázere to Rabaçal in one long day: the older Confraternity sheet gives ~33 km. Choose by your legs, and ask where you can sleep.

### Day 7: Tomar to Alvaiázere (~32 km)

The first serious climbs. You leave through the old centre, cross the Nabão by the bridge of King Manuel I and go out by the old road towards Coimbra. The regional board says the way follows a stretch of the Roman road that once linked Tomar with Conímbriga. Then come ups and downs through olive groves, vines and native woodland, with outcrops of limestone from the Sicó massif.

Tables give ~31 to ~32 km, so this is a long day on tired legs, after three days of ~30 km. Wikipedia places Alvaiázere in the district of Leiria. Its name comes from an Arabic word that means "the falconer". Ask the night before where you can buy lunch.

### Day 8: Alvaiázere to Ansião (~23 km)

A shorter, easier day, a rest for the legs after the last one. The way crosses the Serra de Ariques, among holm oaks and olive trees. The older sheet makes Alvaiázere to Rabaçal one day of ~33 km, so this is the day on which you decide which plan you are walking.

The regional board's guide tells a local legend about Ansião: that its name comes from alms given by Queen Isabel, the Holy Queen, to a poor old man, *um ancião*. A bridge at the edge of town is tied to her pilgrimage. It is a legend, and a pleasant one for a day that asks little.

### Day 9: Ansião to Cernache (~26 km)

The table calls this one of the loveliest days since Lisbon, and an easy one. It is mostly a gentle descent by old Roman roads and paths among vines, olive groves and woods along streams. At Rabaçal there is a Roman villa, whose mosaics the regional board dates to the fourth and fifth centuries, and a local cheese with a protected name.

The highlight comes at the end: Conímbriga. Wikipedia says it was not the largest Roman city in Portugal but is the best preserved, a national monument since 1910, ~16 km from Coimbra. It lay on the Roman road that linked Lisbon and Braga. Its museum opened in 1962, and the House of Fountains has mosaics of hunting scenes. The older sheet suggests splitting the day at the ruins: ~11 km from Rabaçal and ~21 km on to Coimbra. The regional board's guide describes Cernache as a short detour from the waymarked line, and the tables name it as a night stop, so ask.

### Day 10: Cernache to Coimbra (~11 km)

The shortest day of the road. Table distances differ by a few kilometres, because another table starts the day at Condeixa-a-Nova, ~16 km out. Either way you reach Coimbra early. Keep the afternoon.

The way climbs gently, past the remains of an old aqueduct, to the quarters above the city, and crosses the Mondego. Coimbra was the capital of Portugal from 1131 to 1255, Wikipedia says. Its university, which Wikipedia says dates from 1290 and has been in Coimbra for good since 1537, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, listed in 2013, and has an eighteenth-century Baroque library, the Joanina. On the south bank of the river is the monastery of Santa Clara-a-Nova, where Queen Isabel's Gothic tomb stands. The regional board says it shows the shell, the cross of Santiago and the pilgrim's staff, and the Xunta says she was buried with her staff.

The Old Cathedral, the Sé Velha, was begun in the twelfth century, and the church of Santa Cruz is the national pantheon, with the tombs of the first two kings of Portugal. Coimbra is a good place for a rest day, and the easiest on this road to defend.
