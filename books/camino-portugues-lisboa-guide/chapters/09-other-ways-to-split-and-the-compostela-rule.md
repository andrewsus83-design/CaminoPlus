# Other ways to split the days, other roads, and the Compostela rule

## Other ways to split the days

The 27 days in this book are one plan among several. Sources count 24 to 28 days for the whole road, and the older Confraternity sheet plans only 13 days from Lisbon to Porto, against this book's 16. Here are the commonest changes.

- **Days 1 and 2.** The first days end in different places in different plans. This book stops at Alpriate and Vila Franca de Xira. Another table stops at Santa Iría de Azoia and Vila Franca (~19 and ~22 km). The older sheet joins them in one day of ~37 km, and also offers a ~8 km first day to the Parque das Nações or a ~11 km one to Sacavém, for those who want a gentle start and can return to Lisbon.
- **Day 6.** Golegã to Tomar is ~31 km in two tables and ~22 km in the older sheet. Ask locally before you choose.
- **Days 8 to 10.** From Alvaiázere to Coimbra, this book takes three days: ~23, ~26 and ~11 km. The regional board names Rabaçal as the stage place, and the older sheet joins Alvaiázere to Rabaçal (~33 km) and Rabaçal to Coimbra (~32 km), or splits the second at Conímbriga (~11 and ~21 km). Another table goes by Condeixa-a-Nova.
- **Days 11 to 16.** The older sheet plans Coimbra to Porto in five days of ~26, ~31, ~19.5, ~29 and ~35 km. This book makes it six, by stopping at Grijó, which is also where the regional board's article puts the last stop before Porto.
- **Rest days.** Tomar, Coimbra and Porto are the natural places, and Tomar comes after the three longest days.

Whatever you choose, the arithmetic is the same: ~620 km on the catalog's figure, ~23 km a day over 27 days, less on the short days and more on the long.

## Other roads

**Fátima.** The Confraternity says you can detour to Fátima from Santarém. The older sheet adds that in the first days from Lisbon the arrows are shared with markers for the Fátima way. Blue arrows and the Fátima markers belong to that road, so follow the yellow ones to Santiago.

**The western road.** Wikipedia describes an older way from Lisbon by Caldas da Rainha and Alcobaça. The regional board's guide of 2023 lists a Caminho do Oeste as "in development", based on written accounts of the sixteenth and eighteenth centuries, by places such as Torres Vedras, Óbidos, Alcobaça and Batalha, and a Caminho Marítimo by the ports of the coast, also in development. For the ways not yet waymarked, the board says, you must bring the information and the GPS tracks you need. Neither is the road of this book.

**The Coastal Way and the Spiritual Variant** leave the road after Porto and Pontevedra. The chapter on the road beyond Porto says where.

## The Compostela rule

The Compostela is the certificate the Pilgrim's Office in Santiago gives to those who come to the tomb of the Apostle for religious and/or spiritual reasons and follow the routes of the Way of St James on foot, by bicycle or on horseback. The Office asks for at least the last hundred kilometres on foot or on horseback, or the last two hundred kilometres by bicycle, shown by the credencial, stamped along the route. On foot or on horseback, the Office asks for at least a hundred continuous kilometres on the same route to Santiago that it recognises. The final stage must include at least the last one leading into the Cathedral.

The Office awards the Compostela to those who walk for religious or spiritual reasons, and it describes the pilgrimage it has in mind as one made "with a Christian sentiment, even if it is only with an attitude of search". If that does not describe your reason for walking, ask the Pilgrim's Office what it can give you: it also offers a Certificate of Distance, which records the kilometres you walked from wherever you started.

The Way may be done in stages, if they follow in chronological and geographical order. A pilgrim who walks only the minimum distance must get the credencial stamped at the start and the end of each stage, with the date. A pilgrim who has already walked part of a recognised route outside Spain needs no less than seventy kilometres in Spain. The Cabildo, the cathedral's governing council, reserves the right to grant the Compostela in special and exceptional cases.

From Lisbon the road is ~620 km, far more than the last hundred kilometres the Office asks for. The Office decides, and rules change: ask it in any doubt.
