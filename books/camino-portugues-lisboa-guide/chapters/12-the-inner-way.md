# The inner Way

People walk to Santiago for many reasons: faith, sport, grief, curiosity, a rest from work, a promise made long ago. A road of four weeks has room for all of them, and it gives something that shorter roads give less of: time. Time to be bored, to be tired, to stop being either, and to find out what is left.

## A long road starts slowly

The first days are the least photogenic. You leave a capital on foot, through old quarters, then suburbs, a canal, industrial estates and the shoulder of a national road. There is nothing to be done about that except to walk it slowly. These places belong to the pilgrimage as much as the Roman road after Tomar, and the walker who learns to be present in them has learned something that the pretty days will not teach.

Then the plain opens, and the long days begin. A day of ~30 km on flat ground is a different thing from a climb. It has no summit and no reward at the top. It has a rhythm, a horizon that does not change, and your own thoughts, for hours. Some people find that hard and some find it a gift. You will know by the third day.

## Those who came before

Others have walked this road with their reasons. The Xunta de Galicia names two Portuguese pilgrims: Elizabeth of Portugal, the Holy Queen, who made the pilgrimage in the fourteenth century and was buried at Coimbra with a pilgrim's staff, and King Manuel I, who made the pilgrimage from Lisbon to Santiago in 1502 and, the Xunta says, ordered that a lamp should be kept lit in the church at Santiago night and day. A queen and a king, and one lamp. You can walk for greater or lesser reasons than theirs. The road does not ask.

## Questions for the walk

Take one a day, and do not hurry to answer.

- What did you hope the first week would give you, and what is it giving you instead?
- At what hour of the day do you feel most yourself: the early light, the long afternoon, the evening?
- What are you carrying that you could leave behind, in your pack and elsewhere?
- Who is walking with you in your head? Is there something you would like to say to them?
- If the road asks nothing of you but the next step, what is your next step?
- What would you like to bring home that you did not bring?

If you walk for a faith, the road is also a place to pray, in your own way and at your own pace. If you walk for no faith at all, nothing here asks you to pretend. A road that has taken queens and kings, and tired people from every country, has room for you.
