# Going home

You may come home with sore feet and an odd new sense of scale. After 27 days a four-hour bus journey may feel short, a kilometre may feel like nothing, and an ordinary week may feel too full. That is normal, and it passes slowly.

## Getting home

Plan the return before you set out, with the timetables in front of you, not on the last day. The Confraternity notes a regular bus between Santiago and Porto in about four hours, so a flight home from either city is possible. Lisbon, where you began, has its own airport, ~7 km north-east of the historic centre, according to Wikipedia. How to get from Santiago back to Lisbon is not something this book can tell you: ask the app, the station and the airport, and check the current times.

## Keeping the Way alive

Keep the credencial: it is now a record of a road. Write down what you remember in the first week at home, while it is still sharp: the plain in the early light, a town you reached tired, a word from someone you met. Keep walking, even for an hour a week, in the shoes you wore.

## Walking again

You may find that you want another road. The Camino to Finisterre and Muxía, ~90 km from Santiago, has its own book in this series, *To the End of the Earth*. If you walked the Central road from Porto, *The Coastal Way* describes the sea road from Porto to the same cathedral. A road you have not walked is a good reason to come back. *Bom caminho*, again.
