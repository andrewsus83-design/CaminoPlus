# With the app, and sources

## With the CaminoPlus app

**CaminoPlus** is the offline-first companion app for the Camino de Santiago, with maps that work without signal, stays and cafes, a pilgrim passport with stamps, a journal and friends. The paid plan is called **Pilgrim Plus**. The app is still in development. The route from Lisbon is on its list of routes, but its catalog holds no stages for it yet, so this book makes no claim about what the app holds for it today. Look in the app, and use it, not this book, for beds, cafes, closures and weather.

A reminder about the passport: the Pilgrim's Office asks for the stamped credencial. A passport in CaminoPlus, or in any app other than the Office's own digital credential, is a keepsake until the Office says otherwise.

## Sources and thanks

This book rests on public sources, and its fact ledger lists every fact against the page it came from. The chief ones are:

- **The Xunta de Galicia**, on its official Camino de Santiago portal: the page on the Portuguese Way, for its history, its two Portuguese pilgrims and the places of the road in Portugal, and the page on the last stage into Santiago.
- **Turismo Centro de Portugal**, the regional tourism board: its guide to the Caminhos de Santiago in Central Portugal (2023), its sub-regional guide to the Médio Tejo (2024) and its English article on the Central Portuguese St James Way, for the towns of the road from Tomar to Grijó, the certification of the road in July 2023, and its advice to walkers.
- **Via Lusitana**, the Portuguese pilgrims' association: its pages on its aims, the credencial, the hostels and the Compostela.
- **The Pilgrim's Reception Office of the Cathedral of Santiago**: the pages on the Compostela, the credencial and the Certificate of Distance.
- **The Confraternity of St James**: its page on the Camino Portugués, and a practical guide to the Lisbon to Porto stretch that presents itself as the Confraternity's own (a revised draft of December 2013, quoted in this book as the older sheet, and dated: its details may have changed).
- **Two published stage tables** for the road from Lisbon, from walking-holiday companies, named in the fact ledger. With the older sheet they give the stage towns and lengths, and they disagree, which is why the book says so.
- **Wikipedia**, for climate tables, history and statistics: the articles on the Camino Portugués, Lisbon, Lisbon Cathedral, Lisbon Airport, the Parque das Nações, the Tagus, Vila Franca de Xira, Azambuja, Santarém, Golegã, Tomar, the Convent of Christ, Conímbriga, Coimbra, Alvaiázere, Albergaria-a-Velha, Oliveira de Azeméis, the Dom Luís I Bridge, Porto, Santiago de Compostela and its cathedral, the Jacobean Holy Year, and the time zones of Portugal and Spain.
- **The CaminoPlus route catalog** and the app's own route note, for the route's start, end, ~620 km and difficulty rating.
- **A standard solar calculation**, for the hours of daylight.

Visit Portugal's page on the road could not be read, because the site asks every visitor to pass a bot check, and nothing in this book rests on it. Published figures differ between these sources, so every distance is marked ~. Where the sources say nothing, the book says nothing, and tells you to check. If you know a better figure, or a fact that has changed, tell CaminoPlus.

Thanks go to the people who keep the Way: Via Lusitana and the associations of friends of the Camino, who paint the arrows and keep the lists, the hosts who open their doors, and the people in the villages and towns who point the way and say *bom caminho* to a stranger.

## A short glossary

- **Albergue**: a pilgrims' hostel with shared rooms.
- **Albergaria**: an old Portuguese word for a hostel for travellers.
- **Bairrada**: the wine country around Mealhada, known for sparkling wines and roast suckling pig.
- **Bom caminho**: "good way", the Portuguese greeting between pilgrims; Spanish *Buen Camino*.
- **Botafumeiro**: the great censer of Santiago cathedral.
- **Cabildo**: the governing council of the cathedral.
- **Caminho**: "way" or "road", in Portuguese; *Camino* in Spanish.
- **Carimbo**: a stamp in the credencial; *sello* in Spanish.
- **Compostela**: the Latin certificate the Pilgrim's Office issues.
- **Credencial**: the pilgrim's passport.
- **Donativo**: a donation in place of a fixed price.
- **Hospitalero**: the host of a pilgrims' hostel.
- **Leitão**: roast suckling pig, a speciality of the Bairrada.
- **Peregrino**: a pilgrim.
- **Ribatejo**: the traditional region on the Tagus north-east of Lisbon.
- **Sé**: a cathedral, in Portuguese.
