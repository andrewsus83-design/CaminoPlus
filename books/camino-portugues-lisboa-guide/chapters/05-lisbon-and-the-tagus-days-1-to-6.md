# Lisbon and the Tagus: Days 1 to 6

## The character of these days

Six days from the cathedral of Lisbon to Tomar, ~156 km on the figures of one published table. The first two days leave a capital and its suburbs. The next four follow the Tagus upstream across the Ribatejo, the flat, farmed floodplain, and the last three of them are ~30 km or more. Wikipedia calls the Tagus the longest river in the Iberian Peninsula.

A note on the figures. The regional tourism board of Central Portugal begins its account at Tomar. Visit Portugal's page on the road could not be read, because it asks every visitor to pass a bot check. And the CaminoPlus catalog holds no stage data for this route. So these days rest on two published stage tables and on the Confraternity of St James, whose practical sheet for the stretch to Porto, called here the older sheet, dates from 2013. Where they disagree, this book says so. All three stage lists agree on the towns from Azambuja to Tomar. They disagree about where the first days end.

### Day 1: Lisbon to Alpriate (~22 km)

You start at the Sé. The arrows begin there, and in the old streets many are painted low on the wall. The way crosses the old quarters, runs beside the Tagus and passes through the Parque das Nações, the district laid out in 1998 for the World Exposition, according to Wikipedia. It leaves the city at Sacavém and ends at Alpriate, a farming hamlet, in ~22 km by the table.

Plans for this day differ. Another table ends it at Santa Iría de Azoia, ~19 km. The older Confraternity sheet offers several choices, from a ~8 km walk to the Parque das Nações to ~37 km in a day. Beyond Sacavém it calls the way an unlovely walk through industrial estates beside an old canal, but very well marked.

### Day 2: Alpriate to Vila Franca de Xira (~19 km)

A short, flat day, with the river close. The way passes Póvoa de Santa Iria and Alverca, and in places follows boardwalks beside the Tagus. The table warns about a stretch of road before Alhandra. From there a path along the river, paved, leads to Vila Franca de Xira.

Wikipedia says the municipality lies on both banks of the Tagus, ~32 km north-east of Lisbon, and is best known for its bull-running festivals, in July and October. The older sheet says the arrows pass the bullring. Another table gives this day as ~22 km, from a different start.

### Day 3: Vila Franca de Xira to Azambuja (~20 km)

All three stage lists agree: ~20 km. The table describes it as a transition day through the farming heart of the Ribatejo, on the floodplain beside the railway. The older sheet calls the way industrial parks, then a national road with a wide shoulder into Azambuja: unpleasant, it says, but not dangerous. Where there is no path, walk on the shoulder, facing the traffic.

Wikipedia places Azambuja in the district of Lisbon but in the old province of the Ribatejo. Sleep early: tomorrow is the first hard day.

### Day 4: Azambuja to Santarém (~33 km)

The first hard day. Tables give ~32 or ~33 km. The table calls it one of the longest and most demanding days of the whole road, and very exposed to the sun. You follow the Tagus, sometimes along the top of the flood levee, to Valada and Porto de Muge. Then comes a lonely stretch of farmland with no shade and no services, and the last climb up into Santarém. The older sheet agrees, noting about nine kilometres of farm track with no facilities and a levee path that can be overgrown, with a road below it.

Start at first light. Carry lunch and more water than you think you need. Santarém stands on a plateau above the river, ~65 km north-east of Lisbon, and Wikipedia calls it the "Capital of the Portuguese Gothic". Its church of Graça holds the Gothic tomb of Pedro de Meneses, and Pedro Álvares Cabral lies under a plain slab near the main chapel. The Confraternity says the road becomes more beautiful after Santarém.

### Day 5: Santarém to Golegã (~31 km)

You leave through the São Tiago gate in the old walls, the Gate of Santiago, and go down the hill. Tables give ~30.5 to ~30.7 km. The way is easy and flat, on farm tracks among vineyards and fields, by Vale de Figueira and Pombalinho, and through Azinhaga, where José Saramago was born in 1922, Wikipedia says. He won the Nobel Prize in 1998.

Watch the arrows. The older sheet reports that in 2013 the waymarking and the route around Azinhaga were disputed locally, with two ways in use. Follow the arrows you find, and ask. Golegã, the horse capital of Portugal, has an early sixteenth-century church with a Manueline portal, and holds its National Horse Fair every November.

### Day 6: Golegã to Tomar (~31 km)

The tables split here, and by a lot: two give ~31 km, the older sheet ~22 km. It is the largest disagreement on the Tagus road, and this book cannot settle it. Ask at Golegã, and plan for the longer figure.

The way leaves the Tagus after São Caetano and passes Vila Nova da Barquinha and Atalaia, whose parish church is a national monument. Near Vila Nova da Barquinha the regional board's account of the Central Way begins, and it says the road through its region was officially certified in July 2023. Tomar follows. The Templars built their castle and convent there in the twelfth century, and the Convent of Christ has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1983, with the round Charola of the twelfth century. The long Pegões aqueduct, ~6 km, was built between 1503 and 1614. If you want a day off the road, Tomar is a good place.
