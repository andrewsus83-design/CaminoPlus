# From Porto to Santiago: Days 17 to 27

## Where the roads join

At the Sé of Porto the Lisbon road becomes the Central route of the Camino Portugués, the road that *The Portuguese Way* describes day by day. No page this book could read says exactly where the two meet. The Confraternity's older sheet for the Lisbon to Porto stretch ends at the cathedral, and the published tables for the road from Porto begin there. So this book takes the Sé as the meeting point, and does not repeat the eleven days that follow.

They are Days 17 to 27 of this book: Porto to Vilarinho, then Barcelos, Ponte de Lima, Rubiães and Tui, where you cross the Minho into Spain; then O Porriño, Redondela, Pontevedra, Caldas de Reis, Padrón and Santiago. The catalog gives Porto to Santiago ~240 km, the table this book follows ~242 km, and the Confraternity ~252 km. Day 17 is Day 1 of that book, and its chapters on the days take over from here.

## What changes at Porto

- **More company.** Wikipedia reports that in 2025 most pilgrims who received the Compostela had started at Tui, Porto or Valença, all on this stretch.
- **Better waymarking and more support.** The Confraternity says yellow arrows are plentiful from the cathedral at Porto to the border, thanks to the Associação dos Amigos do Caminho Português de Santiago, and that in Galicia granite pillars add the distance to Santiago. It also says the road from Porto has more pilgrim infrastructure than the road before it.
- **A border and a clock.** The Minho is the border, and Galicia is an hour ahead of Portugal. Portuguese gives way to Galician and Spanish.

## Choices at Porto

A walker from Lisbon can also take the Coastal Way. The Confraternity says it leaves the Central route soon after Porto, follows the shore, and meets the Central route again at Redondela. It has its own book in this series, *The Coastal Way*. The Confraternity also describes a Litoral route that leaves Porto along the Douro and the sea, and the Spiritual Variant, which leaves the Central route at Pontevedra and goes by boat from Vilanova de Arousa up the estuary to Pontecesures. Each has its own waymarks, so know which road you are on, and ask locally about the boat before you plan around it.
