# When to go

This road starts on a dry, hot plain and ends in a green, wet corner of Spain. The regional tourism board of Central Portugal, in its guide to the Caminhos de Santiago, says that spring and autumn are the best seasons, because summer and winter can be very harsh. The Confraternity of St James describes northern Portugal and Galicia as a changeable maritime climate, with westerly winds and generous rain, and calls early autumn fairly dry and settled. Everything below is a pattern, not a forecast.

## Three climates in one walk

**The Tagus valley (Days 1 to 6).** This is the hot part. Wikipedia's tables (1991 to 2020) give a mean daytime high in Lisbon of ~28 °C in July and ~29 °C in August, and in Santarém of ~31 °C in July and ~32 °C in August. Rain is nearly absent: ~3 mm in July in Lisbon and ~5 mm in August. Lisbon's record is ~44 °C, set in August 2018, Wikipedia says. The days here are long, and part of the way runs along an open flood levee with little shade.

**Tomar to Porto (Days 7 to 16).** Milder. Coimbra's mean high is ~28 °C in July, and its record, in the same tables, ~41.6 °C. Rain grows as you go north: a year brings ~608 mm in Santarém and ~866 mm in Coimbra.

**Porto and Galicia (Days 17 to 27).** Cooler and much wetter. At Porto airport the mean high in July is ~24 °C and the year brings ~1,147 mm of rain. At Santiago the July high is ~25 °C and the year brings ~1,527 mm, with ~214 mm in October.

## The seasons

**Spring.** A rewarding time, the Confraternity says, cool, with fresh growth in the north. In the south the plain is not yet at its hottest: Lisbon's mean high in May is ~23 °C. Rain is still common further north: ~96 mm in April at Porto airport.

**Summer.** The board calls it harsh, and the numbers show why on the plain. The board advises making the way by day, preferably in the morning, and avoiding the hottest hours. A heatwave on a ~33 km day, such as Day 4, is a safety matter, not a discomfort. In the north, the Confraternity adds, summer can be hot and humid and the peak holiday months crowded. Wikipedia says most pilgrims on the Portuguese Way finish between May and September.

**Autumn.** September is still warm in Lisbon, with a mean high of ~27 °C, and rain returns slowly: ~39 mm in September, ~111 mm in October and ~134 mm in November. At Porto airport October brings ~149 mm and at Santiago ~214 mm.

**Winter.** Mild and damp, and short. The mean high in January is ~15 °C in Lisbon and ~12 °C at Santiago. Rain in January is ~104 mm in Lisbon and ~196 mm at Santiago. Wikipedia says frost at Santiago is common only from December to February, about 13 days a year, and snow rare, two or three days. The trouble in winter is rain and the light, not cold.

## Daylight

Computed for each city (a standard solar calculation), Lisbon has about nine and a half hours of daylight at the winter solstice and about fifteen at the summer solstice. Santiago has about nine and about fifteen. In December the sun rises at about eight in the morning in Lisbon and about nine in Santiago, and sets at about twenty past five and about six. In June it sets at about five past nine in Lisbon and about a quarter past ten in Santiago, by the local clocks.

The clocks change at the border. Wikipedia says continental Portugal keeps Western European Time, and peninsular Spain, Galicia included, Central European Time, an hour ahead. Your phone will jump, and your breakfast plan should jump with it.

## A month, in a line each

- **March and April:** cool, and wet in the north. Lisbon's mean high is ~19 °C in March.
- **May and June:** mild on the plain, with highs of ~23 and ~26 °C in Lisbon, and the days at their longest.
- **July and August:** hot and dry on the plain, and the hardest time for the first six days.
- **September:** warm, and the rain starts to come back.
- **October and November:** wet, with the days shortening. The National Horse Fair takes place in Golegã in November, Wikipedia says, so ask about beds if you pass then.
- **December to February:** the shortest days, with rain.

## Check before you go

Before any date, look for diversions, closures and, in hot dry weather, heat and fire warnings from the national weather services. The Tagus levee and some farm tracks can be overgrown or boggy at the wrong time of year, as the older Confraternity sheet says. Tomar's Festa dos Tabuleiros is held every four years, most recently in June and July 2023 according to Wikipedia, so the next may fall in 2027: if you plan to pass Tomar in early summer, ask.
