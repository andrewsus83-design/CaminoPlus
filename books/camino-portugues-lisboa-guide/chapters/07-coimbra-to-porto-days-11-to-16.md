# Coimbra to Porto: Days 11 to 16

## The character of these days

Six days, ~127 km on the table's figures, through the Bairrada and the district of Aveiro. It is a landscape of small towns that run into one another, vineyards, pine and eucalyptus woods, and industrial estates, with the suburbs of Porto at the end. The days are moderate: the table rates two of the six Moderate, four Easy, and none Hard. The regional board's account follows the road as far as Albergaria-a-Velha, and its guide says the way runs along Roman roads here, with strong medieval marks.

Take care with the figures. Tables differ here by ~3 to ~7 km a day, most on Day 12.

### Day 11: Coimbra to Mealhada (~25 km)

You cross the Mondego and walk on tracks and minor roads, over rolling ground, through pine and eucalyptus woods and the first vineyards of the Bairrada. The villages follow one another so closely that the table advises keeping an eye on the arrows. Tables give ~23, ~25 and ~26 km.

Wikipedia says the Bairrada is well known for its vineyards and its wine industry. The regional board mentions the sparkling wines and the roast suckling pig, *leitão*, of the Bairrada, and a Roman milestone, ~1.8 m high, in the entrance to the town hall of Mealhada, part of the Roman road that linked Lisbon and Braga.

### Day 12: Mealhada to Águeda (~24 km)

A transition day, the table says, with a gentle profile and plenty of settlements: villages, farmland and the odd industrial estate, with stretches of asphalt that deserve extra care. The sources disagree here more than on any other day: ~24, ~25 and ~31 km. The older Confraternity sheet has the longest, and does not say why. Ask locally, and do not plan the night on a single figure.

The regional board says the arrows point towards Avelãs de Caminho, in the municipality of Anadia, and then on to Águeda. It describes Águeda as known today for the urban art of its centre. The municipality also holds the Pateira de Fermentelos, which the board calls the largest natural lagoon in the Iberian Peninsula.

### Day 13: Águeda to Albergaria-a-Velha (~16 km)

A short day, the table says, good for resting your legs, and one of the most beautiful of this part. The way climbs gradually out of the Águeda valley and, from Pedaçães, follows dirt paths through thick vegetation. Tables give ~16 to ~19.5 km.

The regional board says you cross the old bridge over the Marnel, a tributary of the Vouga, at Lamas do Vouga before reaching Albergaria-a-Velha. The town's name comes from *albergaria*, an old word for a hostel, and the board's guide says it goes back to the one that Queen Teresa is said to have founded for pilgrims in 1117. Wikipedia records a donation of the lands of Albergaria in the same year.

### Day 14: Albergaria-a-Velha to São João da Madeira (~29 km)

The longest day of the week, and the tables agree: ~29 km. It runs on dirt tracks through pine and eucalyptus woods and small settlements, in a landscape that grows steadily more built up. You cross the old centres of Bemposta and Oliveira de Azeméis. Wikipedia calls Oliveira an industrial centre, ~35 km south of Porto. Two short days remain to Porto.

Start early, and ask in Albergaria in the morning where you can buy lunch.

### Day 15: São João da Madeira to Grijó (~19 km)

An urban day that tells you Porto is near: mostly asphalt, with an occasional short, steep climb. The way crosses the old centre of Arrifana and climbs towards Escapães. Another table gives ~16 km, so ask at São João. Grijó is the last town the regional board names before Porto.

### Day 16: Grijó to Porto (~15 km)

A short day through Vila Nova de Gaia, between streets and the odd park, with the best ending of the road: you cross the Douro on the Dom Luís I Bridge into Porto. Wikipedia says its upper deck was inaugurated in 1886, that it carries pedestrians and the metro, and that the lower deck carries road traffic, cyclists and walkers. The older sheet describes a high crossing, which leads to the upper town near the cathedral, and a low one, by the riverside. Decide which you want before the arrow sends you down.

You arrive at the Sé, the cathedral of Porto, where *The Portuguese Way* begins its first day. Here the road you have walked joins the road described in that book. Mark the moment: you have walked about 374 km, and ~242 km remain.
