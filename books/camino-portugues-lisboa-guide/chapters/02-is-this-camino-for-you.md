# Is this Camino for you?

## How hard is it?

The app's catalog rates this road 3 on a scale of 1 to 5. It rates the Camino Francés 3 as well, the Portuguese Way from Porto 2 and the Camino del Norte 4. The rating is a rough guide, and the catalog does not say what it weighs. Here the honest reading is that nothing on the road is steep for long, and almost everything about it is long.

The length is the first difficulty: about four weeks, not two. The second is the run of long days at the start. One published table has four stages in a row of ~30 km or more, from Azambuja to Alvaiázere, with stretches open to the sun. The third is the surface. The Confraternity's older sheet for the stretch to Porto (written for it in 2013, and dated) puts the walking at at least three quarters on asphalt, on industrial estates, quiet rural roads and residential streets, and notes reports of heavy lorry traffic on national roads. That was the author's estimate, and routes change, so treat it as a warning, not a measurement. Expect hard ground, and look after your feet.

The hills are modest. That table marks the first serious climbs after Tomar, in the limestone hills of the Serra de Sicó, and calls the stage into Santarém demanding because of its final climb. It rates two of its 16 stages Hard, Azambuja to Santarém and Tomar to Alvaiázere, both ~32 km or more.

## Who it suits

It suits a walker who has four weeks and wants a road with a shape: a capital, a river, a Templar town, a university city, Porto, and then the border and the arrival. It suits people who like their history in stone and in old roads. The regional board's guide follows stretches of Roman road, and Wikipedia says Conímbriga was a junction on the Roman road that linked Lisbon and Braga.

It suits people who can be alone. The road before Porto may have few other pilgrims, if the statistics are a guide, and it has less pilgrim infrastructure than the road from Porto on, as the Confraternity says. If you like a quiet road and are comfortable planning a night ahead, that may be what you want.

## Who might choose another

If you want the company of the albergues and a dense network of hostels, start in Porto. *The Portuguese Way* describes that road, and the Confraternity says the road from Porto is better supported.

If you have two or three weeks, not four, start at Coimbra, which is 17 stages from Santiago on the table this book follows, or at Porto, which is 11. The chapter on before you go gives the lengths.

If you want the sea, the Coastal Way leaves the Central route soon after Porto, and has its own book in this series, *The Coastal Way*. If you want mountains, the French Way, ~773 km in the catalog, crosses the Pyrenees and the mountains of León.

If you cannot walk ~30 km on a hard surface and then do it again two days later, start later. On this book's days the longest from Coimbra is ~29 km, and the older sheet warns that on the way to Porto some days of ~30 km or more are unavoidable, because buses are very infrequent.

## Questions worth asking yourself

- Can you walk ~20 km on a hard surface, two or three days in a row, with your pack, and add a few kilometres?
- Do you want the whole road from Lisbon, or the best of it, from Santarém, Tomar or Coimbra?
- How do you feel about walking alone for days, through suburbs and farmland, before the road fills up at Porto?
- What do you want from the walk: sport, solitude, history, a rest, prayer, a promise kept? Every answer is a good one, and the road does not ask.

If most of your answers are yes, and the others are "we shall see", you are ready for the next chapter.
