# Arriving

## The last streets

On the last day the road crosses the valley of the Sar and the sanctuary of A Escravitude, and reaches O Milladoiro, where, the Xunta says, pilgrims knelt when they first saw the cathedral. The Xunta then describes the way into the old quarter by the Porta Faxeira and the Rúa do Franco, and says the traditional entrance for pilgrims on the Portuguese Way is through the square of As Praterías. The last stage, from Padrón, is ~25 km on the Xunta's figures.

Wikipedia says the cathedral was begun in 1075, a Romanesque building with later Gothic and Baroque additions. The Baroque façade on the Praza do Obradoiro was completed by Fernando de Casas Novoa in 1740, and the Pórtico da Gloria, the carved doorway behind it, by Master Mateo, in 1188.

The Holy Door is opened only in a Jacobean Holy Year, a year in which 25 July, the feast of St James, falls on a Sunday. Wikipedia lists the next as 2027 and 2032. The botafumeiro, the great censer, is swung on certain religious holidays, and at every Pilgrims' Mass in a Holy Year. If you walk this road in 2027, you may arrive in one.

Take a moment in the square before you do anything else. Sit on the stones if you like. After 27 days, the arrival may be quieter or noisier than you expected. There is no right feeling.

## The Pilgrim's Office and the Compostela

The Pilgrim's Office in Santiago issues the Compostela. The rule it follows is in the chapter on other ways to split the days, and it is the Office's to apply. Bring your credencial, stamped along the way. To collect the Compostela you take a ticket with a QR code, which lets you follow your place in the queue, and the Office warns that in times of great influx it cannot guarantee same-day collection. Its website gives its address and its current hours, which are not in this book because they change.

If you walked for other reasons, or are unsure that yours count, the Office also offers a Certificate of Distance. It certifies the kilometres you travelled, whatever your starting point, and records the day and place you started, the day you arrived and the route. The Office says its reception service and the Compostela itself are free, and gives the price of the certificate, which is not in this book.

The Office meets a great many people in a day. Be patient, be warm, and if you have a doubt about your credencial, ask before you queue.

## The pilgrims' Mass

The cathedral holds a Pilgrims' Mass. Look at the cathedral's own website for the time, which changes. You do not need to be a believer to sit at the back and listen. It is a cathedral full of people who have walked.

## The next day

Try not to leave on the morning after you arrive. A day in Santiago lets the walk settle. Wash your clothes, sleep late, and look at the old town.

If you still have legs and time, some pilgrims walk on to Finisterre and Muxía, ~90 km farther, a route on the CaminoPlus app's list of routes that has its own book in this series, *To the End of the Earth*. If you do not, think about the way home: the next chapter helps.
