# Welcome

The long Portuguese Way starts at the cathedral of Lisbon and ends at the cathedral of Santiago de Compostela. It follows the Tagus upstream across the flat farmland of the Ribatejo, reaches the Templar town of Tomar, passes the university city of Coimbra and reaches Porto after sixteen days. At Porto it joins the road that *The Portuguese Way*, another book in this series, describes day by day. The app's catalog gives the whole road ~620 km. This book walks it in 27 days and spends its pages on the part that book leaves out: the 16 days from Lisbon to Porto.

**The promise of this book is simple.** By the last page you will know whether this long road is your Camino, how its 27 walking days fall, region by region, and what to check before you leave home.

## How to use it

If you are a month or more away, read the next four chapters in order: the Way in brief, whether it suits you, when to go, and what to do before you go. Then read the days, in three chapters from Lisbon to Porto, and the short chapter on the road beyond Porto. The chapter on other ways to split the days also gives the rule for the Compostela.

If you are on the road tonight, go straight to your day. Each one says what the walking is like, what is worth a pause, and where to take care.

If you are only dreaming, start with "Is this Camino for you?" and finish with "The inner Way". Four weeks is a long time to imagine, and it is fine to decide later.

## What this book is not

It is not a live map, a timetable or a booking service. It names no hostels, cafes or shops, and it gives no prices, opening hours or bed counts. They change from season to season, and a wrong one costs a tired person dearly. For those, use the CaminoPlus app and ask locally.

This book was written with AI assistance, from public sources, and its author has not walked the road. So it gives you sources instead of memories, and it says plainly when the sources disagree.

On this road they disagree more than most. No official page gives a stage list for the whole way, so the book follows published stage tables and says where they part company. Every distance carries a tilde, **~**, because it is approximate. Where something depends on the season (heat on the Tagus plain, a path after rain, a diversion), the book says *check before you go*, and means it.

## A few words you will meet

*Caminho* is the Portuguese word for "way" or "road", and *Camino* the Spanish; this road has both names. Pilgrims greet each other with *bom caminho* in Portuguese and *Buen Camino* in Spanish. A *peregrino* is a pilgrim, in both languages. An *albergue* is a pilgrims' hostel. The *credencial* is your pilgrim's passport, a folded card you carry and have stamped; a stamp is a *carimbo* in Portuguese and a *sello* in Spanish. The *Compostela* is the certificate the Pilgrim's Office in Santiago gives to those who meet its rules. The way is marked with yellow arrows, painted on walls, posts and paving stones.

They are explained again when they first matter, and used plainly after that.

Carry little, start early on the Tagus plain, and take your time over the hills after Tomar. *Bom caminho.*
