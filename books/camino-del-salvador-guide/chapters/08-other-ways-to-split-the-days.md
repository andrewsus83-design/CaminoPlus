# Other ways to split the days

## What the sources count

There is no single agreed list of stages. Asturias says the official route recognises five stages, the last three in Asturias, and publishes those three (~22.1, ~19.0 and ~18.4 km). It does not name the first two. Oviedo's cathedral and the association of the León municipalities both give the six stages of the first chapter, and this book follows them, but their figures differ a little. The association's front page says ~24 km for the second day, ~25 for the fourth, ~14 for the fifth and ~19 for the sixth, which Asturias counts as ~18. One page of the association gives ~14 km for the fourth day. That is the third day's figure and no other source agrees, so this book does not use it. The cathedral's six figures add up to ~120.3 km, though its text says ~122.

## Gentler plans

The association also suggests plans of five, seven, eight and nine days. They are rough: its own pages give ~24.8 km for Bendueños to Mieres in one plan and ~16.8 km in another. Its nine-day plan, the gentlest, runs:

1. León to Cabanillas: ~17.5 km.
2. Cabanillas to La Robla: ~9.5 km.
3. La Robla to Buiza: ~13.5 km.
4. Buiza to Poladura de la Tercia: ~9.7 km.
5. Poladura de la Tercia to Pajares: ~14.1 km.
6. Pajares to Bendueños: ~16.8 km.
7. Bendueños to Pola de Lena: ~10.7 km.
8. Pola de Lena to Mieres: ~14.1 km.
9. Mieres to Oviedo: ~19 km.

These add up to ~125 km, a little more than the six-day route. The plan cuts the second day at Buiza, so the climb to the Forcadas de San Antón falls on a short day, and it spreads the long fourth day over three shorter ones. In its shorter plans the association offers other ends for the mountain days: Poladura to Llanos de Somerón (~18.7 km) or to Bendueños instead of Poladura to Pajares. Its five-day plan joins Pola de Lena to Oviedo in one day, ~32 km by the figures above, a long day.

## Two lines between Buiza and Arbás

The line that the association, the cathedral and the Junta's leaflet draw climbs by the Forcadas de San Antón and Poladura de la Tercia. The Junta's guide gives ~9.5 km from Buiza to Poladura and ~9 km on to Arbás, ~18.5 km in all.

The same guide says the route it describes goes another way: down the valley by Villasimpliz, Villamanín, Villanueva de la Tercia and Busdongo to Arbás. Its table adds up to ~18.7 km for that line, about the same. Spanish Wikipedia calls the valley line the main route and the Poladura line an alternative. The Confraternity of St James mentions an unofficial, unsigned winter route, an escape from the mountains in bad weather, described in its own guidebook. Whether that is the valley line this book cannot say.

The association, for its part, says the road is never an alternative. These accounts pull in different directions, so before the second night ask your host which lines are waymarked and passable that week.

## Taking only part of it

Asturias's three stages cover ~59.5 km from the pass, so a walk of a few days starts there, and you would need to reach the pass yourself. In bad weather the association's advice is a taxi, a train or a bus past the mountain, and a return another time. The cathedral's page names no minimum distance for the Salvadorana, so ask before you plan on it.
