# With the app, and sources

## With the CaminoPlus app

**CaminoPlus** is the offline-first companion app for the Camino de Santiago, with maps that work without signal, stays and cafes, a pilgrim passport with stamps, a journal and friends. The paid plan is called **Pilgrim Plus**. The app's catalog lists the Camino del Salvador as a road from León to Oviedo of ~120 km. The app is still in development, so look in it for what is available on this road today, and use it, not this book, for beds, cafes, closures and weather.

## Sources and thanks

This book rests on public sources, and the fact ledger that goes with it lists every fact against the page it came from. The chief ones are:

- **Turismo Asturias** (turismoasturias.es), the tourism service of the Principado de Asturias: the page on the Way of El Salvador, its three Asturian stages, and its practical information for pilgrims. Its English pages are machine-translated, so this book read the Spanish originals for every figure and every warning.
- **The Junta de Castilla y León**: its guide to the historic roads, in the chapter on the Camino del Salvador (data updated in February 2011), its leaflet map of the road, and its tourism page on the route.
- **The association of the Camino de San Salvador** (caminodesansalvador.org), whose members are municipalities of León province: its six stages, its plans of five to nine days, and its practical advice.
- **The Cabildo of the cathedral of Oviedo** (catedraldeoviedo.com): its pages on the road, the Holy Chamber, the care of pilgrims, and visiting.
- **The Confraternity of St James** (csj.org.uk): its page on the Camino San Salvador.
- **The Pilgrim's Reception Office of the Cathedral of Santiago** (oficinadelperegrino.com): the rules of the Compostela, the credencial and the Certificate of Distance.
- **Your Europe**, the EU's information service, for the emergency number 112, and **AEMET**, the Spanish state weather agency, for warnings and forecasts.
- **Wikipedia**, for history, geography and transport: its articles on León, Oviedo, the Pajares Base Tunnel and Poladura de la Tercia, and, in Spanish, on the Puerto de Pajares, the Camino de El Salvador and the confiscations of church property. The climate figures for León and Oviedo are Wikipedia's, from AEMET.
- **A standard solar calculation**, for sunrise and sunset at León, and **the CaminoPlus route catalog and route guide**, for the road's ends, ~120 km and difficulty rating.

Published lengths differ, sometimes by several kilometres, and this book says so. The translations from Spanish are the author's own. If you know a better figure, or a fact that has changed, tell CaminoPlus.

Thanks go to the people who keep the Way: the associations and municipalities that mark it, the hosts who open the hostels, and the people in the villages who say *Buen Camino* to a stranger.

## A short glossary

- **Albergue**: a pilgrims' hostel with shared rooms.
- **Buen Camino**: "good Way", the greeting between pilgrims.
- **Cabildo**: the governing council of a cathedral.
- **Cámara Santa**: the Holy Chamber of Oviedo's cathedral, which keeps its relics.
- **Collada**: a gap in a ridge.
- **Colegiata**: a collegiate church, as at Arbás.
- **Credencial**: the pilgrim's passport.
- **Hospitalero**: the host of a pilgrims' hostel.
- **Puerto**: a mountain pass.
- **Salvadorana**: the certificate of Oviedo's cathedral for pilgrims who have walked the road.
- **Sello**: a stamp in the credencial.
- **Sudario**: the linen cloth that tradition says covered Christ's head, kept in the Cámara Santa.
