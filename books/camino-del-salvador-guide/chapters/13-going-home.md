# Going home

You walked out of a mountain and into a city, and then, quite soon, into a train or a plane. The change is sharper than on a longer road, because the road was short. Give yourself a day before you go back to the usual speed of things.

Keep your credencial, with its stamps in order, and the Salvadorana if you asked for it. Both are modest documents, and in a few years you may find they say more than a photograph. If you kept notes along the way, read them on the journey home. The questions of the inner Way are still good ones when you are no longer walking.

Some of you turned back on the third day, or took a taxi past the mountain, and wonder whether that counts. The association's answer is the one this book would give too: the Way will always be there, and you can do it another time. Walking well means knowing when the mountain says no.

If you want to keep walking, the road in front of you is already named. The Camino Primitivo begins at the cathedral where you stopped, ~320 km to Santiago, and has its own book in this series, *The Original Way*. The Camino Francés goes west from León, where you began, and has *The French Way*. Neither is a sequel that you owe. A short road can be a whole one.

*Buen Camino*, in whatever direction you walk next.
