# Welcome

The Camino del Salvador is a short road with a mountain in the middle of it. It leaves León, follows the Bernesga river north, crosses the Cantabrian range, and drops through the mining valleys of Asturias to Oviedo: ~120 km by the CaminoPlus catalog. It ends at the cathedral of San Salvador, the Saviour, and not at Santiago.

**The promise of this book is simple.** By the last page you will know why pilgrims have taken this detour since the Middle Ages, what the one hard day asks of you, how the days can be cut, what the certificate of Oviedo is, and what the Pilgrim's Office says about a walk that does not end in Santiago.

## How to use it

If you are a month or more away, read the next four chapters in order: the Way in brief, whether it suits you, when to go, and what to do before you go. Then read the six walking days, and the chapter on other ways to split them, which matters here because the sources count five days, six or nine. Read the safety chapter before you go, not after.

If you are on the road tonight, go to your stage. If you are only dreaming, read "Is this Camino for you?" and "The inner Way".

## What this book is not

It is not a live map, a timetable or a booking service. It names no hostels, cafes or shops, and gives no prices, opening hours or bed counts. They change, and in the mountains a wrong guess costs more than anywhere. For those, use the CaminoPlus app and ask locally.

This book was written with AI assistance, from public sources, and its author has not walked the road. So it gives you sources instead of memories, and it says plainly when the sources disagree.

It is also **not a Compostela book**. The Compostela is the certificate of the Pilgrim's Office for a walk that arrives at the cathedral of Santiago, and this walk arrives at Oviedo. The chapter on arriving says what the Office says, and what Oviedo's cathedral offers instead.

Every distance carries a tilde, **~**, because it is approximate and the published figures differ. Where something depends on the day (snow, fog, a swollen stream), the book says *check before you go*, and means it. On this road the line matters most on the third day.

## A few words you will meet

*Buen Camino*, "good Way", is what pilgrims and locals say as you pass. An *albergue* is a pilgrims' hostel. The *credencial* is your pilgrim's passport, a card stamped along the way; each stamp is a *sello*. The *Salvadorana* is the certificate that Oviedo's cathedral gives to those who have walked this road. A *puerto* is a mountain pass.

The official pages in Asturias give place names in Spanish and in Asturian (Oviedo and Uviéu, Pajares and Payares). This book uses the Spanish forms.

Carry what the mountain asks for, and take the short day slowly. *Buen Camino.*
