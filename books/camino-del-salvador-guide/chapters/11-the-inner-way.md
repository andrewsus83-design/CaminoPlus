# The inner Way

People walk this road for many reasons, and they do not have to be yours. Some walk out of faith. The road ends at the Holy Chamber of Oviedo, where, the cathedral says, the most venerated relic is the *sudarium*, kept there since the ninth century. Some walk to find their legs before the Camino Primitivo. Some walk for sport, or for a rest from work, or because a mountain is a good place to be quiet. Some walk out of grief, at a turning point, or from curiosity about what is on the other side of the range. All are welcome on the road, and none needs to defend the reason.

## A detour on purpose

*Quien va a Santiago y no al Salvador, visita al criado y olvida al Señor.* Whatever you make of the saying, it describes a choice most walkers do not make: a detour taken on purpose, away from the road everyone follows, to a goal that is not the famous one. The cathedral's words of welcome, in the book's translation, are for all who have come from the most diverse places to this goal of pilgrims.

For many, the cathedral's page says, Oviedo was the end of the journey, and for others the beginning of the road to Santiago. It may be both, depending on the day.

## Old gestures on the road

At Arbás the bells were rung in fog, the Junta's guide says, so that pilgrims should not lose their way. Someone cared whether you arrived.

At the Cruz de la Salve, the association says, pilgrims prayed a *Salve* for protection as they looked at the mountains they would cross the next day. You do not need to pray. You can stand and look, and decide what you are asking of the day.

At La Manxoya, Asturias says, it is told that the old pilgrims cried out *¡Mi Dios!* at the first sight of the cathedral tower.

## Walking with a question

You do not need a question, but if you have one it will keep you company. Here is one for each day, to take or leave.

1. **León to La Robla.** You are leaving a city and a road you know. What are you leaving with it, and what are you carrying anyway?
2. **La Robla to Poladura de la Tercia.** The mountain is ahead and you can see it coming. What are you afraid of, and what would you do if it happened?
3. **Poladura de la Tercia to Pajares.** This is the day on which you may have to wait or turn back. What do you need to hear from yourself before you decide?
4. **Pajares to Pola de Lena.** You have crossed. What can you put down now?
5. **Pola de Lena to Mieres.** The association calls this a rest. What is it like to have nowhere to hurry to?
6. **Mieres to Oviedo.** Tomorrow is the cathedral. Is it your goal or your starting line, and what will you do if you feel nothing at all?

## At the end

The cathedral offers pilgrims a prayer before the image of the Saviour. It asks him to be a companion to those on the roads of life, to explain the Scriptures to them, and to be known in the breaking of bread. Those words recall the walk to Emmaus in the Gospel of Luke, chapter 24. If you believe, they are yours to use. If you do not, they still describe what a long walk does: it makes you a companion to yourself, and lets you notice who else is walking beside you.
