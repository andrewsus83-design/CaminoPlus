# The crossing

## The character of this day

This is the short day and the hard one. The association calls it the *etapa reina*, the queen stage, and rates it at the top of its scale. It is the day on which the Camino del Salvador crosses the Cantabrian range, and the day on which a bad forecast should change your plan.

The heights below are as the sources give them, and the sources differ a little. Treat them as a guide to how far up you go, not as a surveyed list.

- León: ~837 m (Wikipedia and the association agree).
- La Robla: ~950 m (the association).
- Buiza: ~1,130 m (the association).
- Poladura de la Tercia: ~1,225 m (Wikipedia), ~1,230 m (the association).
- Forcadas de San Antón: ~1,450 m (the association), ~1,462 m (the Junta's leaflet).
- Alto de la Gobia: ~1,418 m (the Junta's leaflet).
- The high point of the whole Way: ~1,568 m (the association names it as the Collada del Coito; the Junta's leaflet marks ~1,567 m and ~1,568 m, for El Cuchillo and El Canto la Tusa, on the stretch between Poladura and Arbás).

The Puerto de Pajares itself, the road pass, is ~1,378 m, says Spanish Wikipedia. That figure is not your high point. The walking line crosses the ridge above the pass first, where the highest ground is ~190 m above the road, then comes down to Arbás and climbs again towards the pass.

### Day 3: Poladura de la Tercia to Pajares (~14 km)

The association's description is short and clear. You climb steep ramps to the Cruz del Salvador, then to the Collada del Coito, the highest point of the whole Way. Ahead lies the Sierra del Cuchillo, which you cross before you drop to the collegiate church at Arbás. From the top of the Pajares pass ~5 km of descent through forest remain. The whole route runs on tracks and mountain paths. There are several streams to cross, which the association says may carry a lot of water in the thaw, and mud in the forest of Valgrande.

In snow, the association says, the snowfields on this day can make the crossing very difficult, even impossible, and the safety chapter has the rest. If the forecast is good, start early and give the day its whole length. It is only ~14 km, but it is the association's pick for the day that takes most out of you.

Arbás del Puerto is the last place in Castilla y León on the road, and it is worth the stop. Asturias says the road would be incomplete without a visit to the village and its collegiate church of Santa María, from the twelfth and thirteenth centuries, a milestone on the way to San Salvador in Oviedo. The Junta's guide calls the church a fine example of rural Leonese Romanesque, and says that the stories of its founding are many while the documented history is thin. What it says about the pilgrims is better than any legend. In foggy weather the bells were rung so that pilgrims would not lose their way. And pilgrims were awaited at a place called the *canto de los pobres* and walked to the hospital that stood opposite the monastery and has since gone.

From Arbás a path climbs to the Alto de la Gobia and the Boquete de La Calera, says Asturias, then drops by the valley of La Casa Tibigracias to Samiguel del Río and the village of Payares, as Asturias writes it (Pajares in Spanish), the first village in Asturias. The Junta's guide notes that in Pajares an old Calle Real still runs through the village, and that an arch and a stone house recall the pilgrims' hospital. You have crossed. Tell someone you have arrived.
