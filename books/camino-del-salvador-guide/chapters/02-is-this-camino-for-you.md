# Is this Camino for you?

## Short, and not easy

The CaminoPlus catalog rates the Camino del Salvador 4 on a scale of 1 to 5. The Primitivo and the Norte get a 4 as well, the Francés a 3, and the Inglés a 2. The distance is small, so the rating is about the mountain.

The sources agree on that, in different tones. Asturias calls the road essentially a mountain route, and says the Asturian side is not to be underestimated either, even with the pass behind you. The association of the León municipalities says the road is not exceptionally difficult, but that parts of it cross high mountain, with big changes of height and a top of more than ~1,500 m, and that some preparation is wise. The Confraternity of St James says the mountain terrain brings poor weather, and services so scant that careful planning of where to stay and eat matters. It reports a stretch of ~50 km with no shops.

## Who it suits

- You have five or six days, or up to nine if you want to go gently.
- You are happy to walk all day on mountain tracks, to read a forecast, and to change the plan when the forecast says so.
- You want a walk with a destination that matters in itself: the cathedral of Oviedo and its Holy Chamber.
- You want to begin the Camino Primitivo at its source with a few days of preparation behind you.
- You can carry food and water for a day, and you have a plan B.

Whatever brings you, the road asks the same of you: to be ready for the weather.

## Who might choose another road

- If you need a shop, a cafe and a bed in every village. The mountain days will not give you that.
- If you can only walk in the snowy months. The association says plainly not to venture onto the mountain when it is snowed over.
- If you want a Compostela from this walk alone. It ends at Oviedo, and the chapter on arriving explains what that means.
- If your knees dislike steep descents. Asturias praises the scenery as you come down from the pass, and the association says the descents of the fourth day are what will test your knees.
- If you want company. No source read for this book gives a count of pilgrims on this road, so ask locally.

## How it compares with its neighbours

The Camino Francés leaves León to the west. The Salvador turns north into the range. The Camino Primitivo, which begins where this road ends, is ~320 km and mountainous too, and has its own book in this series, *The Original Way*. The Salvador is the short one: a few days, with a high pass and a goal.

## You do not have to walk all of it

Asturias publishes its part of the road as three stages, ~59.5 km in all, counted from the pass. The association says the hostels allow up to nine days, and that in bad weather the mountain stages can be left for another time, by taxi, train or bus. Oviedo's cathedral gives the Salvadorana to those who show a stamped credencial, with one stamp per stage enough, and its page names no minimum distance. If you plan to walk only part of the road and want the certificate, ask the cathedral first.
