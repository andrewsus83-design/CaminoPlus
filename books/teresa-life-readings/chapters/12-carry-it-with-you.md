# Carry it with you

You have come to the end of the ten days, or you are stopping early, or you plan to go back to a day you skipped. Any of these is fine. The book is not a course, and nobody is marking it. It was made to be a little company on a road across Spain, and company does not have to stay once the road is done.

## Ten small things

Ten small things fit in a pocket, and each is something you have already held once.

- The stones that would not stand, and the wish that stayed.
- The hour-glass: going in anyway, for ten minutes, on a day with no wish to.
- The picture in the corner, and being stopped by it.
- The four waters: the work that is yours, and being ready for rain.
- The hand held out, and a thank-you that is exact.
- The children, and asking plainly.
- The house that is not mine: doing your part and handing back the rest.
- The little bird with weak wings: a large hope, a small step, a rest.
- The well with no water: lowering the bucket, and looking after the body.
- The body and the kettle: we are not angels.

Pick one. Choose the one you can remember without looking. It will do more for you than all ten kept in a drawer.

## Going on

If you would like more of Teresa, she is easy to find. The edition we have used is on Project Gutenberg as ebook number 8120, and it is the same text every passage here was checked against. Our ten days come from chapters I, VII, VIII, IX, XI, XIII, XXII, XXXI and XXXVI. The edition also prints, after the *Life*, a set of shorter papers by Teresa called the Relations, and before it a long introduction by its editor, which you can skip.

A warning is fair. Much of the book is about visions, voices and raptures that we have left aside, and a good deal of it is about her sins, which she describes in a way that may seem extreme. Read on if you want to, and read what helps. Pass quietly over what does not. Teresa herself, in this book, was careful about the strange parts, and asked wise people to judge them.

Some of it will not have worked, and that is fair too. A day that left you cold on the road may speak to you at your own table in March. A passage that asked for more belief than you had was not a test you failed. If something hardly touched you, put it down. Nothing has gone wrong.

## A last word

Teresa does not end her book on a summit. She ends it with a request that her reader be careful with it, with the news that she had little time to write it, and with a hope that she and he may one day see more clearly the mercies they have been shown. It is an ordinary ending, a letter coming to a close, and it is more believable for that.

If you keep only one thing, keep the sentence from her last chapter that tells you what you may expect: that in this life we do not continue in the same state. Some days fervent, some not, some quiet, some troubled. The advice that goes with it is short: hope, and do not be afraid.

Walk again when you can. Or tomorrow, ten minutes after supper, to the end of the street and back, with one bucket and no particular rain in mind.

*Buen Camino.*
