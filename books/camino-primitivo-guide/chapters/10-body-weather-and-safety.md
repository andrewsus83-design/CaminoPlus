# Body, weather and safety

This chapter is plain on purpose, and it asks for more care than usual, because the Primitivo has real mountain days and the weather can turn quickly. Nothing here is a reason to stay at home. It is what a careful walker does.

## Feet and legs

Your feet decide how the walk goes. Stop at the first hot spot and dress it, before it becomes a blister. Change your socks at lunch if they are damp. Rest for a few minutes each hour, and eat and drink before you are hungry or thirsty.

The descents are as demanding as the climbs. The official pages describe a drop of ~300 m in ~3 km on the day to Pola de Allande and a long descent of ~8 km, in places very steep, on the day to Grandas de Salime. Shorten your stride and use poles if you have them. If you feel a sharp or growing pain in a knee, a shin or the top of a foot, stop. Rest it for a day, or take a shorter stage. A day lost to rest is better than a week lost to an injury.

## Mountain weather

The Confraternity of St James says the weather here can be harsh, even in summer, damp and cold, and can change very quickly. The Xunta de Galicia says the pass into Galicia is covered in snow in the harsh seasons. Take both seriously.

- **Look at the forecast the evening before and again in the morning.** AEMET, the Spanish state weather agency, publishes forecasts. Look at the high ground, not only the town you are leaving.
- **Start early on mountain days,** so that a slow climb or a wrong turn does not leave you on a ridge at dusk.
- **Wet clothes cool you quickly,** even in mild weather. Carry a waterproof, a warm layer, a hat and gloves, and keep one dry layer in a bag that cannot get wet. If you start to shiver, stop and get warm before you go on.
- **In mist, stay on the marked way.** The Confraternity says the waymarking is good overall, but if you cannot see the next marker, stop, and do not guess. Go back to the last one you saw. If you have a saved map, use it.
- **If a day looks wrong, change the plan.** Take the lower road, stop early, or wait a day. The chapter on the two roads gives a rule for the Hospitales.

Paths, steps and old stones are slippery in the wet. After heavy rain, ask locally about flooded sections.

## Heat

Wikipedia's climate tables show averages of ~24 to ~25 °C in the warmest months, and past extremes of ~41 °C in both Oviedo and Lugo. On a hot day, start early, wear a hat, put on sun cream, and carry more water than you think you need. Rest in the shade through the hottest hours. A headache, dizziness or nausea in the heat are signs to stop, get into the shade, and drink. If they do not pass, ask for help. In dry summers, ask locally about fires and closed paths.

## Roads, paths and dogs

Much of the Primitivo is on tracks and paths, and much of it is not. The official pages describe the first day as mostly on tarmac roads. On the day to Grandas de Salime, the climb of more than ~5 km runs mostly along the shoulder of the AS-14 road. Between Paradavella and O Cádavo the path runs near the C-630, and on the way to Arca it crosses the N-547 several times. The Xunta asks you to pay special attention to vehicles.

Wherever you must walk on a road, face the oncoming traffic, keep well to the edge, and wear something bright. Never walk on a road in the dark or in thick mist.

Dogs on farms may bark as you pass. Walk on calmly and do not stare or run.

## Staying safe on your own

Tell someone where you plan to sleep each night, and message them when you arrive. Keep your phone charged, carry your map saved on it, and know the name of the last village you passed. Mountain signal can fail, so do not rely on it.

## When to stop

Stop, and get help, for chest pain, a fever, severe dizziness, a fall with a blow to the head, a swollen joint, or anything that frightens you. Stop too if you are cold and cannot get warm, if you are lost in mist, or if you have run out of water. A doctor is the right person for a worry, and a plan can always change. The Way will still be there.

## Emergencies

**112** is the European emergency number and it works in Spain. Say where you are as exactly as you can: the village, the stage, the last landmark you passed, and whether you are on the high road or the low road.

## Check before you go

Before each mountain day, check the forecast, any local notice about the path, and the state of the high ground: snow, mist, wind. Closures and diversions change, so look at the official pages and the app, and ask where you slept.
