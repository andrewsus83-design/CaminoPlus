# Going home

The road ends in Santiago, and then you have to come back to a life with a ceiling and a kettle. You may find the first days strange: your legs want to walk, your pack feels missing, and the quiet of the mountains is gone. That is allowed. Give yourself a week before you decide what the walk meant.

## The first days

- **Look after your feet and knees.** Climbs and descents leave their mark. If something still hurts after a week, see a doctor.
- **Wash and dry your gear** while the memory of what failed is fresh, and note what you carried and never used.
- **Write something down** while it is still sharp. Three lines from each region will do: the day you were tired, the day you were glad, the moment you did not expect.
- **Keep your credencial.** It has its own small history, now complete.

## Keeping the Way alive

You do not need a pack to keep walking. A weekly walk, however short, keeps the habit. Choose a day to walk without headphones, as you did on the road. Say *Buen Camino* to a stranger. Send a word to someone who helped you on the way.

## Walking again

You have walked the Original Way, and the last ~53 km of the Camino Francés with it. If you want more, the Camino Francés (~773 km) has its own book in this series, and so does the Camino del Norte (~820 km), which, Wikipedia says, also joins the Francés at Arzúa. The Camino Inglés (~120 km) is short, and has a book too. A road does not have to be longer or harder to be worth walking. It only has to be yours.

*Buen Camino*, and good to have you home.
