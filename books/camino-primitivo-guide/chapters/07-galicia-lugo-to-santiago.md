# Galicia: Lugo to Santiago

## The character of these days

The last five days change character half-way. The first two cross the country between Lugo and Melide: Roman roads, Romanesque churches, small villages, and the Careón hills on the border of the provinces of Lugo and A Coruña. At Melide the Primitivo meets the Camino Francés, and the last three days are on the shared road, ~53 km to the cathedral by the Xunta's count.

The distances here are the Xunta de Galicia's stage lengths, from Lugo on. The Xunta rates them low, medium, low, low and medium. The longest, to Melide, is ~28 km.

### Day 10: Lugo to San Romao da Retorta (~19 km)

You leave the city through the Roman walls, either by the Porta Miñá (also called the Porta do Carme) or by the Porta de Santiago, in front of the cathedral's neoclassical façade. The Xunta says the path follows the track of a hidden Roman road to the River Miño, which you cross by a bridge that is also of Roman origin. After that you leave the river behind, and from San Vicente do Burgo the Xunta promises striking views of the city receding.

The Xunta calls a detour to the temple of Santalla de Bóveda well worth taking. It is a late Roman structure of the fourth century, listed as a national monument in 1931. The Xunta says the Roman road from Braga to Lugo passed beside it. Back on the Way you reach Bacurín, with its Romanesque church of San Miguel, and the village of O Francés, also known as Hospital. San Vicente do Burgo, ~10 km from Lugo, once had a pilgrims' hospital. The day ends in San Romao da Retorta, in the municipality of Guntín, where a Roman milestone stands outside the Romanesque church. The Xunta calls it evidence that the Primitivo follows the path of an ancient road.

### Day 11: San Romao da Retorta to Melide (~28 km)

This is the longest Galician day, and the Xunta rates it medium, with an estimate of about seven hours. Carry lunch, and an early start is wise.

The first ~12 km take you through small villages (O Burgo, Vilacapide, Pacio, Mosteiro and Ferreira) past the Romanesque church of Santa Cruz da Retorta with its stone cross, and across the medieval Ferreira bridge. By the Spanish Wikipedia's kilometre table the bridge is ~10 km beyond San Romao, so it is one place to break the day. Near the end of that stretch is Augas Santas, whose curative springs and ninth-century monastery are described by the Xunta, and the Romanesque church of San Salvador de Merlán.

The next ~8.5 km climb into the Careón mountains, which span the border of Lugo and A Coruña. The Xunta calls the area one of great ecological value, and gives the highest peak, O Careón, as ~798 m. You then cross into the municipality of Toques and pass Vilouriz. Just off the Way is the eleventh-century Romanesque church of Santo Antoniño de Toques, which the Xunta says reuses capitals and pieces from the Visigothic period.

At Melide the road changes. The Xunta says the Primitive Route converges there with the French Route, at the historic town, and continues to Santiago Cathedral ~53 km away. Wikipedia gives the town an elevation of ~456 m.

### Day 12: Melide to Arzúa (~15 km)

The first day on the shared road is a short one. The Xunta lists the same stage, with the same length, for both the Primitive and the French Ways, and rates it low. It describes the surface as earth and stone with small asphalt sections between villages, and gradual climbs and descents alternating with flat stretches.

At Boente you will see a fountain with four pipes and a Calvary, across the main road from the church. At Ribadiso you cross the River Iso on a bridge. Arzúa, the Xunta says, is the land of cheese par excellence, and Wikipedia adds that Arzúa-Ulloa cheese has Protected Designation of Origin status. Wikipedia also says that the Camino del Norte meets the Francés at Arzúa. That road has its own book in this series.

### Day 13: Arzúa to Arca (~18 km)

Meadows, oaks and eucalyptus surround hamlets with Jacobean names: A Calzada, A Calle, Ferreiros, A Salceda, A Brea, Santa Irene. The land is practically flat. The one thing to watch is the road. The Xunta warns that the path crosses the N-547 highway several times, and asks you to pay special attention to the vehicles.

You arrive at Arca, capital of the municipality of O Pino, the last municipality before Santiago. O Pedrouzo is the main village of the parish of Arca.

### Day 14: Arca to Santiago (~20 km)

The last day is rated medium by the Xunta, with an estimate of a little over five hours. You climb from Santo Antón into the municipality of Santiago, and pass O Amenal, Cimadevila and San Paio. At Lavacolla, near the airport, the Xunta says pilgrims once washed in the stream before arriving.

Monte do Gozo, ~380 m high, is where pilgrims first saw the distant cathedral. The Xunta says that by custom the first to reach the top was proclaimed king of the pilgrimage. A ~5 km descent remains. You enter the city by the San Lázaro neighbourhood, the Rúa de San Pedro and the Porta do Camiño, then go through pedestrian streets and squares (Casas Reais, Plaza de Cervantes, Acibechería) to the basilica. In Holy Years the Xunta says the alternative access is the Holy Door at A Quintana.
