# When to go

## Patterns, not forecasts

This chapter gives patterns, not forecasts, and the patterns come with a warning: the figures below are for three towns, and the Primitivo spends several days well above any of them. Oviedo's weather station sits at ~336 m and Lugo's at ~445 m. The pass into Galicia is ~1,030 m, according to the Xunta de Galicia, and A Fonsagrada, the first Galician town, stands at ~965 m, according to Wikipedia. Expect it to be colder up there. The Confraternity of St James says the weather in these hills can be harsh even in summer, damp and cold, and can change very quickly.

## The rain

All three places have an oceanic climate: mild, green and wet. Wikipedia's climate tables for the period 1991 to 2020 give roughly this:

- **Oviedo:** ~1,030 mm of rain a year. The driest month is July (~44 mm) and the wettest November (~134 mm).
- **Lugo:** ~1,140 mm a year. July is the driest (~33 mm) and November the wettest (~154 mm).
- **Santiago:** ~1,500 mm a year, and the article's own text says about ~1,800. July is the driest (~31 mm) and October the wettest (~214 mm).

So the shape is the same everywhere. July is the driest month, with June and August close behind, and the heavy rain runs from October to January. None of these months is guaranteed dry. Carry rain gear in every season.

## Warm months

In Oviedo the average afternoon high is ~24 °C in August, in Lugo ~25 °C, and in Santiago ~25 °C in July and August. That is an average. The same tables record extremes of ~41 °C at both Oviedo and Lugo in the past, and the safety chapter has a section on heat. Wikipedia reports that most pilgrims finish the Primitivo in August, and the Confraternity calls the route very popular in July and August. In a busy month, work out where you will sleep before you set out each day, and use the app and local information to see what is open.

## Spring and autumn

Spring and autumn sit between the extremes, with the added risk of the wettest months. Oviedo's average afternoon high is ~19 °C in September and ~14 °C in November, and its rain roughly triples between July and November. Lugo's April high is ~17 °C, and its record lows go below freezing from October to May. Take layers, a proper waterproof, and a day you can lose to the weather.

## Winter

Winter is the hardest case, and the high ground is the reason. The Xunta says the pass is covered in snow in the harsh seasons. In Oviedo's table the average January high is ~12 °C and the record low about −6 °C, and Lugo's average January high is ~11 °C with a low of ~2 °C and a record of about −12 °C. Oviedo's station counts about four snowy days a year, at ~336 m. The pass is ~700 m higher.

The days are short as well. For Oviedo and Lugo, a standard solar calculation gives ~9 hours of daylight at the December solstice, with sunrise near nine in the morning, and ~15 hours at the June solstice, with sunset after ten at night. Do not plan to walk in the dark.

Before you set out in winter, ask locally and read current notices. The Confraternity's advice, if you are contemplating the high-level variant, is to restrict yourself to the summer months. The Asturias tourism pages give no closing date for that variant and no weather rule for it. This book will not guess one. *Check before you go.*

## A Holy Year

2027 is a Jacobean Holy Year, a year in which 25 July falls on a Sunday; the next is 2032. The cathedral's Holy Door is opened only in Holy Years, and the Pilgrim's Office reports that 272,703 pilgrims received the Compostela in the Holy Year of 2010. If you plan to walk in 2027, arrange your beds earlier than usual.

## Choosing a month

The driest weather and the longest evenings are in June to September, and so are the crowds of July and August. The shoulder months are quieter and wetter, and ask for extra layers and flexible days. Winter asks for mountain skills, not just boots. Whichever month you choose, look at the forecast of the Spanish weather agency, AEMET, for the high ground before every mountain day.
