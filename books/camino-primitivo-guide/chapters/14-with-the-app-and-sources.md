# With the app, and sources

## With the CaminoPlus app

**CaminoPlus** is the offline-first companion app for the Camino de Santiago, with maps that work without signal, stays and cafes, a pilgrim passport with stamps, a journal and friends. The paid plan is called **Pilgrim Plus**. The app is still in development. The Camino Primitivo is on its list of routes, but this book makes no claim about what the app holds for it today, so look in the app, and use it, not this book, for beds, cafes, closures and weather.

A reminder about the passport: the Pilgrim's Office asks for the stamped credencial. A passport in any app is a keepsake until the Office says otherwise.

## Sources and thanks

This book rests on public sources, and its fact ledger lists every fact against the page it came from. The chief ones are:

- **The Principado de Asturias tourism pages** (turismoasturias.es): the pages on the Camino Primitivo and its seven stages through Asturias, the practical information for pilgrims, the page on gastronomy, and the official Camino brochure.
- **The Xunta de Galicia**, on its official Camino de Santiago portal (caminodesantiago.gal): the Primitive Way page and its Galician stages, with their lengths, sections and descriptions, and the Melide to Arzúa stage of the French Way.
- **The Pilgrim's Reception Office of the Cathedral of Santiago** (oficinadelperegrino.com): the rules of the Compostela, the credencial and the history of the pilgrimage.
- **The Confraternity of St James** (csj.org.uk): its page on the Camino Primitivo.
- **Wikipedia**, for history, geography, climate tables and the Pilgrim's Office statistics for 2024: the articles on the Camino Primitivo, Oviedo, Lugo, A Fonsagrada, Melide, Arzúa, Santiago de Compostela and its cathedral, Santiago airport, the Jacobean Holy Year, Galicia, Asturias, and three dishes; and the Spanish Wikipedia's article on the Ruta Jacobea Primitiva, for its kilometre table and its account of the Hospitales variant.
- **A standard solar calculation**, for the hours of daylight at Oviedo and Lugo.
- **The CaminoPlus route catalog**, for the route's start, end, ~320 km and difficulty rating.

Published figures differ between these sources, so every distance in this book is marked ~. Where the sources say nothing, for example on a closing date for the high variant, the book says nothing and tells you to check. If you know a better figure, or a fact that has changed, tell CaminoPlus.

Thanks go to the people who keep the Way: the associations of friends of the Camino, the hosts who open the hostels, and the people in the villages and towns who point the way and say *Buen Camino* to a stranger.

## A short glossary

- **Albergue**: a pilgrims' hostel with shared rooms.
- **Botafumeiro**: the great censer of Santiago cathedral.
- **Buen Camino**: "good Way", the greeting between pilgrims.
- **Cabildo**: the governing council of the cathedral.
- **Compostela**: the Latin certificate the Pilgrim's Office issues.
- **Credencial**: the pilgrim's passport.
- **Hórreo**: a raised granary, a mark of the Asturian countryside.
- **Hospitales**: the high variant of the Camino Primitivo, named for its ruined pilgrims' hospitals.
- **Hospitalero**: the host of a pilgrims' hostel.
- **Menú del peregrino**: a set meal for pilgrims.
- **Palloza**: a primitive rural dwelling of the mountains.
- **Sello**: a stamp in the credencial.
