# The border and the high Lugo hills

## The character of these days

Three long days take you over the border and across the high ground of the province of Lugo. You climb to a pass of ~1,030 m, walk through hill country of small hamlets and medieval hospital sites, and descend at last to the Roman walls of Lugo. The days are long (~25 km, ~25 km, ~30 km), the country is open, and the weather on the first of them is the weather of the mountains.

From here the figures come from the Xunta de Galicia's route pages, which describe only the Galician part of the Way, from the pass to Santiago, in eight stages with their own stage towns. This book's days add up the Xunta's sections to reach the usual stage towns, so their lengths are sums, good to a kilometre or two.

### Day 7: Grandas de Salime to A Fonsagrada (~25 km)

The day begins with a climb that the official Asturias pages call continuous, straight and not very steep, except for the last push to the top. It is ~13 km, rising ~600 m. Most of it is on dirt and gravel tracks, through the villages of Cereixeira, Castro, Xestoselo and Penafonte, and between them the path often runs through tunnels of vegetation. At Castro, the large archaeological site of Chao Samartín preserves pre-Roman dwellings and the ruins of a Roman mansion; the pages say it was declared an Asset of Cultural Interest in 2014 and has a modern interpretation centre.

The pass is the border. Asturias calls it Puerto del Acebo, and the Xunta calls it Alto do Acevo and gives its height as ~1,030 m. The Xunta says it is a lovely spot, covered in snow in the harsh seasons and lush in the milder months. In winter, *check before you go*: ask locally about snow and mist before you start this climb.

On the Galician side the first goal is Fonfría and its freshwater spring. Between Fonfría and Paradanova the Xunta places a major pilgrims' hospital, on land held by San Xoán de Paradanova, which it says was still working at the start of the twentieth century. At Paradanova the Xunta marks two options: one runs through the centre of A Fonsagrada, past a holy fountain linked by tradition to a miracle attributed to St James, and the other goes by A Proba de Burón. The Xunta measures the first at ~167 km for the whole Galician Way and the second at ~168 km. Both meet again at Montouto. The Spanish Wikipedia says the Burón route was the main one until the eighteenth century. A Fonsagrada, according to Wikipedia, stands at ~965 m.

### Day 8: A Fonsagrada to O Cádavo (~25 km)

The Xunta rates this stretch low to medium-low. It stays high, from ~965 m at A Fonsagrada to ~710 m at O Cádavo. From A Fonsagrada you pass San Xoán do Padrón and reach Hospital de Montouto, where the two options come together. A half-ruined hospital stands there, which Wikipedia calls the New Royal Hospital of Santiago de Montouto, with roots in an older royal hospital founded, it says, by Pedro I, "the Cruel". The Xunta's page shows a dolmen at the site, and the Confraternity of St James lists the old hospital and the dolmen among the things worth seeing.

Then Paradavella, which the Xunta says is noted for its *pallozas*, the primitive rural dwellings. Beyond it the path alternates between dense forest and open land near the C-630 road, so take care near the traffic. It passes A Degolada, where you enter the municipality of Baleira, then climbs to the Alto de A Vaqueriza at ~840 m. Near A Fontaneira, the Xunta records a tradition that the Campo da Matanza was the site of a clash between Alfonso II and a Muslim army. At O Cádavo, ~710 m up, you are in the capital of Baleira, which the Xunta calls the second most important town after A Fonsagrada on the Primitive Route in Galicia.

### Day 9: O Cádavo to Lugo (~30 km)

By this book's figures this is the longest day of the whole walk. The Xunta's own stage, from Paradavella, ends at Castroverde, ~7.5 km beyond O Cádavo, and its next stage runs from Castroverde to Lugo, ~22 km. Walking both in one go makes ~30 km. If it is too much, Castroverde gives you a place to stop, and the walk to Lugo becomes a ~22 km day the morning after.

From O Cádavo you pass Vilalle. Close by, on a complementary route, is Vilabade, where the church of Santa María, a National Monument built in the middle of the fifteenth century as a Franciscan convent church, holds a Baroque altarpiece of 1759. Castroverde, at ~540 m, has an old castle keep high above the town, a reminder, says the Xunta, of the houses of Lemos and Altamira.

Beyond it the path reaches Soutomerille, an abandoned village with a pre-Romanesque church of San Salvador and a stone lattice window from the time of Alfonso III, in the ninth or tenth century. At Gondar you cross the River Romeán and enter the municipality of Lugo, then pass Carballido, and Lugo comes into view.

Lugo is worth a day. Wikipedia says its Roman walls, a circuit of ~2,117 m with 71 towers and ten gates, are from the third century, are the only completely intact Roman walls in the world, and have been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2000. The walk along the top is continuous. The cathedral dates from around 1129, and the Xunta says it holds a papal privilege to display the Holy Sacrament around the clock. After ~30 km a rest day here is not an indulgence.
