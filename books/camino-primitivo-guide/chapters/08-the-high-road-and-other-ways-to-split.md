# The high road, and other ways to split the days

## Two roads over the same mountains

At Tineo the Primitivo offers a choice. The official itinerary goes by Pola de Allande, over the Alto del Palo, to Berducedo. The other way is the Hospitales variant, a high route through the Fonfaraón range, which rejoins at Montefurado, ~8 km before Berducedo. This is the one big decision of the Camino. Read it before you reach Tineo.

### What the official pages say

The Asturias tourism pages are the source here. They say the Hospitales section takes its name from the ruins of the pilgrims' establishments preserved there, and that it is believed to have been part of the original route. They also say its extreme hardship, with very steep slopes crossing completely unpopulated land, led pilgrims gradually to choose Pola de Allande instead. They say those who follow it will enjoy magnificent surroundings, "although it is essential to be in good physical shape". They advise sleeping at Campiello or Bourres the night before.

Their numbers are these. The Asturian part is ~146 km by the official route and ~142 km by Hospitales, so the high road is ~4 to ~5 km shorter, depending on whether you read the web page or the brochure. The variant is described in two parts: Salas to Bourres, ~32 km on one page and ~36 km in the official brochure, and Bourres to Berducedo, ~24 km. Taking away the ~19 km from Salas to Tineo, that leaves ~13 to ~16 km on the first day and ~24 km on the second. In the hills, the pages count a climb of more than ~500 m between La Mortera and the top, beside the ruins of the Fonfaraón hospital. They add that the path is a very logical natural line which avoids steep slopes except in a few stretches, with long almost level sections. Along it are the ruins of the old pilgrim hospitals of Paradiella, Valparaíso, Fonfaraón and La Freita, and the great Roman mine cut of La Freita.

The lower road has its own ~600 m climb to the Alto del Palo, in ~8 km, on the day from Pola de Allande. It is not the easy option. It is the sheltered one.

### What other sources add

The Spanish Wikipedia calls the Hospitales variant the oldest and most natural line, and says it saves some kilometres and avoids descending to Pola de Allande and climbing the hard Puerto del Palo. In exchange, it says, it crosses the beautiful but inhospitable Sierra de Fonfaraón, without villages and largely above ~1,100 m. It adds that the route is dangerous in winter or in bad weather, which is why the main route went down to the valley.

The Confraternity of St James warns that the weather in these hills can change very quickly and that you can be shrouded in damp mist for a whole day. Its advice is to have hill-walking experience, and to restrict yourself to the summer months if you are contemplating the high-level option. Its page names the Hospitales route as the alternative to Pola de Allande.

### What this book does not know

The official Asturias pages used for this book give no closing date and no weather rule for the high road. This book does not guess one. If there is a closure, a notice or a warning, it will be local and current. *Check before you go.*

### A rule for the day

Decide on the day, at Tineo or on the way to Campiello and Bourres, not weeks ahead.

- Look at the forecast for the high ground, and at the cloud on the hills.
- Choose the lower road if the forecast promises rain, mist, snow or strong wind, if the days before were stormy, if you are tired or unwell, or if you have never walked on mountains.
- Choose the lower road if you start late. There is no village on the high road to stop in.
- If you take the high road, carry food and water for the whole day, warm layers, a saved map, and tell someone your plan. Turn back if the mist closes in.

## Other ways to split the days

This book follows fourteen walking days. They are not the only plan. The Spanish Wikipedia says the standard walk takes ten to fifteen days.

- **In Asturias.** The official pages divide the road to the pass into seven stages: Oviedo to Grado (~26 km), Grado to Salas (~22 km), Salas to Tineo (~19 km), Tineo to Pola de Allande (~28 km), Pola de Allande to Berducedo (~17 km), Berducedo to Grandas de Salime (~20 km) and Grandas de Salime to the pass (~13 km). They say each can be subdivided as often as you like.
- **In Galicia.** The Xunta de Galicia divides its part into eight stages: Alto do Acevo to Paradavella (~25 km), Paradavella to Castroverde (~20 km), Castroverde to Lugo (~22 km), Lugo to San Romao da Retorta (~19 km), San Romao da Retorta to Melide (~28 km), Melide to Arzúa (~15 km), Arzúa to Arca (~18 km) and Arca to Santiago (~20 km).
- **Shorter days.** The day to Lugo can break at Castroverde, and the day to Melide at the bridge of Ferreira.

Which towns have a hostel, and which are open this month, changes from season to season, so this book lists none. The Asturias pages say there is enough lodging to let you stop when your feet tire. Use the CaminoPlus app and ask locally.
