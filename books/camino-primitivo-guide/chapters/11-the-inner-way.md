# The inner Way

## Every reason is a reason

People come to the Primitivo for faith, for sport, for grief, for curiosity, for a rest from work, or for no reason they can name yet. This book does not rank them. The Pilgrim's Office describes the credencial as meant for those who walk with a Christian sentiment, "even if it is only with an attitude of search". You are welcome on the road whatever you bring to it, and the road does not ask you to explain.

## An old road

The tradition behind this Way is old and short on certainty. Asturias tells of a king who came to see a discovery, and says in the same breath that the historical sources are not very reliable. You will walk beside ruins that remind you how long the road has been used: the sites of the old pilgrims' hospitals on the Hospitales, at Fonfaraón and Valparaíso, the half-ruined hospital at Montouto, the chapel that survives at Buspol. The Confraternity of St James records a saying that those who go to Santiago and do not visit the Saviour in Oviedo visit the servant and leave the Lord.

Asturias also says that those who know the Jacobean routes well call the Primitivo the one that has best kept the essence of the old pilgrimage, and gives the cause: a country of mountains that stayed remote and tied to the land. You do not have to believe in the essence. You can notice that the days are quiet.

## What a climb gives

A long climb is a plain teacher. It sets the pace and will not be hurried, and it asks only for the next step. You may find that your thoughts slow down to match. You may find the opposite, and that a day of hard walking brings up everything you were not thinking about. Either is allowed. Wet days and mist have their own lesson, which is to attend to the ground and to the next marker.

If you walk in grief, the road holds you without asking. If you walk with doubt, walk with it. If you walk for sport, walk well, and notice who is walking beside you. If you walk with others, leave a day of the road to yourself.

## Questions for the walk

Take one a day, and let it ride in your pocket. You do not have to answer.

- What are you carrying that you could put down?
- What did you come here to leave behind, and what did you come to find?
- What would you do with this hour if you were not in a hurry?
- Who do you wish were walking beside you, and what would you say to them?
- What does your body need that you have been ignoring?
- When did you last feel that you had enough?

## When the road is hard

Some days the climb is not a teacher. It is only a climb. On those days the kindest thing is a short plan: the next village, the next marker. Walk to that, and ask for help when you need it.
