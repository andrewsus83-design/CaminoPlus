# Before you go

## Your credencial

The credencial is your pilgrim's passport. The Pilgrim's Office in Santiago describes the official model as a card of sixteen concertina-folded pages, with boxes for the stamps you collect on the way. You can get it in person at the Office, or from institutions the cathedral has authorised: parish churches, associations of friends of the Way, pilgrim hostels and confraternities, in Spain and abroad. Some associations are authorised to issue their own. The Office also describes a digital credential, an app for Android and iOS.

The Office gives the credencial two practical purposes: access to the hostels of the Way, and proof when you ask for the Compostela. It also says the credencial "does not generate any rights". Stamps are usually collected where you sleep, and also in parish churches, monasteries, cathedrals, hotels, town halls and other places. Get yours before you start walking, and keep it dry.

## Getting to Oviedo, and home from Santiago

Wikipedia says Oviedo has a railway station with long-distance, regional and suburban services, and that the city is served by Asturias Airport, ~48 km away, linked to Oviedo by motorway and a scheduled bus. Santiago's airport, according to Wikipedia, lies ~12 km east of the city and has flights to Spanish and European cities. Timetables and routes change, so check them close to your date, and book your way home before you go: it is easier to plan with a clear head than at the end of the road.

If you would rather not carry your pack, the Asturias tourism pages list luggage-transport services that move bags from one lodging to the next. Ask which of them cover your days; the pages carry the current names and terms.

## Training

Asturias advises starting to walk a few months before you go, in comfortable footwear that is already broken in. The climbs matter here, so train on hills, and walk several days in a row with the pack you will carry. Practise the long days, not only the short ones. If you cannot do ~20 km on tired legs at home, the mountains will tell you so.

Surfaces change a lot. The official stage pages describe the first day as mostly on tarmac roads, the fourth and fifth as mostly on dirt and gravel tracks, and the sixth as about half tarmac. Choose shoes that suit both, and test them for a long day before you leave.

## Packing

The Asturias tourism pages recommend a rucksack that does not weigh more than ten kilos. They list sun cream, a waterproof jacket, a basic first-aid kit, comfortable broken-in footwear, sandals or flip-flops for the evening, and a torch or head torch. They also name a walking stick among the essential items. For this road add what the weather asks for:

- **Layers.** A warm layer, a hat and light gloves. The Confraternity warns that these hills can be damp and cold even in summer.
- **A real waterproof**, and a way to keep one dry set of clothes dry.
- **Poles**, if you use them, for the long descents.
- **Food and water for the days that pass few villages.** The official page calls the fifth stage extremely natural and the sixth one that hardly passes through any towns.
- **Your maps, saved before you go.** Mountain signal can fail. The Confraternity says the waymarking is good overall, and also that you can be shrouded in mist for a whole day.

Weigh the pack before you leave. Everything you add is carried up the climbs of ~600 m.

## Money and paperwork

Carry some cash for small villages, and a card for the bigger towns. Keep your passport or identity card with your credencial. Check the cover on your health insurance, and make a note of **112**, the European emergency number.

## Language

Spanish will serve you in both regions. In Asturias it is the only official language, though Asturian is also spoken and protected by law, and the official pages give place names in both. In Galicia, Galician and Spanish are both official languages, and the Xunta's pages use the Galician names. A few words go a long way: *buenos días*, *gracias*, *¿dónde está...?* and *Buen Camino*.

## What to decide before you leave

1. **The high road or the low road.** Decide the rule you will use on the day, not the answer: the chapter on the two roads gives it.
2. **How many days.** This book follows fourteen. Add a rest day in Lugo, or two short days in the mountains, if you are unsure.
3. **Where you will sleep on the days that pass few villages.** Look in the app and ask locally, and book if you are walking in a busy month.
