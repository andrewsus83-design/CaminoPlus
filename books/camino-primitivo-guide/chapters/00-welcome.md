# Welcome

The Camino Primitivo is the Original Way, and it is the demanding one. It leaves Oviedo, the old capital of Asturias, climbs through slate-roofed mountain villages, crosses into Galicia at a pass of ~1,030 m, passes the Roman walls of Lugo, and at Melide joins the last stretch of the Camino Francés to the cathedral of Santiago de Compostela. It is ~320 km in all.

**The promise of this book is simple.** By the last page you will know whether the mountains are your Camino, what each of the fourteen walking days asks of you, and what to check before you leave home.

## How to use it

If you are a month or more away, read the next four chapters in order: the Way in brief, whether it suits you, when to go, and what to do before you go. Then read the days by region, and the chapter on the high road, which covers the one big choice on this Camino.

If you are on the road tonight, go straight to your stage. Each day says what the walking is like, what is worth a pause, and where to take care.

If you are only dreaming, start with "Is this Camino for you?" and finish with "The inner Way".

## What this book is not

It is not a live map, a timetable or a booking service. It is written from public sources, named at the end, and not from a walk. It names no hostels, cafes or shops, and it gives no prices, opening hours or bed counts. They change from season to season, and a wrong one costs a tired person dearly. For those, use the CaminoPlus app and ask locally.

Every distance here carries a tilde, **~**, because it is approximate. Published lengths differ between sources, and this book says so where it matters. Where something depends on the season (snow, mist, a closed path, heat, a river after rain), it says *check before you go*, and means it. On this road that line matters more than on most.

## A few words you will meet

*Buen Camino*, "good Way", is what pilgrims and locals say to one another as you pass. An *albergue* is a pilgrims' hostel. The *credencial* is your pilgrim's passport, a folded card that you carry and have stamped along the way. Each stamp is a *sello*. The *Compostela* is the certificate the Pilgrim's Office in Santiago gives to those who meet its rules, which the chapter on arriving sets out.

In Asturias the official pages give both the Spanish and the Asturian name of a place (Oviedo and Uviéu, Grado and Grau, Tineo and Tinéu); this book uses the Spanish forms there. In Galicia it follows the Galician forms of the Xunta de Galicia, such as O Cádavo and A Fonsagrada.

Carry what you need, ask before you gamble with the weather, and take your time over the long hill. *Buen Camino.*
