# The inner Way

People walk to Santiago for many reasons, and they do not have to be the same as yours. Some walk out of faith: the Way is a Christian pilgrimage to the tomb of the apostle James, and the Pilgrim's Office gives the Compostela to those who come for religious or spiritual reasons. Some walk for sport, or for a rest from work, or because a friend did. Some walk out of grief, or at a turning point, when a life needs a pause. Some walk simply because they are curious about what six days of walking will do. All of these are welcome on the road, and none of them needs defending.

## What the road does

A short Camino has its own gift. Six days is long enough to leave your phone in your pocket for an hour, then for an afternoon, and short enough that you can take the time off without a great arrangement. On the English Way the first days run beside water, and water has a way of slowing the mind. Then the road climbs, and the hills teach patience. Then it goes through woods, and the woods teach attention. By the last morning, in the oak forest before the city, many walkers find they have stopped counting kilometres.

There is also an old story under your feet. For centuries people came here by sea, after weeks on a ship, to set foot on this coast and walk the last stretch on land. The official site says the voyage took at least two to four weeks, wind and currents permitting. Think of them when you cross the old bridge at Pontedeume or sit in the shade of a church. They did not have a map on a phone, and you are not the first to be tired here.

## Walking with a question

You do not need a question, but if you have one, it will keep you company. Here are a few for the days, one for each stage, to take or leave.

1. **Ferrol to Neda.** What did you bring that you do not need? You can put some of it down.
2. **Neda to Pontedeume.** Whom are you walking with, even if you walk alone?
3. **Pontedeume to Betanzos.** What is a hill you have climbed before, and what did it take?
4. **Betanzos to Hospital de Bruma.** What is the hard thing in front of you, and what is the next small step?
5. **Hospital de Bruma to Sigüeiro.** What can you let go of, as the road lets go of the hills?
6. **Sigüeiro to Santiago.** What do you hope to carry home, and what will you leave here?

## For every kind of walker

If you walk with faith, you will find churches along the way, open some of the time, where you can sit quietly or pray. If you do not, you will find the same places quiet and cool, and worth a rest. If your reason is grief, give yourself permission to cry on the road: most walkers will understand. If you walk to get fit, let the beauty in anyway. The road itself does not ask you for a reason at the door. It only asks you to keep walking.
