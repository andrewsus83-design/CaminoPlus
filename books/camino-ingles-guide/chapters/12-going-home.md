# Going home

The walk ends on a Tuesday afternoon, or a Saturday, or whenever it ends, and then you are back in a kitchen with a washing machine and a phone full of messages. You may find the first days at home odd. The pace is faster, the rooms are louder, and the thing you carried for six days has no obvious place to go.

Be gentle with yourself. A few small habits help.

- **Keep your credencial.** The stamps are a record of where you slept, and the pages will mean more in ten years than they do now. Put it where you will see it.
- **Write it down.** Three lines a day for the first week, about a place, a person or a feeling, are enough. The details go fast.
- **Keep one thing from the road.** An early walk before work, a minute of silence at lunch, a Sunday spent on foot. One habit is enough.
- **Tell someone.** The people who did not walk with you will want a story. Give them one, and keep one back for yourself.
- **Mend your feet, and thank them.**

The Way has a habit of calling people back. Some walk the Camino Inglés once and know it was enough. Others find themselves looking at a map of the Portuguese or the French Way a month later. If that is you, you have time. Rest, then plan, and be ready to carry less next time.

*Buen Camino*, whenever you set out again.
