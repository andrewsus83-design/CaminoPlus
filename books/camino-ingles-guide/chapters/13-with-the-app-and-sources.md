# With the app, and sources

## With the CaminoPlus app

**CaminoPlus** is the offline-first companion app for the Camino de Santiago, with maps that work without signal, stays and cafes, a pilgrim passport with stamps, a journal and friends. The paid plan is called **Pilgrim Plus**. The app is still in development, so look in the app for what is available on the English Way today, and use it, not this book, for beds, cafes, closures and weather.

A reminder about the passport: the Pilgrim's Office asks for the stamped credencial. A passport in any app is a keepsake until the Office says otherwise.

## Sources and thanks

This book rests on public sources, and the fact ledger that goes with it lists every fact against the page it came from. The chief ones are:

- **The Asociación de Concellos do Camiño Inglés**, the official English Way site (caminoingles.gal): the stage figures and descriptions, the history page, the planning page, the questions and answers, the gastronomy page, the transport page and the A Coruña route.
- **The Xunta de Galicia**, the regional government, on its official Camino de Santiago portal (caminodesantiago.gal): the length of the route, its stages and the Neda to Miño stage.
- **The Pilgrim's Reception Office of the Cathedral of Santiago** (oficinadelperegrino.com): the rules of the Compostela, the credencial, the certificate of distance and the history of the pilgrimage.
- **The Confraternity of St James** (csj.org.uk): the rule for the Compostela, the credencial and the certificate.
- **Wikipedia**, for history and geography: the articles on the English Way, Ferrol, Pontedeume, Betanzos, Santiago de Compostela and its cathedral, A Coruña, Galicia, Spain, the Jacobean Holy Year, Margery Kempe, Fernán Pérez de Andrade and Santiago airport. It is also where this book found the Pilgrim's Office statistics for 2024.
- **Published day-length tables for Santiago de Compostela**, checked against a standard solar calculation, for the hours of daylight in December and June.
- **The CaminoPlus route catalog**, for the names and order of the six stages. Stage lengths were compared with the Xunta portal and with a dossier on the Camino Inglés by the Spanish federation of associations of friends of the Camino, published in April 2024.

Published stage lengths differ by up to about a kilometre, and a few pages disagree with themselves, so every distance in this book is marked ~. If you know a better figure, or a fact that has changed, tell CaminoPlus.

Thanks go to the people who keep the Way: the associations of friends of the Camino, the hosts who open the hostels, and the people in the towns and villages who point the way and say *Buen Camino* to a stranger.

## A short glossary

- **Albergue**: a pilgrims' hostel with shared rooms.
- **Botafumeiro**: the great censer of Santiago cathedral.
- **Buen Camino**: "good Way", the greeting between pilgrims.
- **Castro**: a prehistoric fortified settlement.
- **Compostela**: the Latin certificate the Pilgrim's Office issues.
- **Credencial**: the pilgrim's passport.
- **Hospitalero**: the host of a pilgrims' hostel.
- **Menú del peregrino**: a set meal for pilgrims.
- **Ría**: a long sea inlet, as at Ferrol and Betanzos.
- **Sello**: a stamp in the credencial.
- **Xacobeo**: the Galician name for a Holy Year.
