# Welcome

The English Way is the short Camino. Six walking days take you from the old naval city of Ferrol, on Galicia's Atlantic coast, to the cathedral of Santiago de Compostela. It is ~113 km in all, which is small enough to hold in your head and big enough to change you a little.

**The promise of this book is simple.** By the last page you will know whether this is your Camino, what each of the six days asks of you, and what to check before you leave home.

## How to use it

If you are a month or more away, read the next four chapters in order: the Way in brief, whether it suits you, when to go, and what to do before you go. Then read the days, and the chapter on the A Coruña start, which explains a choice many people puzzle over.

If you are on the road tonight, go straight to your stage. Each day says what the walking is like, what is worth a pause, and where to take care.

If you are only dreaming, start with "Is this Camino for you?" and finish with "The inner Way". You can decide the rest later.

## What this book is not

It is not a live map, a timetable or a booking service, and it does not pretend to be. It names no hostels, cafes or shops, and it gives no prices, opening hours or bed counts. They change from season to season, and a wrong one costs a tired person dearly. For those, use the CaminoPlus app and ask locally.

This book was written with AI assistance, from public sources, and its author has not walked the road. So it gives you sources instead of memories, and it says plainly when the sources disagree.

Every distance here carries a tilde, **~**, because it is approximate. Published stage lengths differ from one source to the next, by up to about a kilometre, and this book says so where it matters. Where something depends on the season (a closure, a diversion, heat, a river after rain), it says *check before you go*, and means it.

## A few words you will meet

*Buen Camino*, "good Way", is what pilgrims and locals say to one another as you pass. An *albergue* is a pilgrims' hostel. The *credencial* is your pilgrim's passport, a folded card that you carry and have stamped along the way. Each stamp is a *sello*. The *Compostela* is the certificate the Pilgrim's Office in Santiago gives to those who meet its rules; you will meet those rules in plain language later in the book.

They are explained again when they first matter, and used plainly after that.

Pack lightly, walk gently, and take your time over the long hill. *Buen Camino.*
