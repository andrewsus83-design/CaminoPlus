# When to go

The official site says the English Way can be walked at any time of the year. It also says that the thing to prepare for, in whichever season you choose, is rain. This chapter gives you patterns, not forecasts. For the weather on your own days, look at a forecast the evening before.

## The shape of the year

Galicia has an Atlantic climate: mild, green and wet. The best long-term figures are for Santiago, at the end of the route. They describe a temperate oceanic climate with between ~1,500 and ~1,800 mm of rain a year, depending on which table you read. The wettest months are October, November, December and January. The driest and brightest are the summer months, and the official site says there is less rain on the route from May to September.

Temperatures stay moderate all year. In Santiago the average daily high in January is ~12 °C and the average low ~5 °C. In July the average high is ~25 °C and the low ~14 °C. Those are averages, so the days of a particular week can be warmer or colder, and a hot spell in summer is worth respecting.

## Daylight

Daylight matters more than people expect. In Santiago it lasts ~9 hours around the shortest day in December and ~15 hours around the longest in June. Galicia sits at the western edge of Spain's time zone, so by the clock the sun comes up late and goes down late: close to nine o'clock on a midwinter morning, and a little after ten in the evening at midsummer.

For you that means two things. In winter, a ~24 km day has to fit into the light, and a late start can end in the dark. In summer you have the opposite luxury, and the sensible plan is to walk early, rest through the hottest hours, and enjoy the long evening.

## How busy is it?

The Pilgrim's Office counts the pilgrims who finish each route. For the English Way in 2024, as reported by Wikipedia, the pattern by month was this:

- January and February: ~130 and ~170 finishers
- March: ~1,100
- April: ~2,500
- May, June and July: between ~3,200 and ~3,600 each
- August: ~4,700, the busiest month
- September: ~3,700
- October: ~2,500
- November and December: ~450 and ~320

Read it as a rough guide. A month of ~2,500 is still dozens of pilgrims a day, so April and October are quiet without being empty. In August you will want to think about beds. In January the count works out at only a handful of pilgrims a day, so in the depth of winter you may walk for hours without meeting another one, and fewer places along the way may be open: another reason to check before you go.

## What a season feels like

- **Spring** is a change from wet to drier. The days lengthen quickly, and the official site's drier season begins in May. Before that, expect showers and muddy paths: this is Galicia.
- **Summer** is the brightest and the busiest. Start early, carry water, and book ahead for the nights you cannot afford to be without a bed.
- **Autumn** trades the crowds of summer for the return of the rain. The light is still reasonable in October, and the walking is quiet.
- **Winter** is not cold by the figures above, but it is wet, with short days. It can be beautiful, and it asks for more planning, not less.

## Festivals and Holy Years

Towns on the way have their fiestas. Betanzos holds an August festival for San Roque with a very large paper balloon, and later in the month decorated boats go along the Mandeo to Os Caneiros, according to Wikipedia. Pontedeume's patron-saint festivities fall in September, on the 8th and 10th. A town in festival is wonderful and busy, so look at the dates when you plan your nights.

The next **Holy Year**, or Xacobeo, is 2027, then 2032. A Jacobean Holy Year falls whenever 25 July is a Sunday. The Holy Door of the cathedral is opened only in those years, and in a Holy Year the great censer, the botafumeiro, is used at all the Pilgrims' Masses. In the Holy Year of 2010 the Pilgrim's Office recorded 272,703 pilgrims who qualified for the Compostela, which gives you an idea of how many people the city welcomes. If you are walking in 2027, plan for a busier Santiago.

## Check before you go

Closures, diversions, fires, storms and heat change from year to year. Before you set out, check the official Camino Inglés site and the current information in the CaminoPlus app for any change to the route or the services. If you are going in the quiet months, ask locally what is open.
