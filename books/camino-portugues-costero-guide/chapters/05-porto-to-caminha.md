# Porto to Caminha: Days 1 to 4

## The character of these days

Four days take you out of a big city and north along the Portuguese shore to the mouth of the Minho. They are towns of the sea (Vila do Conde, Póvoa de Varzim, Esposende, Viana do Castelo, Caminha), each with a river or an estuary to cross.

A note on the figures. This book found no official stage table for the Portuguese part that it could read. The day lengths follow the legs marked on an official guide leaflet of the ten municipalities (its file dates from 2017), and published plans differ from them by several kilometres, as each day says. The road also has more than one line, so a distance depends on the line. Treat every figure as a good guide, not a measurement.

### Day 1: Porto to Vila do Conde (~29 km)

You start at the Sé, and the Confraternity of St James says that the Central and Coastal routes alike spend their first day walking through the urban and industrial sprawl of Porto. That is the honest summary. If that does not appeal, the Confraternity describes a different start, the Litoral, which the chapter on other ways sets out.

The traditional line passes Matosinhos, where Wikipedia says the monastery of Leça do Balio, dated 1180, was known for the help it gave pilgrims, and then the countryside around Maia. The certification text names the monasteries of Leça do Balio and of Moreira da Maia among the places that testify to pilgrimage along the route. It also says that the way into Vila do Conde partly follows the Via Veteris, the old road of the royal inquiries of 1258, which was for centuries the chief link between the two towns. So this is the oldest, best-documented stretch of the whole road.

The leaflet marks ~29 km. Published plans cut the day differently: one stops at Labruge, ~25 km out; others run on past Vila do Conde to Póvoa de Varzim, ~37 to ~40 km from Porto. Vila do Conde is where the leaflet ends the first day. Wikipedia dates its Monastery of Santa Clara to 1318 and says the church of Azurara, by the river, was rebuilt in 1502 to commemorate the pilgrimage of King Manuel I.

### Day 2: Vila do Conde to Esposende (~27 km)

The way goes through Póvoa de Varzim, where Wikipedia mentions a small chapel of St James, dated 1582, on the square, and a fishermen's quarter of the 18th century. It is also a town with a sobering sea story: Wikipedia records that on 27 February 1892 seven fishing boats were wrecked in a storm just off the shore and 105 fishermen died. Boardwalks run along the beach here, and Wikipedia pictures one at Santo André.

Esposende lies on the Cávado. Wikipedia says the crossing is now made at Fão, on a bridge of 1892, where barges once ran from Barca do Lago. It pictures a way of ~1.5 km over the dunes at Ofir and a pine wood at Fão.

The leaflet marks ~27 km to Marinhas, on the Esposende side, a little beyond the town. If you stop in Esposende itself, the day is shorter and the next one longer. Published plans give ~21 km from Póvoa de Varzim to Esposende and ~24.5 km from Póvoa to Marinhas.

### Day 3: Esposende to Viana do Castelo (~26 km)

This is the day of the Neiva and the Lima. You cross the Neiva and come to Castelo de Neiva, where the certification text records a church of St James consecrated in the 9th century, and the Municipality of Viana do Castelo calls it the oldest consecrated to the apostle outside Spanish territory.

The leaflet marks ~26 km from Marinhas to Viana. One published plan says ~21 km from Marinhas, another ~24 km from Esposende; the road has more than one line here, and the differences are real. Viana do Castelo waits across the Eiffel bridge over the Lima, which Wikipedia says carried the railway, reaching the town in 1878, to a design by Gustave Eiffel. The Municipality points to the Hospital Velho, a medieval pilgrims' hostel, and the sanctuary of Santa Luzia stands above the city on its hill, begun in 1904 according to Wikipedia.

### Day 4: Viana do Castelo to Caminha (~27 km)

The Municipality of Viana describes the way out of the city through Areosa and Carreço, past carved rocks, farm buildings, chapels and wayside crosses, to Afife, the convent of Cabanas and the old *cividade* of Afife, where Viana's part ends. Wikipedia describes the *cividade* as a settlement site of the Castro culture, occupied up to Roman times. The leaflet and one published plan agree on ~27 km, and another plan stops at Vila Praia de Âncora, ~19 km from Viana, to make two shorter days.

Caminha sits on the Minho estuary, about two kilometres from the Atlantic, says Wikipedia, and its old keep became the town's clock tower in the 17th century. The Mother Church dates from the 16th. The estuaries of the Minho and the Coura form a protected landscape, which Wikipedia describes as wetlands, sandbanks and reed beds that attract birds, above all in winter. From here the road goes into Spain, by one of three ways, which the next chapter sets out.

*Check before you go:* diversions and path closures, and the state of the boardwalks after storms.
