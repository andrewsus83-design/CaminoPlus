# Other ways in and out, and other ways to split the days

## The Litoral start

The Confraternity of St James describes a way out of Porto for those who do not fancy the first day of the usual route. The Litoral starts at the cathedral like the others but drops to the promenades of the Douro estuary, follows them to the sea, and goes north on promenades and boardwalks to Vila do Conde, where it becomes the Coastal route. Wikipedia describes a similar idea as a contemporary version of the Coastal Way, pushed by German pilgrims, that keeps to the sea along beach walkways. It says this version is gaining importance because the traditional line is increasingly built up, and that bicycles are not allowed on some walkways. This book found no official length for either line, and its day figures follow the traditional one.

## Three ways into Spain

At Caminha the road has a choice. The Municipality of Viana do Castelo, the tourism authority of Porto and the North and the Municipality of Vila Nova de Cerveira all say that the Coastal Way is an alternative link to Galicia and that the Minho can be crossed at three places.

- **Caminha to A Guarda, by boat.** This is the line the Xunta describes for the Coastal route, and the one this book follows. It joins the Central route at Redondela.
- **Vila Nova de Cerveira to Goián, across the river.** The Portuguese sources name it as the second crossing, and this book found nothing more about it. Ask locally.
- **Valença to Tui, over the bridge.** This is the end of the route that Portugal has certified: ~138 km from Porto to the border at Valença, according to the national tourism authority. From Caminha to Valença the Municipality of Vila Nova de Cerveira describes a gentle way parallel to the Minho, at the foot of Monte de Goios and the Serra da Gávea, through the old centre of Vila Nova de Cerveira, whose castle King Dinis had built to guard the crossing. The leaflet marks ~16 km to Vila Nova de Cerveira and ~16 km more to Valença; one published plan marks ~12.5 and ~14.5. The tourism authority calls Valença the meeting point of the Central and Coastal routes. From Tui you walk the Central route, which *The Portuguese Way* describes, and meet this book's road again at Redondela.

So there are two joins, not one: Redondela, if you take the boat, and Valença and Tui, if you follow the river. The Xunta's coastal route takes the first, and the Portuguese certification the second.

## The Spiritual Variant

The Confraternity describes the Spiritual Variant as relatively newly established. It begins in Pontevedra, leaves the Central route for Vilanova de Arousa on the shore of the Sea of Arousa, and from there goes by boat up the estuary to Pontecesures, where it rejoins the Central route to Santiago. The Confraternity adds that this is the way by which the body of St James is said to have travelled, in a stone boat.

The Xunta has a page for a related route, the Mar de Arousa and Río Ulla Route, which commemorates the arrival of the apostle's body by sea. It speaks of forty nautical miles from the entrance of the ría to the old Iria Flavia, and then about 25 km on foot to Santiago. The Xunta pages read for this book do not mention the Spiritual Variant by name, and this book found no official length for it. A walker on the Coastal Way reaches Pontevedra on Day 9, so the variant is open from there. A route that depends on a boat depends on the boat: ask locally before you plan around it.

## Other ways to split the days

The twelve days follow the leaflet's legs in Portugal and the Xunta's stages in Galicia. They are not the only plan: the official portal says an average of seven days for the Portuguese part, the published plans read for this book count thirteen stages for the whole road, and the Xunta has eight stages in Galicia. Here are the commoner changes.

- **Cut Day 1.** One published plan stops at Labruge, ~25 km from Porto. Others run on past Vila do Conde to Póvoa de Varzim, ~37 to ~40 km.
- **Stop short on Day 4.** One published plan ends at Vila Praia de Âncora, ~19 km from Viana do Castelo, and walks on to Caminha the next morning.
- **Join Days 5 and 6.** The Xunta's two stages from A Guarda to Baiona come to ~35 km and divide at Oia. The leaflet puts its stop a little further on, at Mougás.
- **Cut Day 7.** The Xunta's sections give you places to stop: A Ramallosa, Nigrán, Coruxo.
- **Add a rest day.** Pontevedra is the natural place, as *The Portuguese Way* explains.

Whatever you choose, the arithmetic is the same: twelve days, ~272 km on this book's figures, so ~23 km a day on average, less on the short days.
