# Vigo to Santiago: Days 8 to 12

## The character of these days

One day of coast remains, the walk from Vigo to Redondela, and then four days on the road that the Central route shares. *The Portuguese Way*, in this series, walks that road day by day from the other side; here you get each day in short. The lengths and descriptions are, again, those of the Xunta's coastal stage pages. Its Central pages differ from these by up to ~0.4 km on some of the same days (~25.2 km against ~24.8 km on the last one), which shows how soft such figures are.

### Day 8: Vigo to Redondela (~16 km)

Rated Low, ~16.3 km, ~3 h 10 min. In Vigo the way passes the Paseo de Alfonso XII, by the olive tree that is the city's symbol, near a surviving stretch of the old entrance to the walled town. It goes down to the O Berbés quarter and by A Pedra, known for its restaurants and the oysters of the ría. On Rosalía de Castro Street the Xunta points to the Salinae site, an underground museum of Roman saltworks.

Then comes the longer part, ~10.1 km, through Paradela, Parada, Trasmañó, Rande and Portela. Redondela is where the coastal route meets the inland Portuguese route. It has railway viaducts of the 1870s, a church of Santiago that Archbishop Gelmírez consecrated in 1114, and each May the Festa do Choco, a cuttlefish festival.

### Day 9: Redondela to Pontevedra (~18 km)

Rated Medium, ~18.2 km, ~4 h 35 min. Leaving Redondela you pass the chapel of As Angustias and the parish of Cesantes, and the Ría de Vigo opens on your left. The Spanish page puts it to the west; the English page says east, which does not match the Spanish. In the middle of the ría are the islands of San Simón and San Antón, and under the water lie the galleons of the Battle of Rande of 1702. In the distance Rande Bridge rises above the mussel rafts.

At Arcade the way crosses the medieval Sampaio Bridge over the Verdugo, where the municipality of Pontevedra begins, mid-bridge, and goes on along old stone paths, with sections of the Roman road Via XIX, such as the climb of A Brea Vella de A Canicouva. It reaches Pontevedra past the shrine of the Virxe Peregrina and on into the historic centre. *The Portuguese Way* suggests Pontevedra for a rest day, and says why. It is also where the Spiritual Variant leaves the road, as the next chapter explains.

### Day 10: Pontevedra to Caldas de Reis (~23 km)

Rated Medium, ~23 km, ~5 h 45 min. You cross the Lérez on the bridge of O Burgo and skirt the wetland of A Xunqueira de Alba. Three stone crosses stand along the way, one carved with St James the Pilgrim looking north to Compostela. Then comes a warning: watch the signs, because the route weaves with the N-550 road, which is always present, and a new high-speed railway has altered the landscape.

Caldas de Reis is a spa town, the *Aquae Celenis* of the Antonine Itinerary, a document of the 3rd century that records the routes of the Roman Empire. You cross a bridge to the hot spring that gives the town its name, and a medieval bridge over the Bermaña closes the day.

### Day 11: Caldas de Reis to Padrón (~19 km)

Rated Medium, ~18.7 km, ~4 h 45 min. You leave Caldas by the N-550 but take a path almost at once, climbing to the church complex of Santa Mariña de Carracedo, then through the woods and old mills of Valga. In Pontecesures the bridge over the Ulla, Roman in origin and much changed, separates the provinces of Pontevedra and A Coruña. Padrón is the cradle of the Jacobean tradition, the Xunta says. In the church of Santiago is kept O Pedrón, a Roman altar stone to which a carved cross is said to have been added by the disciples of St James.

### Day 12: Padrón to Santiago (~25 km)

Rated Low, ~24.8 km, ~5 h. You pass Iria Flavia, once a Roman city and an episcopal see until the 11th century, and the foundation of the writer Camilo José Cela. At the crossing of the N-550 a branch to the right takes you out of the traffic. The sanctuary of A Escravitude gives views over the valleys.

Then O Milladoiro: the *humilladoiro*, where pilgrims knelt on first seeing the cathedral. Beyond it the way crosses the Sar by the Old Bridge and offers two clearly signed ways into the city. It enters the old quarter by the Porta Faxeira and the Rúa do Franco, and the traditional entrance to the cathedral for pilgrims on the Portuguese route is through the Praza das Praterías.

*Check before you go:* diversions around the N-550 and the new railway, and the Xunta's current route.
