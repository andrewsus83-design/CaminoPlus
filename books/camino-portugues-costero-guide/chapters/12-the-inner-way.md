# The inner Way

## Why people walk

People walk this road for faith, for sport, for grief, for curiosity, for a rest from work, for a promise made to someone else. The Pilgrim's Office has its own rule for what it certifies, and the chapter on the Compostela rule gives it. The road itself asks nothing of the kind. Every reason is a good one for walking, and none of them needs to be explained to the people you meet.

## What the road gives

It gives you hours with no task. For twelve days the feet, the weather, the tide and the next town set the agenda, and what you do with the quiet is yours.

This road has its own places for thinking. A shore where, the Xunta says, the mouth of the Miño is at its most beautiful at low tide and at sunset, with rushes and cormorants on the marsh. A boat you cannot hurry, which for once makes the timetable somebody else's. A coast with the ocean always on your left. A hill at O Milladoiro, the *humilladoiro*, where the Xunta says pilgrims knelt on first seeing the cathedral.

The sea is also where the old story of this road begins. The Xunta tells how ancient Christian traditions and medieval texts, above all Book III of the Codex Calixtinus, say that the body of the Apostle came to Galicia by sea, up the Ulla to Padrón, in a stone boat. You need not believe a legend to feel something when you cross the Ulla at Pontecesures. Wikipedia keeps a harder memory of the same sea: the fishermen of Póvoa de Varzim who died in the storm of February 1892. A coast is a place where people lived by the water, and sometimes lost.

## Questions for the walk

Take one a day, or none. They are better walked than answered.

- What are you carrying that is not in your pack?
- Whom would you like to have beside you on this road, and what would you say to them?
- What would "enough" look like today, for your feet and for your heart?
- When the wind or the weather is against you, what do you do, and what does it tell you about yourself?
- What are you walking towards, and what are you walking away from? Is the second a reason to turn round, or only a reason to keep going?

You do not have to arrive at an answer in Santiago. A better question is a fair thing to bring home.
