# Across the Minho and the Galician coast: Days 5 to 7

## The character of these days

Three days take you across a border by water and along the coast of southern Galicia: cliffs, a lighthouse, a fortress, then the long ría of Vigo with its beaches and its city. The lengths and descriptions, unless another source is named, are those of the Xunta de Galicia's stage pages for its coastal route. The English pages of the Xunta read like translations and differ from the Spanish in places, so this book read the Spanish originals for every figure and warning. Other sources differ by a kilometre or two.

## The crossing

The Xunta says the coastal route enters Galicia at A Pasaxe, in A Guarda, "after crossing the mouth of the River Miño", and that the walker has said goodbye to Portugal at Caminha. The Confraternity of St James says a boat crosses the estuary, and Wikipedia says the contemporary crossing is by ferryboat from Caminha to A Guarda. The official leaflet marks the leg at ~4 km.

In April 2022 the Xunta announced a boat service for pilgrims and residents between A Guarda and Caminha, with two boats, one electric, and a dedicated electric boat planned for that summer. That was a press release, not a timetable. This book cannot tell you whether a boat runs on your day, or at what hour. A road that depends on a boat depends on the boat. Ask in Caminha the day before, and read the chapter on other ways in and out for the alternative.

### Day 5: Caminha to Oia (~17 km on foot, plus the boat)

A Guarda to Oia is rated Low, ~16.7 km, ~3 h 20 min, though its first section, ~10.8 km to Portecelo, is rated Medium-High. The leaflet breaks the day further on, at Mougás, a little beyond Oia, ~24 km from A Guarda.

A Guarda grew on the lower slopes of Mount Santa Trega, a viewpoint over the Atlantic and Portugal and the site of a pre-Roman fortified settlement, occupied from the 1st century BC to the 1st century AD. It was declared a National Historic-Artistic Monument in 1931, and some of the hill's stones carry petroglyphs. From the top you look down on the mouth of the Miño, especially beautiful at low tide and at sunset, with rushes, cormorants and ducks on the marsh.

From Portecelo the route follows the coast with the ocean on your left. This section is flat, with hardly any shade, and the sea breeze lessens the summer sun. It leads to the monastery of Santa María de Oia, on the shore. Its front, in the austere Cistercian style, dates from the late 12th century; the church has baroque altarpieces; and in the mid-18th century, the Xunta says, the monks beat off an attack by a Turkish fleet. The Xunta also says the monastery was being restored, so check what you can visit. It gives the route its other name, the Monastic Route.

### Day 6: Oia to Baiona (~19 km)

Rated Low, ~18.6 km, ~3 h 45 min. You leave the fishing village of Oia and walk along the foot of the cliffs, past the lighthouse of Cape Silleiro, opened in 1866 and the chief landmark for ships on the south side of the Ría de Vigo.

The route then climbs to Baredo by the old royal path, *vereda real*, and some kilometres later reaches the castle of Monterreal, a hotel today. Its walls held off the English corsair Francis Drake, who landed in 1585, and a path runs around it beside the sea.

Behind the castle lies Baiona. On 1 March 1493 it was the first port in Europe to learn of the discovery of America, from the caravel La Pinta, captained by Martín Alonso Pinzón. The ría of Vigo begins here, sheltered by the Cíes Islands, the heart of the Illas Atlánticas National Park, which you can reach by boat from Baiona.

### Day 7: Baiona to Vigo (~27 km)

The longest day in Galicia: ~26.8 km, ~5 h 25 min, rated Low. Its four sections are ~4.8, ~4.2, ~9 and ~8.9 km, which are the places to split it.

You leave Baiona past the historic fountain of O Pombal and cross a Romanesque bridge over the Groba at Sabarís. The bridge at A Ramallosa is also Romanesque, with ten arches; the Xunta says it may stand on a Roman one. It marks the mouth of the Miñor river, a marsh of great ecological richness. In Nigrán you pass country houses and stone crosses, and at Panxón the Templo Votivo do Mar, by the architect Antonio Palacios. The whole stage has fine white sand beaches and, the Xunta says, a mild climate for most of the year.

Take care on this day. The regional road, the C-550, is a constant presence. For the end, the Xunta recommends the river path beside the Lagares from Samil beach, with its wetlands. An alternative through the Coia district is closer to the old tradition but, says the Xunta, spoilt by large housing developments and too much traffic. Vigo is Galicia's largest city, with close to 300,000 inhabitants and a fishing and container port.

*Check before you go:* the boat across the Minho, access to the monastery at Oia, and the state of the path at Vigo.
