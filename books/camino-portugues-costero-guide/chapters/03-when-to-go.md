# When to go

A road beside the sea is a road beside the weather. The Confraternity of St James describes northern Portugal and Galicia as a changeable maritime climate, where westerly winds bring generous rain, hence "Green Portugal" and "Green Spain". Everything below is a pattern, not a forecast. The figures are averages from weather stations at Porto airport, Viana do Castelo, Vigo airport and Santiago, as Wikipedia reports them. The Viana figures are older (1981 to 2010 for temperature, 1971 to 2000 for rain), and the Vigo and Santiago stations stand inland and high, so the shore itself may differ.

## The seasons

**Spring.** The Confraternity calls spring a rewarding time, cool, with fresh growth. It is also wet: in April the rain is ~96 mm at Porto, ~118 mm at Viana and ~150 mm at Vigo. Carry a proper rain jacket and a plan for wet feet.

**Summer.** The Confraternity warns that summer can be hot and humid, and that the coast is crowded in the peak holiday months. The averages are mild: in July the mean daytime high is ~24 °C at Porto airport, ~26 °C at Viana, ~25 °C at Vigo and ~25 °C at Santiago. Wikipedia adds that the beaches near Porto are often windier and cooler than the land inland. The averages hide the extremes: the record highs are ~38.6 °C at Porto airport, ~39.5 °C at Viana, ~40.8 °C at Vigo airport (7 August 2016) and ~39.4 °C at Santiago. Rain is scarce, ~17 mm in July at Porto and ~40 mm at Vigo. The Pilgrim's Office statistics, as Wikipedia reports them, say that most pilgrims finish the Coastal route in May and in September.

**Autumn.** The Confraternity says early autumn is regarded as fairly dry and settled. By October the rain has returned: ~149 mm at Porto, ~164 mm at Viana, ~208 mm at Vigo and ~214 mm at Santiago, and November is wetter still at Vigo, ~236 mm.

**Winter.** Mild and damp. The mean daytime high in January is ~14 °C at Porto, ~15 °C at Viana and ~12 °C at Vigo and Santiago, with lows of ~5 to ~6 °C. The trouble in winter is rain and short days: January brings ~151 mm at Porto, ~190 mm at Viana, ~222 mm at Vigo.

Over the whole year the rain grows as you go north: ~1,150 mm at Porto, ~1,470 mm at Viana, ~1,530 mm at Santiago and ~1,700 mm at Vigo, which Wikipedia calls the rainiest city in Galicia, with rain of 1 mm or more on ~126 days a year.

## Wind

The sources read for this book give no wind speeds, so there is nothing to quote about how hard it blows. They do say where the wind matters. The Confraternity names westerly winds. Wikipedia says the beaches are windier and cooler than the inland. The Xunta says the sea breeze eases the summer sun on the shore beyond Portecelo. Expect a wind on the open stretches, and look at the day's forecast for the wind as well as the rain.

## Daylight and the clock

At the winter solstice there are about nine hours of daylight at Porto and at Vigo, and about fifteen at the summer solstice (computed for the cities, not looked up). The clocks add a twist: mainland Portugal keeps Western European Time and Galicia Central European Time, so your watch jumps forward an hour as you cross the border. Computed by the local clock, the winter sun rises at about eight o'clock in Porto and about nine in Vigo, and sets at about ten past five in Porto and just after six in Vigo. In June it rises at about six in Porto and at about seven in Vigo, and sets at about ten past nine and about a quarter past ten. In December, plan to be indoors by five in Portugal and six in Spain.

## A month, in a line each

- **March and April:** cool, fresh and wet.
- **May and June:** mild, highs of ~20 to ~25 °C, long days; May is one of the two months when most pilgrims finish.
- **July and August:** warm and dry, crowded on the coast; start early.
- **September:** warm, with the rain beginning to return, and the other busy month.
- **October and November:** wet, and the days shorten.
- **December to February:** the shortest days, with rain.

## Holy Year and checking

Wikipedia lists the next Jacobean Holy Years as 2027 and 2032, years in which the cathedral's Holy Door is opened. If you plan to arrive in 2027, plan early and ask the Pilgrim's Office.

Before any date, look for diversions, closures, fire and heat warnings, and the boat across the Minho. A path can change between this book's date and your walk.
