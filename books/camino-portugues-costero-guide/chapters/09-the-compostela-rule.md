# The Compostela rule, and where your start leaves you

## What the Pilgrim's Office says

The Compostela is the certificate that the Pilgrim's Office in Santiago issues to those who come to the tomb of the Apostle and follow the routes of the Way of St James on foot, by bicycle or on horseback. The Office asks for at least the last hundred kilometres on foot or on horseback, or the last two hundred kilometres by bicycle, shown by the credencial, stamped along the route. On foot or on horseback, the Office asks for at least a hundred continuous kilometres on the same route to Santiago that it recognises. The final stage must include at least the last one leading into the Cathedral.

The Way may be done in stages, if they follow in chronological and geographical order. A pilgrim who walks only the minimum distance must get the credencial stamped at the start and the end of each stage, with the date. A pilgrim who has already walked part of a recognised route outside Spain needs no less than seventy kilometres in Spain. The Cabildo, the cathedral's governing council, reserves the right to grant the Compostela in special and exceptional cases.

The Office awards the Compostela to those who walk for religious or spiritual reasons, and it describes the pilgrimage it has in mind as one made "with a Christian sentiment, even if it is only with an attitude of search". If that does not describe your reason for walking, ask the Pilgrim's Office what it can give you: it also offers a Certificate of Distance, which records the kilometres you walked from wherever you started.

## Which routes the Office recognises

The Office's pages read for this book speak of routes recognised by its own system, and say there are many traditional and recognised routes, but they do not list them. They do not mention the Portuguese Coastal route by name. The Office's own statistics, as Wikipedia reports them, count it as a route of its own. They also do not say how the Office treats a walk that starts on one recognised route and changes to another at a junction, as this one does at Redondela. The Xunta presents the Coastal route as a single route from A Guarda to Santiago. If any of this matters to your plan, ask the Office.

## The arithmetic for this road

Here is the arithmetic, with its sources.

- **From Porto.** The Xunta gives its coastal route in Galicia, from A Guarda, as ~163 km. That is well over the seventy kilometres in Spain that the Office names for those who have walked a recognised route outside Spain.
- **From Vigo.** Add the Xunta's stage figures from Vigo to Santiago and you get ~101 km on its coastal pages, and ~101.4 km if you use its Central figures for the shared days. The Confraternity of St James calls Vigo the hundred-kilometre mark. It is just above the hundred, and where in Vigo you begin counting, which line you walk and how the Office measures can all move it.
- **From Baiona.** The Xunta's stage from Baiona to Vigo adds ~26.8 km, so ~128 km.
- **From Redondela.** ~85 km by the same figures: under the hundred.

None of that is a decision. The Office decides, and rules change. If you plan to start in Vigo, or anywhere near the limit, ask the Pilgrim's Office before you go.
