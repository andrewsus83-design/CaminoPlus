# With the app, and sources

## With the CaminoPlus app

**CaminoPlus** is the offline-first companion app for the Camino de Santiago, with maps that work without signal, stays and cafes, a pilgrim passport with stamps, a journal and friends. The paid plan is called **Pilgrim Plus**. The app is still in development, so look in the app for what is available on the Camino Portugués Costero today, and use it, not this book, for beds, cafes, closures, the boat and the weather.

A reminder about the passport: the Pilgrim's Office asks for the stamped credencial. A passport in any app is a keepsake until the Office says otherwise.

## Sources and thanks

This book rests on public sources, and the fact ledger that goes with it lists every fact against the page it came from. The chief ones are:

- **The Xunta de Galicia**, the regional government, on its official portal (caminodesantiago.gal): the page on the Portuguese Coastal Way and its eight stage pages, read in Spanish as well as English; the page on the Mar de Arousa and Río Ulla Route; and its press release of April 2022 on the boat across the Minho.
- **The government of Portugal**: the order that certifies the route (Portaria n.º 67/2022, of 2 February, in the Diário da República), the national tourism authority's page on the certification, and the pages of the tourism authority of Porto and the North.
- **The ten municipalities of the Caminho Português da Costa**: their official portal and guide leaflet (the file dates from 2017), and the pages of Viana do Castelo and Vila Nova de Cerveira.
- **The Pilgrim's Reception Office of the Cathedral of Santiago** (oficinadelperegrino.com): the Compostela, the credencial, the Certificate of Distance and the notes on arriving.
- **The Confraternity of St James** (csj.org.uk): its page on the Camino Portugués, for the routes out of Porto, the Spiritual Variant, the weather and the way there.
- **Wikipedia**, for history, geography, climate and the Pilgrim's Office statistics for 2025: the articles on the Portuguese Way, Porto, Vila do Conde, Póvoa de Varzim, Viana do Castelo, Caminha, Vigo, Santiago de Compostela and its cathedral, the Jacobean Holy Year, the Schengen Area, the number 112 and time in Portugal and Spain. The climate averages are those of the Portuguese and Spanish weather services.
- **Published walking plans** for the Portuguese days, compared with the leaflet. They are not named here; the ledger lists them.
- **Day-length figures**, computed with a standard solar calculation, and **the CaminoPlus route catalog**, for the route's name, ends, length and difficulty.

The tourism authority's own site, Visit Portugal, could not be read when this was written, so nothing here rests on it. Stage lengths differ from one source to the next, and a few pages disagree with themselves, so every distance in this book is marked ~. This book found no official stage table for Portugal that it could read; the Portuguese figures are therefore the least certain. If you know a better figure, or a fact that has changed, tell CaminoPlus.

Thanks go to the people who keep the Way: the associations of friends of the Camino, the hosts who open the hostels, the municipalities that waymark the road, and the people in the towns and villages who point the way and say *bom caminho* to a stranger.

## A short glossary

- **Albergue**: a pilgrims' hostel with shared rooms.
- **Bom caminho, Buen Camino**: "good Way", the greeting between pilgrims, in Portuguese and in Spanish.
- **Botafumeiro**: the great censer of Santiago cathedral.
- **Carimbo, sello**: a stamp in the credencial, in Portuguese and in Spanish.
- **Certificate of Distance**: the Pilgrim's Office document that records the kilometres you walked.
- **Compostela**: the Latin certificate the Pilgrim's Office issues.
- **Credencial**: the pilgrim's passport.
- **Hospitalero**: the host of a pilgrims' hostel.
- **Humilladoiro**: the place where pilgrims knelt on first seeing the cathedral, in Galician.
- **Menú del peregrino**: a set meal for pilgrims.
- **Ría**: a long sea inlet, as at Vigo and Arousa.
- **Sé**: the cathedral, in Portuguese.
- **Vereda real**: a royal path, an old main road.
- **Xunta**: the regional government of Galicia.
