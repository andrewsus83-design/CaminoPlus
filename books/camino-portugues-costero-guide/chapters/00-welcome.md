# Welcome

The Coastal Way starts where the Portuguese Way starts, at the Sé, the cathedral of Porto, and ends where every Way ends, in Santiago de Compostela. In between it chooses the sea. It follows the Atlantic north through the fishing and beach towns of Portugal, crosses the border by boat across the Minho on its usual line, and in Galicia runs along cliffs and inlets to Baiona and Vigo. At Redondela it meets the other Portuguese road, and the last four days are the same for both. The CaminoPlus catalog gives the length as ~260 km. The twelve walking days of this book add up to ~272 km, and the chapters say why the figures differ.

**The promise of this book is simple.** By the last page you will know whether this is your Camino, what each of the twelve days asks of you, where the road joins its neighbours, and what to check before you leave home.

## How to use it

If you are a month or more away, read the next four chapters in order: the Way in brief, whether it suits you, when to go, and what to do before you go. Then read the days, which come in three chapters, and the two chapters on the other ways in and out and on the Compostela rule.

If you are on the road tonight, go straight to your stage. Each day says what the walking is like, what is worth a pause, and where to take care.

If you are only dreaming, start with "Is this Camino for you?" and finish with "The inner Way". You can decide the rest later.

## What this book is not

It is not a live map, a timetable or a booking service. It names no hostels, cafes or shops, and it gives no prices, opening hours or bed counts. They change from season to season, and a wrong one costs a tired person dearly. For those, use the CaminoPlus app and ask locally. The boat across the Minho is the clearest case: whether it runs, and when, is a question for the day.

This book was written with AI assistance, from public sources, and its author has not walked the road. So it gives you sources instead of memories, and it says plainly when the sources disagree.

It is also not a second copy of *The Portuguese Way*, the book in this series about the Central route. Where the two roads share the ground, from Redondela to Santiago, this book gives the days in short and points you there.

Every distance carries a tilde, **~**, because it is approximate. Where something depends on the season or the day (a boat, a wet boardwalk, a diversion), the book says *check before you go*, and means it.

## A few words you will meet

The road has several names. In English it is the Coastal Way; in Portuguese the *Caminho Português da Costa*; in Spanish the *Camino Portugués de la Costa*; the app's catalog calls it *Costero*. *Caminho* and *Camino* both mean "way" or "road". The greeting between pilgrims is *Buen Camino* in Spanish and *bom caminho* in Portuguese. An *albergue* is a pilgrims' hostel. The *credencial* is your pilgrim's passport, a folded card you carry and have stamped along the way; a stamp is a *sello* in Spanish and a *carimbo* in Portuguese. The *Compostela* is the certificate the Pilgrim's Office in Santiago gives to those who meet its rules, and the chapter on the rule sets them out. A *ría* is a long sea inlet.

They are explained again when they first matter, and used plainly after that.

Pack lightly, carry something against wind and sun, and take your time. *Bom caminho.*
