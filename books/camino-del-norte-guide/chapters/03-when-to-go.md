# When to go

## The short answer

The Confraternity of St James says that, for the weather, any time from May to early October is best. The weather on the northern coast, it says, can be very changeable, from fine summer days to very damp and cold autumn ones, and along the coast the very hot, dry conditions found further south are unusual. The rest of this chapter says what that means.

## Rain and temperature

This is the green side of Spain, and the green comes from rain. These figures come from Wikipedia's climate tables, which use national weather data. Treat them as patterns, not forecasts.

- **San Sebastián** (the weather station at its airport, at Hondarribia, 1991 to 2020): about 1,750 mm of rain a year, on about 143 days with at least a millimetre, which is more than one day in three. November is the wettest month. The average day is about 9 °C in January and about 22 °C in August.
- **Santander**: drier, about 1,070 mm a year on about 128 rainy days. The average day is about 11 °C in January and about 21 °C in August. November is the wettest month here too.
- **Santiago**: the table gives about 1,530 mm a year, while the article's text says about 1,800 mm. Either way it rains, and the wettest months fall in autumn and early winter. The average day is about 9 °C in January and about 20 °C in August.

Heat is rarer on this coast but not impossible. The record for Santander's downtown station is about 40 °C, in August 1940. Carry water and sun cream in summer.

## Daylight

A standard solar calculation gives about nine hours of daylight at the December solstice and about 15.4 hours at the June solstice, for Irun, Santander and Santiago alike. On 1 October there are about 11.6 hours, and on 1 November about 10. From late autumn to early spring, plan to finish each day early.

## Crowds and beds

The northern coast is a holiday coast, and its summer is short. The Confraternity warns that in the height of the summer season lodging can be scarce near the most popular places, and that you should be ready to adapt. It suggests that local buses or trains may take you to a bed a few kilometres from the busiest resorts. The Basque Government's brochure says the same from the other side: many pilgrim hostels open only in summer, and you should plan the next day's stage. Walking towards the start or the end of the year, find out what is open before you leave.

Two Basque festivals fall at the start of the road. The Basque brochure says that on 30 June Irun holds its *alarde*, a parade that descends from the old annual muster of arms, and that Hondarribia holds its own on 8 September.

## 2027 is a Holy Year

A Jacobean Holy Year falls when 25 July, the feast of St James, is a Sunday. That happens in 2027 and again in 2032. In a Holy Year the Holy Door of the cathedral is opened, and Wikipedia says the botafumeiro is used at all the Pilgrims' Masses. Santiago may be busier in such a year, so do not plan a tight connection on the day you arrive.

## A season at a time

- **Spring (March to May).** Daylight grows from about 11 hours on 1 March to about 12.7 on 1 April. The Confraternity's good weather starts in May. Before that, rain is likely and some hostels have not yet opened.
- **Summer (June to August).** The warmest months, and the busiest on the coast. Lodging is tightest near the popular places, so find out before you go.
- **Autumn (September to November).** The Confraternity's good weather lasts into early October. After that the rain returns, San Sebastián's wettest month is November, and the days are down to about ten hours.
- **Winter (December to February).** About nine hours of daylight, a lot of rain, and many hostels closed for the season. Plan every night ahead.

*Check before you go*: closures, boat times and the weather change from year to year.
