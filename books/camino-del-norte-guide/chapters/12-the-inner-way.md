# The inner Way

Some people walk this road for faith, and some for sport. Some come after a loss, or before a decision. Some are curious, and some are tired of a screen and a chair. The Way asks none of them for a reason at the door. A path of five or six weeks that follows the sea is long enough for whatever you bring.

The first week is hard on the legs, and for a while the walking is the whole of the day. Later the road runs beside the sea, with long stretches in which the mind is free, and you may find that the thinking you meant to do on the last day starts on the fifth.

## Questions for the walk

Take one a day, or one a week, or none. Let them walk with you, not ahead of you.

- What did you bring that you do not need? Think of the pack, and then of the rest.
- What would you say to the person you were a year ago?
- Who are you walking for, and do they know?
- What do you notice here that you would hurry past at home?
- What would you like to still be doing in a year, and what can you begin today?
- Is there something you need to forgive, or to ask forgiveness for?

## Sea and silence

The sea is the companion of this road. On the days it is with you, it makes a daily rhythm: tide, wind and light. On the days it goes out of sight, as it does on the inland stretches of Bizkaia and Galicia, notice whether you miss it. The Way may be as much about what you leave behind as about where you arrive. You do not have to find that. You only have to walk.

## Alone and with others

Some of the best days are walked in silence, and some in company. The hostels and the long evenings make company easy. Allow yourself the silence too. The Way will not mind either.

## If you are walking for a faith

The Way is a Christian pilgrimage, and its churches and hospitals were built for pilgrims. Pray, or sit in a church, or light a candle, or only listen. If you walk for another reason, you are welcome too. The chapter on arriving says what the Pilgrim's Office writes about its certificates, in its own words.
