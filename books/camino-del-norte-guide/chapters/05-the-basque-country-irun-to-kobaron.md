# The Basque Country: Irun to Kobaron

## The character of these days

Eight days of hills, sea and old industry. The first three run along the Gipuzkoan coast, where the road climbs out of one river valley and drops into the next. Then it turns inland through the valleys of Bizkaia to Gernika and Bilbao, and ends along the old mining shore of the ría, at the last village before Cantabria.

The distances come from the Basque Government's tourism portal, whose eight stages add up to ~181 km. For the first day the Basque Government's brochure gives a slightly different figure, and the book says so. Official Basque pages give names in Basque and Spanish; this book uses the Spanish form and gives the Basque one where it differs. Many pilgrims split these days differently. This book follows the official stage towns.

### Day 1: Irun to San Sebastián (~24 km)

The road starts with a climb. From the Santiago bridge a cycle path follows the estuary into Irun. Just past the town hall the Coast Way and the Interior Way part, and you keep right. The road passes through Hondarribia's old town and climbs to the sanctuary and fort of Guadalupe, with views over the bay of Txingudi. The official way over Jaizkibel begins with a steep ascent and follows the ridge past towers from the second Carlist war; some pilgrims take a track round the mountain's side instead. Then comes the descent to Lezo and Pasai Donibane, and the first boat of the road, across the bay to Pasai San Pedro. The Basque brochure gives ~24.6 km, the portal ~23.8 km from Hondarribia. *Check the boat before you set out.*

### Day 2: San Sebastián to Zarautz (~23 km)

The road leaves the city past the Miramar palace and the Antiguo district and goes round the slopes of Igeldo, where the Basque heritage list for the stage names a megalithic site. It reaches the Oria at Orio, a small old town with the hermitage of San Martín. The portal says an interpretation centre for the Way, covering both the coast and the interior roads, stands in Orio, close to Zarautz. The day ends in Zarautz, with its old town and the Narros palace. It is a little shorter than the first day, and it still goes up and down.

### Day 3: Zarautz to Deba (~21 km)

Two ports and a climb. The road passes the hermitage and causeway of Santa Bárbara and reaches Getaria, whose old town and Monte San Antón are on the Basque list. Then comes Zumaia, with its hermitage of Santiago and its old streets, and the climb to Itziar, where the national tourism site points to the black Virgin, one of the most venerated statues of the area. From there the road drops to Deba. The same site singles out Zarautz and the ports of Getaria and Zumaia for their sea views.

### Day 4: Deba to Markina-Xemein (~22 km)

The road leaves the sea here. The Basque list for the stage names the old town of Deba and the Sasiola convent, the old town and port of Mutriku with the hamlet of Astigarribia, and the tower and ironworks of Antsotegi. The Basque Government says the landscape changes as you enter Bizkaia at Markina-Xemein: the broken Gipuzkoan coast gives way to the calm valleys of Lea-Artibai. Carry food and water for the day.

### Day 5: Markina-Xemein to Gernika (~25 km)

A day of valleys and small villages. The Basque list names Markina's fronton, the "university of pelota", the collegiate church of Cenarruza at Bolibar, and the towers and country churches of Mendata and Arratzu. The national tourism site calls the road through inland Bizkaia one of the most rugged and hardest on the Camino, with mountain paths and quiet forests. You arrive in Gernika-Lumo, home of the Casa de Juntas, the assembly house of Bizkaia. The portal gives ~24.8 km.

### Day 6: Gernika to Bilbao (~30 km)

The longest day in the Basque list. The road leaves Gernika by the Roman site of Forua and goes by Muxika, Larrabetzu, Lezama and Zamudio, where the list names towers, churches and an old town. The list ends with the dolmens of Irumugarrieta, the trenches and fort of Monte Avril, and the sanctuary of Begoña, above Bilbao. The portal gives ~29.7 km. If you want to cut the day, decide where before you leave Gernika, and ask the app. Bilbao is a big city, and a rest day here is a good use of a day.

### Day 7: Bilbao to Portugalete (~20 km)

A short official stage, along the left bank of the ría. You leave Bilbao's old town and pass Barakaldo and Sestao, where the heritage list turns industrial: an old furnace, an ore-loading bay. The day ends in Portugalete, with its old town and the convent of Santa Clara. The Vizcaya transporter bridge joins Portugalete to Getxo, and the national tourism site lists it as a World Heritage site. The portal gives ~19.6 km.

### Day 8: Portugalete to Kobaron (~17 km)

A day through the old mining shore. The Basque list for the stage names the miners' village at Trapagaran, an open mining pit at Abanto-Zierbena, and at Muskiz the towers, an old ironworks and the hermitage of Nuestra Señora del Socorro. The day ends at the mining complex of Pobeña and Kobaron, also written Covarón. This is where the Basque Country ends. The Basque Government says the way then opens towards Cantabria by Ontón. The portal gives ~16.8 km.
