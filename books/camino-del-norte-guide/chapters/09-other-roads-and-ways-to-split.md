# Other roads, and other ways to split the days

## Two ways into Galicia

At La Caridad the road divides. The days of this book take the way to Ribadeo, which the Xunta de Galicia lists first, at ~190 km from Ribadeo to Santiago.

The other way is the one the Asturias list follows. It turns inland by El Franco, Porcía and Brul to Vegadeo (A Veiga), ~25.6 km, then goes on to Abres, ~7.3 km, where it crosses the Eo. The Xunta calls it the road "through Santiago de Abres", ~184 km from there to Santiago. From Abres it runs by Trabada to Mondoñedo, ~31.2 km (~11.6 km to Trabada, ~19.6 km on, a section it rates high), and there it rejoins the main road. Counted to Mondoñedo, that is about 64 km by Vegadeo, against about 59 km by Ribadeo, which includes the unofficial ~22 km of Day 28. The Spanish Wikipedia says the Principado treats this inland way as the official one, and that it fell into disuse after the bridge of Los Santos opened in 1987. Ask locally which way is waymarked.

## The Interior Way

At the town hall of Irun the Coast Way and the Camino Vasco del Interior part. The Basque tourism portal describes the Interior Way in seven stages of ~250 km, beginning with ~26.2 km from Irun to Hernani. The Confraternity says it goes through the tunnel of San Adrián, from Hendaye to Santo Domingo de la Calzada on the Camino Francés. It is a different Camino, with its own stages, and it is not covered here. Another branch leaves the coast road at Bilbao: the Basque tourism portal lists a stage of ~34 km to Balmaseda, and the Basque Government says it follows the Kadagua river to meet the Camino Francés in the province of Burgos.

## The Lebaniego

At San Vicente de la Barquera the Camino Lebaniego begins, the way to the monastery of Santo Toribio de Liébana, where the relic of the Lignum Crucis, a piece of the Cross, is kept. The Cantabrian walking guide says it shares the coast road as far as Muñorrodero and then forks, and it gives three stages, ~28.5, ~31.3 and ~12.1 km. The Confraternity describes a tributary route onward to Mansilla de las Mulas on the Camino Francés. Sources differ on its length, from ~55 km to ~72 km, so check before you plan it.

## The links to the Primitivo

The two roads join the Camino Francés at different places: the Primitivo at Melide, the Norte at Arzúa. Between the coast and Oviedo, where the Primitivo begins, Asturias publishes two links.

The first leaves the coast at Casquita, near Villaviciosa, and climbs inland in two stages, ~21.6 km to La Pola Siero and ~17.1 km on, ~38.7 km in all. After San Pedru de Ambás there are two options, the official way or a detour to the monastery of Valdediós, and they meet again at the Alto de la Campa. The Confraternity says some pilgrims leave the coast at Villaviciosa for Valdediós and continue to Oviedo, where they join the Primitivo.

The second joins Avilés and Oviedo in one stage of ~28.8 km, by Cayés, Solís and Nubledo, through four councils. Asturias says many pilgrims, after visiting the Saviour in Oviedo cathedral, went north to Avilés, then the main Asturian seaport.

The Primitivo then runs ~320 km to Santiago, according to the app's catalog. *The Original Way* in this series covers it.

## A shortcut from Baamonde

The Xunta describes a shorter way from Baamonde, ~8 km shorter, straight to Sobrado. It meets the main road again near Boimil and leaves it, without passing Arzúa, by Santiso, and the Xunta says it joins the Camino Francés near Santiago's airport. Ask locally about its waymarks before you take it.

## Other ways to split the days

This book follows thirty-seven days, but the official lists differ, and many pilgrims split the road differently:

- **In the Basque Country** the Basque tourism portal has eight stages, from Hondarribia to Kobaron.
- **In Cantabria** the walking guide has nine stages, and the national tourism site recommends seven or eight.
- **In Asturias** the official list has thirteen stages, which Asturias says can be divided as often as you like.
- **In Galicia** the Xunta has eight stages from Ribadeo, and this book splits the long one at Miraz.

The longest days are in Cantabria (Days 10 to 13, ~35 to ~40 km), then Day 6 in the Basque Country (~30 km) and Day 29 in Galicia (~28 km). Boats and shorter alternatives shorten some of them. Where the beds are changes from season to season, so use the CaminoPlus app and ask locally.
