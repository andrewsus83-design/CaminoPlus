# Galicia: La Caridad to Santiago

## The character of these days

Ten days in which the sea is left behind. You cross the estuary of the Eo into Galicia at Ribadeo and turn inland through green farmland, small medieval towns and the wide plain of the Terra Chá. You reach the monastery of Sobrado dos Monxes and the Camino Francés at Arzúa, then take the last two days to Santiago.

From Ribadeo the distances come from the Xunta de Galicia, whose pages give ~190 km to Santiago by this road, rate it medium, and divide it into eight stages. The Xunta's one very long stage, from Baamonde to Sobrado, is split here at Miraz, using the Xunta's own sections. The Xunta also describes a second way into Galicia, which the next chapter covers. Galician names follow the Xunta's spelling.

### Day 28: La Caridad to Ribadeo (~22 km)

The Asturias list has no stage for this crossing, because it turns inland to Vegadeo. The pilgrim-information sites that publish it give ~21 to ~22 km, so take that as a guide and not a measurement. The road leaves Asturias at the estuary of the Eo. The Spanish Wikipedia says the inland way by Vegadeo was the road for those who did not want to cross the estuary by boat, and that it fell into disuse after the bridge of Los Santos opened in 1987. Ribadeo, the Xunta says, stands on a natural viewpoint above the ría, with a mix of medieval and modern architecture.

### Day 29: Ribadeo to Lourenzá (~28 km)

The Xunta rates this stage high, with an estimated duration of 6 hours 45 minutes, the only stage on this road that it rates high. The path follows an old route, perhaps Roman or late Roman, by Ove, Cubelas and Vilela, past the parish of San Pedro de Arante with its fifteenth-century chapel, and through Barreiros, Vilamartín and Gondán. Vilanova de Lourenzá grew round a Benedictine monastery founded in 969 by Count Osorio Gutiérrez, called the Holy Count. The town is known for its *fabas*, beans with a protected geographical indication, and holds a bean festival on the first Sunday of October. The Xunta puts the length at ~28.2 km.

### Day 30: Lourenzá to Abadín, by Mondoñedo (~22 km)

A steep climb and a descent on the medieval Camiño da Brea, a name from the Latin for the north wind, the Xunta says, meaning in Galician a strong wind with rain. Mondoñedo is a historic-artistic site and was the provincial capital until 1833; the Xunta mentions its cathedral, its almond cake and its angel's-hair jam. From there the road crosses Mount Pico and Mount Santa Cruz to Abadín. The Xunta gives ~21.9 km, and another way from Mondoñedo, by Lousada and Gontán, is ~17.2 km to Abadín.

### Day 31: Abadín to Vilalba (~21 km)

The Xunta says this stage is completely flat. It crosses the Terra Chá, the largest plain in Galicia, with A Limia, where wetlands, stone crosses and washing places mark the way. A medieval bridge over the Arnela and another, with three arches, at Pontevella lead to Martiñán. In 2003 the Terra Chá and the upper Miño were declared a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve. Vilalba, with its fifteenth-century Andrade tower, is known for San Simón cheese and free-range capons. The Xunta gives ~20.6 km and about five hours.

### Day 32: Vilalba to Baamonde (~19 km)

The Xunta says the paths may date from the Middle Ages and the royal roads from the seventeenth century. The road passes Penas Corveiras and crosses the medieval Saa bridge, built of thick slate slabs. It then runs on a strip of land between the A-8 motorway and the Ladra river, where, the Xunta says, you can take a dip in good weather. Baamonde has a fourteenth-century Gothic church of Santiago and a calvary of three stone crosses beside an old chestnut tree with a chapel to the Virgin carved in its trunk. The Xunta gives ~19.0 km.

### Day 33: Baamonde to Miraz (~15 km)

The Xunta warns of an especially long stage, ~40 km to Sobrado, rated medium-low at over seven hours. This book splits it at Miraz, using the Xunta's sections of ~7.7 km and ~7.2 km. The road passes the chapel of Santo Alberte, where a popular pilgrimage is held every May, and goes on by Digañe and San Paio de Seixón to Santiago de Miraz. The fortress of San Paio de Narla is a short detour. The Confraternity says the high point of the whole route, ~710 m, comes shortly after Miraz.

### Day 34: Miraz to Sobrado dos Monxes (~25 km)

The road runs through small villages, by A Roxica, to O Marco das Pías, where you leave the province of Lugo for A Coruña, and on to Guitizá and its artificial lake. Sobrado, the Xunta says, has a Cistercian monastery of Santa María, founded in 952 as a mixed monastery for men and women, with Renaissance and Baroque parts and a medieval kitchen and chapter house. It was the first in Spain to join the Cistercian order, and it still welcomes pilgrims. The Xunta's sections add up to ~25 km.

### Day 35: Sobrado dos Monxes to Arzúa (~22 km)

The Xunta says this day shows Galicia's lush landscape and its country architecture. The road goes by Vilarchao and Carelle, As Corredoiras and Boimorto, and Sendelle, with its twelfth-century Romanesque church. At Arzúa the Norte meets the Camino Francés, and the Primitivo, which joined the Francés at Melide, is on the same road. Arzúa is known for its Arzúa-Ulloa cheese, which has a designation of origin, and its street the Rúa do Camiño recalls the old Camiño de Oviedo. The Xunta gives ~22.2 km.

### Day 36: Arzúa to Arca (~18 km)

A gentle day between meadows, oaks and eucalyptus, rated low. The Xunta warns that the road crosses the N-547 several times, and asks you to take special care of the traffic. Hamlets such as A Calzada and Ferreiros have names with Jacobean echoes. The day ends at Arca, also called O Pedrouzo, in the council of O Pino, the last before Santiago. The Xunta gives ~18.4 km.

### Day 37: Arca to Santiago (~20 km)

The last day. The road passes Lavacolla, near the airport, where, the Xunta says, pilgrims once washed in the stream, and climbs to Monte do Gozo, ~380 m, where pilgrims first saw the cathedral. By the Xunta's account the first to reach the summit was proclaimed king of the pilgrimage. Then the San Lázaro quarter, the Porta do Camiño and the pedestrian streets lead to the cathedral. The Xunta gives ~20.2 km and about five hours.
