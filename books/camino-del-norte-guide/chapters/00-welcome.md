# Welcome

The Camino del Norte is the coast road to Santiago. It starts at Irun, on the French border, and follows the Bay of Biscay west through the Basque Country, Cantabria and Asturias. In Galicia it turns inland, meets the Camino Francés at Arzúa, and walks the last stretch to the cathedral of Santiago de Compostela. The app's catalog gives it ~820 km. It is long, green and often wet, and its hills come one after another.

**The promise of this book is simple.** By the last page you will know whether this long road is your Camino, how its thirty-seven walking days fall region by region, and what to check before you leave home.

## How to use it

If you are a month or more away, read the next four chapters in order: the Way in brief, whether it suits you, when to go, and what to do before you go. Then read the days, one chapter for each region, and the chapter on other roads, which covers the choices this Camino offers: the two ways into Galicia, the links to the Camino Primitivo, and the roads that branch from this one.

If you are on the road tonight, go straight to your day. Each one says what the walking is like, what is worth a pause, and where to take care.

If you are only dreaming, start with "Is this Camino for you?" and finish with "The inner Way".

## What this book is not

It is not a live map, a timetable or a booking service. It names no hostels, cafes or shops, and it gives no prices, opening hours or bed counts. They change from season to season, and a wrong one costs a tired person dearly. For those, use the CaminoPlus app and ask locally.

This book was written with AI assistance, from public sources, and its author has not walked the road. So it gives you sources instead of memories, and it says plainly when the sources disagree.

On this road they disagree more than most. No single body publishes the whole Camino del Norte: the Basque Government, Cantabria, Asturias and the Xunta de Galicia each publish their own part, in their own stages and their own style. Every distance here carries a tilde, **~**, because it is approximate, and the book names the source of each set of figures. Where something depends on the season (a boat, a closed path, a river after rain, a hostel that opens only in summer), it says *check before you go*, and means it.

## A few words you will meet

*Buen Camino*, "good Way", is what pilgrims and locals say to one another as you pass. An *albergue* is a pilgrims' hostel. The *credencial* is your pilgrim's passport, a folded card that you carry and have stamped along the way. Each stamp is a *sello*. The *Compostela* is the certificate the Pilgrim's Office in Santiago gives to those who meet its rules, which the chapter on arriving sets out. A *ría* is a long sea inlet where a river meets the ocean; this road crosses many.

The road crosses four regions with their own languages. Official pages give Basque and Spanish names in the Basque Country, Spanish and Asturian names in Asturias, and Galician names in Galicia. This book uses the Spanish forms in the Basque Country, Cantabria and Asturias, and the Galician forms of the Xunta de Galicia in Galicia, such as Lourenzá and Sobrado dos Monxes.

Carry what you need, walk gently, and take your time over the long hill. *Buen Camino.*
