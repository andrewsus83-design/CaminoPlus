# With the app, and sources

## With the CaminoPlus app

**CaminoPlus** is the offline-first companion app for the Camino de Santiago, with maps that work without signal, stays and cafes, a pilgrim passport with stamps, a journal and friends. The paid plan is called **Pilgrim Plus**. The app is still in development. The Camino del Norte is on its list of routes, but this book makes no claim about what the app holds for it today, so look in the app, and use it, not this book, for beds, cafes, boats, closures and weather.

A reminder about the passport: the Pilgrim's Office asks for the stamped credencial. A passport in any app is a keepsake until the Office says otherwise.

## Sources and thanks

This book rests on public sources, and its fact ledger lists every fact against the page it came from. The chief ones are:

- **The Basque Government**, on its tourism and heritage pages: the stages of the Camino de la Costa in the Basque Country and their lengths, the brochure for the first day, and the heritage list by stage.
- **Cantabria**: the government agreement of 15 October 2015 that defines the Camino de Santiago de la costa through Cantabria, published in the official state gazette, and the walking guide "Caminando por el Patrimonio", for the stages and their lengths.
- **The Principado de Asturias tourism pages** (turismoasturias.es), and its official Camino brochure: the Camino de la Costa and its thirteen stages, the two links to the Camino Primitivo, and the practical information for pilgrims.
- **The Xunta de Galicia**, on its official Camino de Santiago portal: the Northern Way, its stages and sections, and the stages Arzúa to Arca and Arca to Santiago.
- **The Pilgrim's Reception Office of the Cathedral of Santiago**: the pages on the Compostela, the credencial and the Certificate of Distance.
- **The Confraternity of St James**: its page on the Camino del Norte.
- **Spain's national tourism site** (spain.info): the page on the Northern Way.
- **Wikipedia**, for climate tables, the 2024 share of the route, and history: the articles on the Camino de Santiago, Irun, San Sebastián, Santander, Santiago de Compostela, its cathedral, the Jacobean Holy Year, the UNESCO listing, and the Spanish article on the Camino de Santiago de la Costa.
- **A standard solar calculation**, for the hours of daylight.
- **The CaminoPlus route catalog**, for the route's start, end, ~820 km and difficulty rating.

Published figures differ between these sources, so every distance in this book is marked ~. Where the sources say nothing, the book says nothing, and tells you to check. If you know a better figure, or a fact that has changed, tell CaminoPlus.

Thanks go to the people who keep the Way: the associations of friends of the Camino, the hosts who open the hostels, and the people in the villages and towns who point the way and say *Buen Camino* to a stranger.

## A short glossary

- **Albergue**: a pilgrims' hostel with shared rooms.
- **Alarde**: a Basque parade that descends from an old muster of arms.
- **Botafumeiro**: the great censer of Santiago cathedral.
- **Buen Camino**: "good Way", the greeting between pilgrims.
- **Cabildo**: the governing council of the cathedral.
- **Compostela**: the Latin certificate the Pilgrim's Office issues.
- **Credencial**: the pilgrim's passport.
- **Hospitalero**: the host of a pilgrims' hostel.
- **Indiano**: an emigrant who made a fortune abroad and came home to build.
- **Menú del peregrino**: a set meal for pilgrims.
- **Pintxo**: a small Basque bite eaten at the bar.
- **Ría**: a long sea inlet where a river meets the ocean.
- **Sello**: a stamp in the credencial.
