# Asturias: Bustio to La Caridad

## The character of these days

Eleven days between the sea and the mountains. The Principado de Asturias says the road enters by the ría of Tina Mayor and leaves by the ría of the Eo, with more than ~280 km across twenty-one councils. In the east the mountains come close to the water. In the middle lies a heavily industrialised Asturias, between Gijón and Avilés. In the west wide coastal terraces open out, with blue mountain ranges on the horizon.

The distances come from the official Asturias list, which has thirteen stages from Bustio, in the council of Ribadedeva, to the Eo. Asturias says the list can be stretched or shortened as you like, because there are enough hostels and places to stay. Some of its stages end in hamlets, such as Priesca, Casquita, Villamouros and Outur, so this book names each day by its stage towns and the town it passes. The last two Asturian stages, which turn inland to Vegadeo, appear in the chapter on other roads. Official Asturias pages give Spanish and Asturian names; this book uses the Spanish ones.

### Day 17: Unquera (Bustio) to Llanes (~25 km)

The Asturian list begins at Bustio, across the Deva. The road passes Colombres, rich in the houses of the *indianos*, emigrants who made their fortune abroad and came home to build. Then come Buelna and Pendueles, the *bufones* of Arenillas (vertical blowholes that spout sprayed sea on high-tide days), Purón and Andrín. Llanes has its basilica of Santa María, a medieval tower, and the "Cubos de la Memoria" painted by Agustín Ibarrola. Keep well back from cliff edges when the sea is rough. The list gives ~24.7 km.

### Day 18: Llanes to Ribadesella (~30 km)

The road runs through Po, Celoriu, Barru and Niembru. Asturias says the view of the church of Nuestra Señora de los Dolores, rising above the marsh between Barru and Niembru, is probably one of the most singular pictures of eastern Asturias. At Naves stands San Antolín de Bedón, one of its best rural Romanesque churches. Then come Villahormes, Piñeres and Cuerres. Ribadesella has the cave of Tito Bustillo, which Asturias calls one of the great Palaeolithic sanctuaries of northern Spain. The list gives ~29.8 km.

### Day 19: Ribadesella to Priesca, by Colunga (~29 km)

A long day on the road to the pre-Romanesque church of San Salvador at Priesca. On the way are San Esteban, La Vega, Berbes, Arenal de Morís, La Isla, Colunga and Pernús. Asturias says the building zeal of its early kings reached this country, and that the surprising church at Priesca is their legacy; it also points to the Jurassic Museum near Colunga. The list gives ~28.6 km. If it is too long, break it at Colunga.

### Day 20: Priesca to Casquita, by Villaviciosa (~13 km)

A short stage. The road goes by Sebrayu to Villaviciosa, where Asturias singles out the light Romanesque church of Santa María de la Oliva and the house where Charles V spent his first night in the Peninsula. At Amandi, close by, a Romanesque church has an unusual apse. Casquita is a hamlet near Villaviciosa, and it is where the link to Oviedo and the Camino Primitivo leaves the coast. The list gives ~13.0 km.

### Day 21: Casquita to Gijón (~26 km)

The road goes by Niévares, the Alto de la Cruz, Pion, El Curviellu and Cabueñes to Gijón. Asturias says the city centre, with its beaches of San Lorenzo and Poniente, the Cimavilla quarter and the hill of Santa Catalina, is like the figurehead of a ship facing the horizon. The list gives ~25.6 km. Gijón is a big city, and a good place for a rest day.

### Day 22: Gijón to Avilés (~26 km)

The central industrial strip. Asturias says the silhouette of the Universidad Laboral announces it; the official text calls it the largest building in Spain. The road crosses Monte Areo, where Asturias recommends taking time over a burial-mound necropolis, and goes by El Valle, Tamón and Trasona. Avilés surprises, Asturias says, with its old quarter, with the churches of San Francisco and San Nicolás de Bari, the palace of Camposagrado and Galiana street, and with the modern lines of the Niemeyer Centre. The list gives ~26.2 km.

### Day 23: Avilés to Muros de Nalón (~21 km)

The way leaves the industrial area by San Cristoba, La Plata, Piedrasblancas, La Lloba, El Monte and Ranón. Crossing the Nalón at Sotu, Asturias says, marks the entry into the western strip, and you reach Muros de Nalón. The list gives ~21.2 km.

### Day 24: Muros de Nalón to Soto de Luiña (~15 km)

A short stage. The way goes by El Pito, where the Selgas estate stands, then Rellayo and the beach of the Concha de Artedo, which Asturias calls beautiful. It ends at Soto de Luiña, with its elegant Baroque church of Santa María. Asturias adds that the road does not pass through Cudillero, but close to it. The list gives ~15.2 km.

### Day 25: Soto de Luiña to Villamouros (~20 km)

The road goes by Las Chabolas, Montagudu, Silvascura, Busmarzu and San Playu de Teona. Along this western stretch Asturias points to the quiet of Cabo Busto and to Cadavéu, where the house of Padre Galo still stands, and where, it says, modern Asturian literature began. The list gives ~19.9 km.

### Day 26: Villamouros to Outur, by Luarca (~20 km)

The way goes by San Cristuébanu, Queirúas, Caneiru, Caroyas, Barcia and Almuña to Luarca. Asturias calls Luarca one of the most rewarding visits of the stretch, above all for its marine cemetery, with unmatched views over the town and the Cantabrian Sea. Outur lies just beyond. The list gives ~19.7 km.

### Day 27: Outur to La Caridad, by Navia (~25 km)

The road goes by Villapedre, Piñera, Villaoril and La Colorada to Navia, where the poet Ramón de Campoamor was born. Then come Jarrio and Cartavio, and La Caridad (A Caridá in Asturian), in the council of El Franco. The list gives ~25.1 km. At La Caridad the road divides, and the chapter on other roads says how.
