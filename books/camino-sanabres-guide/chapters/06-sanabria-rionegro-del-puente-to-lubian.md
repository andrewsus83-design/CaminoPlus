# Sanabria: Rionegro del Puente to Lubián

## The character of these days

Four days take you up into the mountains. The Junta's table puts Rionegro del Puente at ~810 m, Puebla de Sanabria at ~960 m and the village of Padornelo at ~1,260 m. Holm oak gives way to oak, chestnut and heather, and the houses turn to granite and slate. The villages are small, some of them tiny, and between them run the national road N-525 and the Rías Baixas motorway, which the path crosses several times. The sources are the same as before: the Junta's guide and the Zamora board's leaflet, from 2010 and 2011.

### Day 4: Rionegro del Puente to San Salvador de Palazuelo (~19 km)

This is a day of small places. Mombuey comes first, ~8.5 km out. The Junta says the Templars held the church there, and that its slender Romanesque tower of the thirteenth century has a defensive look the Order is credited with. It calls the tower a landmark for pilgrims.

Beyond lies Valdemerilla, with twelve inhabitants, and an inscription that the chapter on the inner Way quotes. Then come Cernadilla and its reservoir, and San Salvador de Palazuelo, a village strung out along the road in the old Jacobean way. The Zamora board praises the granite markers carved with scallop shells that the council of Cernadilla set up. Both sources give ~19 km. These places are small, so check that the village you want for the night can house you.

### Day 5: San Salvador de Palazuelo to Puebla de Sanabria (~22 km)

The day passes Entrepeñas, Asturianos, Palacios de Sanabria, Remesal, Otero de Sanabria and Triufé. At Entrepeñas the Junta says chestnut trees begin to predominate, and the villages hold a *magosto*, a feast of chestnuts. At Asturianos it singles out two walnut trees centuries old. At Remesal a hermitage dated 1506 recalls, the Junta says, the meeting of Ferdinand the Catholic and Philip the Handsome. At Otero de Sanabria stands the sanctuary of Our Lady of the Remedies, patron of Sanabria.

Puebla de Sanabria is the most important place on the road, the Junta says. The town stands on a hill between the Tera and the Castro, and is declared a historic ensemble. It has a granite castle of the Counts of Benavente from the middle of the fifteenth century, walls of the twelfth and thirteenth, and the Romanesque church of Santa María del Azogue. The Junta says Puebla once had its own hospital for pilgrims. Both sources give ~22 km, and the Zamora board calls it a marathon day.

### Day 6: Puebla de Sanabria to Requejo (~12 km)

The shortest of the mountain days, and a good one to take slowly. The Zamora board gave two ways out of Puebla: along the River Castro, or by Lobeznos and Pedralba de la Pradería. Both reach Terroso, which the Junta calls a Jacobean place above all others. Its church of Santiago has scallop shells carved on its door nineteen times, and a text beside the church says that welcome is Terroso's history.

Requejo stands at the foot of the Padornelo pass. Near it, the Junta says, runs the trail of El Tejedelo, more than five kilometres long, through a mixed forest of 139 hectares with one of the most important populations of yew trees in the Iberian Peninsula. Near Puebla lies the glacial lake of Sanabria, a natural park since 1978, which the Junta says is one of the few glacial lakes of the Iberian Peninsula. It is off the Way, so keep it for a rest day. The Junta's table gives ~12 km and the Zamora board ~12.5 km.

### Day 7: Requejo to Lubián (~19 km)

The Padornelo pass is one of the high points of the road: the village of Padornelo stands at ~1,260 m by the Junta's table, and the Xunta puts the next pass, Portela da Canda, at ~1,262 m. The Junta's entry for Padornelo mentions a stone cross with inscriptions at the top of the Portilla pass, where the border between the kingdoms of Galicia and Castile once ran. The Zamora board gave two ways up, a path and the old national road, and the path may not be as it was in 2010. Ask in Requejo, and cross the pass only in good weather.

After the pass you come down by Padornelo and Aciberos to Lubián, ~1,024 m, the last village of the Camino Sanabrés in Castilla y León. The Junta says that here you meet the first of more than a hundred sculpted stones, each one different, made by the Ourense sculptor Carballo, which stand along the road in the province of Ourense. Three kilometres from the village it lists the Celtic hillfort of As Muradellas, from about the third century BC.

The Junta's table gives ~19 km and the Zamora board ~23 km.
