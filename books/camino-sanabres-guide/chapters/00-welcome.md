# Welcome

The Camino Sanabrés is a branch road. It leaves the Vía de la Plata at Granja de Moreruela, in the province of Zamora, turns west into the Sanabria mountains, crosses into Galicia above A Gudiña, passes through the thermal city of Ourense and comes into Santiago de Compostela over the River Sar. It is ~370 km in all, this book walks it in sixteen days, and it is a road that asks you to plan.

**The promise of this book is simple.** By the last page you will know whether this quiet road is your Camino, what each of its sixteen walking days asks of you, and what to check before you leave home.

## How to use it

If you are a month or more away, read the next four chapters in order: the Way in brief, whether it suits you, when to go, and what to do before you go. Then read the days by region, and the chapter on the long days, which shows where they can be split. If you are on the road tonight, go straight to your stage. If you are only dreaming, start with "Is this Camino for you?" and finish with "The inner Way".

If you are walking the Vía de la Plata from Seville, this book is your last stretch. It begins where the two roads part.

## What this book is not

It is not a live map, a timetable or a booking service. It names no hostels, cafes or shops, and it gives no prices, opening hours or bed counts. They change from season to season, and a wrong one costs a tired person dearly, more so on a road with few pilgrims and long gaps between villages. For those answers, use the CaminoPlus app and ask locally.

This book was written with AI assistance, from public sources, and its author has not walked the road. So it gives you sources instead of memories, and it says plainly when the sources disagree.

Every distance here carries a tilde, **~**, because it is approximate. Where something depends on the season (snow, fog, heat, a flooded path), the book says *check before you go*, and means it.

## A few words you will meet

*Buen Camino*, "good Way", is what pilgrims and locals say to one another as you pass. An *albergue* is a pilgrims' hostel. The *credencial* is your pilgrim's passport, a folded card that you carry and have stamped along the way. Each stamp is a *sello*. The *Compostela* is the certificate the Pilgrim's Office in Santiago gives to those who meet its rules, which the chapter on arriving sets out. The *Xunta* is the regional government of Galicia, and the *Junta* that of Castilla y León, the region of Zamora. Both publish guides that this book uses.

Place names follow the official pages: Spanish in Zamora, Galician from the border (A Gudiña, Laza, Ourense). Older Spanish sources write Orense, La Gudiña and Vilar del Barrio for the same places.

Carry what you need, plan your nights ahead, and take your time over the high pass. *Buen Camino.*
