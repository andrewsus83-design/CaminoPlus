# Is this Camino for you?

## How hard is it?

The app's catalog rates the Camino Sanabrés 3 on a scale of 1 to 5, the same as the Camino Francés and one below the Camino Primitivo. The app's own note for the road says services are thin, so confirm lodging.

The Xunta rates the road in Galicia Medium. It gives its first stage, Lubián to A Gudiña, and its last, into Santiago, a Low, and the seven between a Medium. It marks two sections of the stage from A Gudiña to Laza as High. This book's sixteen days average ~23 km, but three of them are ~34 km or more.

The sources disagree about services. The Junta's guide, in 2010, says the road has enough hostels and services to be entirely workable. The Confraternity of St James, writing about the Vía de la Plata and its Sanabrés branch, says that road is hard because of the lack, or unreliability, of stops for rest and food, and asks for extra care in planning the next day's stage. Both can be true on one road. Plan, then confirm.

## Who it suits

- Walkers who want quiet, and a long view with few people in it.
- Walkers who like to plan: your nights first, then your days.
- Walkers who are fit for a ~30 km day, or who will use the split points in the chapter on the long days.
- People who enjoy changing country: a plain, a river valley, a pass at ~1,260 m, a city of hot springs, and the green hills of the Deza.
- Those who have walked the Vía de la Plata from Seville and are turning towards Santiago.

## Who might choose another

- A first Camino walker who wants company and a bar in every village may be happier on a busier road. The Confraternity says the road is gaining in popularity as an alternative to the busier Caminos, and that you can expect solitude if you want it, with plenty of company where you sleep.
- If sixteen days is too many, you can walk only the end. The last ~107 km begin at Ourense, and the chapter on arriving looks at what that means for the Compostela.
- If you dislike road walking, know that the Way runs beside the national road N-525 and crosses the motorway more than once.
- If you cannot walk in heat, avoid July and August on the Zamora plain.

## How it compares

It is longer than the Camino de Invierno, which is ~260 km and joins this road at A Laxe, ~50 km from Santiago, and far shorter than the Vía de la Plata, which starts in Seville. Wikipedia, citing the Pilgrim's Office, says that of the compostelas granted in 2013 the Vía de la Plata had 4.2 percent and the Camino Francés 70.3 percent. That is an old figure for the whole of the Plata, not this branch, but it shows the scale. The Camino Francés and the Camino de Invierno have their own books in this series.

## Language

Spanish will serve you in Zamora and most of Galicia. The Xunta's pages write place names in Galician, so expect *rúa*, *ponte* and *camiño*, "street", "bridge" and "way".
