# When to go

## The short answer

The app's note for the road says the best time is April to June, and September. The Confraternity of St James, writing about the Vía de la Plata and its Sanabrés branch, points the same way: set off between the early spring rains and well before the heat of early summer, or come in autumn once the summer heat has gone. Summer temperatures, it says, can pass 40 degrees and should definitely be avoided.

These are patterns, not forecasts. For the week you walk, read the warnings of AEMET, the Spanish state weather agency.

## Two climates on one road

The road starts on a high plain and ends in the wet north-west, so the weather changes with the days.

Zamora, on the plain at ~650 m, stands in for the first part. By Wikipedia's table, which draws on AEMET's records for 1991 to 2022, the mean daily high is ~9 °C in January, ~18 °C in April, ~22 °C in May, ~28 °C in June, ~31 °C in July and ~26 °C in September. The record is ~41.8 °C, set on 14 July 2022. Nights in January average ~1 °C, and Wikipedia says fog is common in winter. Rain is light: ~12 mm in July, ~55 mm in October.

Ourense, on the Miño, is hotter in summer and wetter in winter. The mean daily high is ~31 °C in July and August, and Wikipedia's table gives a record of ~44 °C in July. October rain is ~119 mm. Santiago, at the end, is wetter again, with ~214 mm in October and ~196 mm in January, and only ~31 mm in July.

Between the two lies the mountain. The road climbs to ~1,260 m at Padornelo, and the Xunta describes stretches above a thousand metres in the Serra Seca. The Junta's guide says that even in summer, in the last villages of Castilla y León and across Galicia, you can expect a fall in temperature, fog and the risk of rain in any season.

## Month by month

- **April and May.** Cool mornings on the plain, wetter in Galicia. About 13 hours of daylight in mid-April. The Confraternity likes this season for the flowers and the long days.
- **June.** Warm on the plain, ~28 °C at Zamora, with about 15 hours of light at midsummer. Start early.
- **July and August.** The hot months. Walk at first light, plan to end by early afternoon, and think twice about the Zamora days.
- **September.** Still warm, ~26 °C at Zamora and ~28 °C at Ourense, with the rain coming back to Galicia.
- **October.** Mild and wetter. About 11 hours of daylight in mid-October. Check the sunset time for the week you walk.
- **November to March.** Short days, about 9 hours of light at midwinter, with fog on the plain and, the Xunta says, frequent snow in the hardest months on the high ground, even at ~736 m on the road to Bendoiro. Check what is open before you plan a night, and keep a way to stop early.

## Daylight

These daylight figures are this book's own calculation from the sun's position at Puebla de Sanabria and Ourense, and the two places agree: about 9 hours at the December solstice, with sunrise close to nine in the morning, and about 15 at the June solstice. The Xunta advises walking only in daylight, not before dawn or after sunset.

## The Holy Year

2027 is a Jacobean Holy Year, because 25 July falls on a Sunday. The Holy Door of the cathedral is opened only in a Holy Year. You may find Santiago busier, so ask early about the way home.
