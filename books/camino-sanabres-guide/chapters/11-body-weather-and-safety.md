# Body, weather and safety

This chapter is plain on purpose. On a road with few people and long gaps, a small mistake costs more than it does on a busy one.

## Heat

The Zamora plain and the city of Ourense are the hot parts of the road. The mean daily high is ~31 °C in July at both, and Wikipedia's tables give records of ~42 °C at Zamora and ~44 °C at Ourense. The Confraternity of St James says summer temperatures on the Vía de la Plata can pass 40 degrees and should be avoided.

- Walk at first light, and plan to finish by early afternoon.
- Carry water for the whole stage. The Xunta asks for about two litres a day, and in heat you will need more.
- Wear a hat and sun cream, and rest in the shade in the hottest hours.
- If you feel faint, dizzy or confused, stop in the shade, drink, and get help. Call 112 if it does not pass.

## Cold, fog and the high ground

The road climbs to ~1,260 m at Padornelo, and the Xunta puts the pass of Portela da Canda at ~1,262 m. It describes the Serra Seca as landscape above a thousand metres, and says the Alto de Santo Domingo, at only ~736 m, has frequent snow in the hardest months. The Junta's guide says that in the last villages of Castilla y León and across Galicia the temperature can fall, with fog and the risk of rain, in any season.

- Read the AEMET warnings for the area the evening before, and again at breakfast.
- Carry a warm layer and a waterproof even in summer.
- Do not cross a high pass in poor visibility. Choose where you will stop before you set out, for example Requejo before the Padornelo pass, or Campobecerros on the Serra Seca.
- In winter, ask locally about snow and ice before you commit to a mountain day.

## Rain and water on the path

Galicia is wet. By Wikipedia's tables Ourense receives ~108 to ~119 mm a month from October to December, and Santiago ~184 to ~214 mm. The Xunta says that between Vilar de Barrio and Ourense some stretches of the Verea de Santiago are sometimes flooded in winter and spring. The Confraternity warns of high water and muddy tracks after heavy rain on the Vía de la Plata. *Check before you go*, and after heavy rain ask where you slept.

## Roads and traffic

The Way runs beside the national road N-525 and crosses the A-52 motorway. Where there is no verge, stay where drivers can see you and you can see them, and keep your ears free of earphones. The Xunta advises against walking in the dark, before dawn or after sunset.

## Being alone

The quiet that the sources praise in this road is also its risk. The Xunta describes stretches of half-abandoned villages, and a way through woodland that can be difficult, taken in complete solitude.

- Tell your host where you are going, and when you expect to arrive.
- Carry food and water for the whole stage, and a charged phone, but do not count on a signal in the mountains.
- Ring ahead if you will be late.

## Feet and body

The Xunta says that knees, tendons and feet are the weak points, and that sprains and tendinitis are the commonest injuries. Wear boots you have already walked in, change your socks, wash your feet at the end of the day, and look at them. Treat a hot spot before it becomes a blister. Eat light, with plenty of carbohydrate, and drink before, during and after the walk.

## When to stop

Stopping early is a plan, not a failure. If you are hurt, ill or lost, or the weather turns, stop. Anything that worries you is a reason to see a doctor, and this book gives no medical advice beyond that. The European emergency number is 112. The Xunta says it is free, works every hour of every day, and answers any urgent call. The Xunta's portal also offers a guide to emergencies for pilgrims as a PDF.
