# Galicia: Lubián to Ourense

## The character of these days

Four days take you over the border and across the high country of Ourense to the city of hot springs. From here the figures are the Xunta's, from its stage pages for the Vía de la Plata by Laza, which this book follows in the Spanish originals, because the Xunta's English pages can be machine-translated. The Xunta rates the days Low, Medium, Medium and Medium. The Junta's table, from 2011, differs by a kilometre or two, and by ~3 km on the first day. Each day says so.

The Xunta says the road enters Galicia at the pass of Portela da Canda, ~1,262 m, in the municipality of A Mezquita. It calls A Mezquita a historic meeting point of the people of Castile, Galicia and Portugal, a land crossed by pilgrims, but also by reapers, muleteers, dealers and travellers.

### Day 8: Lubián to A Gudiña (~27 km)

The Xunta's page for this stage is titled Lubián to A Gudiña, and its description begins at the pass. It rates the stage Low, though all three of its sections are Medium-High. From A Canda you reach A Vilavella, whose church is dedicated to Santa María de la Cabeza, and then go down by a bridge over a stream and old paved ways between centuries-old stone walls, to O Pereiro. The Xunta calls this a land of great stonemasons. Then you come into O Canizo, ~1,067 m, beside the N-525, and on to A Gudiña.

A Gudiña, the Xunta says, was famous from the eighteenth century for its traffic and its inns for pilgrims and travellers. On 15 July 1506, it adds, Queen Joanna and her husband Philip the Handsome stopped here, the same year, by the Junta's account, that Philip met Ferdinand at Remesal. The Xunta gives ~27 km, the Junta's table ~24 km and the Zamora board ~23 km. Start early.

### Day 9: A Gudiña to Laza (~35 km)

This is the hardest day of the road and the first to plan with care. The Xunta calls the way the Verea Vella, or Verea Sur, and says that in the sixteenth century it was called the Camino Francés and the Camino Real. It crosses the Serra Seca, landscape above a thousand metres, where the Alto de Picaños is ~1,104 m. You pass A Venda do Espiño, A Venda da Teresa, A Venda da Capela and A Venda do Bolaño. A *venda* is an inn, and the Xunta describes this as land of half-abandoned villages. The reservoir of As Portas lies to the right, and the natural park of O Invernadeiro to the north.

The Xunta rates the day Medium, and its first two sections, from A Gudiña to Campobecerros (~21 km) and from there to Porto Camba (~3 km), High. After Portocamba you go through a mountain valley of the Serra de San Mamede, by As Eiras, into Laza, which the Xunta and the Junta both say is famous for its carnival.

Campobecerros, ~21 km in and ~15 km short of Laza, is the natural place to split the day, but ask whether you can sleep there. The Xunta gives ~35.5 km and the Junta ~34.4 km. Carry food and water for the whole day. Do not trust a village on the map.

### Day 10: Laza to Vilar de Barrio (~19 km)

A shorter day after a long one, and it climbs. The Xunta says you go up the valley of the River Támega by Soutelo Verde and Tamicelas, then climb steeply to A Alberguería, where there was an inn for travellers and a charity hospital for pilgrims. Beyond lies high heathland, short of trees, with the colours of heather, and a sharp climb below the Alto de Paradiña and Monte Talariño.

At Sarreaus, the Xunta says, the River Limia rises, the river the Greeks called Lethe, the river of forgetfulness, which the Roman general Decimus Junius Brutus crossed. Vilar de Barrio is the head of a municipality in A Limia, a broad district at the centre of the province of Ourense. The Xunta gives ~19 km and the Junta ~20 km.

### Day 11: Vilar de Barrio to Ourense (~35 km)

Another long day, on a wide, comfortable way. The Xunta says that from Vilar de Gomareite it is called the Verea de Santiago, with stone paving and stretches that are sometimes flooded in winter and spring. *Check before you go* after heavy rain.

You pass Bobadela, Cima de Vila and Padroso, and reach Xunqueira de Ambía, ~14 km in and ~21 km short of Ourense, which the Xunta calls an important spiritual centre in the Middle Ages. Soon after you cross the Roman road XVIII, the Via Nova from Braga to Astorga, where Roman milestones still stand. At Os Casares many pilgrims once turned aside to the sanctuary of Santa Mariña de Augasantas, the Xunta says. The way goes on by Paderne de Allariz, Taboadela and San Cibrao das Viñas to Ourense.

The Xunta calls Ourense a capital of thermal baths, with a famous Roman bridge and a great Romanesque cathedral. Wikipedia places the hot springs called the Burgas in the old town, and the Junta notes that Ourense is the only provincial capital in Galicia without a coast. It deserves a rest day. The Xunta gives ~35 km and the Junta ~35 km.
