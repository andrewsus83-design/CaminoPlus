# With the app, and sources

## With the CaminoPlus app

**CaminoPlus** is the offline-first companion app for the Camino de Santiago, with maps that work without signal, stays and cafes, a pilgrim passport with stamps, a journal and friends. The paid plan is called **Pilgrim Plus**. The app is still in development. The Camino Sanabrés is on its list of routes, from Granja de Moreruela to Santiago de Compostela, and the app gives it no distance and no number of stages. This book makes no claim about what the app holds for it today, so look in the app, and use it, not this book, for beds, cafes, closures and weather.

## Sources and thanks

This book rests on public sources, and its fact ledger lists every fact against the page it came from. The chief ones are:

- **The Xunta de Galicia**, on its official Camino de Santiago portal (caminodesantiago.gal): the page on the Vía de la Plata or Camino Mozárabe, in English and Spanish, and the stage pages for the way by Laza and the way by Verín in Spanish, with their sections, lengths, ratings and descriptions; and the portal's pages on advice for pilgrims, on physical and mental preparation and on the public network of hostels. The Xunta's page on the Camino de Invierno confirms where that road joins.
- **The Junta de Castilla y León**, on its tourism site (turismocastillayleon.com): the chapter on the Camino Mozárabe-Sanabrés in its guide to the historic Caminos, in an edition of 2010 with data updated in February 2011.
- **The Diputación de Zamora**, Patronato de Turismo: its leaflet on the Camino Sanabrés, of March 2010.
- **The Confraternity of St James** (csj.org.uk): its page on the Vía de la Plata, which takes in the Camino Sanabrés, updated in August 2025.
- **The Pilgrim's Reception Office of the Cathedral of Santiago** (oficinadelperegrino.com): the pages on the Compostela, the credencial, the Certificate of Distance, planning and the history of the pilgrimage.
- **Wikipedia**, for geography, climate tables and history: the articles on Granja de Moreruela, Zamora, Puebla de Sanabria, Ourense, Santiago de Compostela, its cathedral and airport, the Jacobean Holy Year and the Camino de Santiago route descriptions.
- **A standard solar calculation**, for the hours of daylight.
- **The CaminoPlus route catalog, website and route guide**, for the route's start and end, the website's ~370 km and its 15 to 17 stages, the difficulty rating and the app's own note.

The Junta's guide and the Zamora leaflet are from 2010 and 2011. Churches and landscape change slowly, but waymarks, surfaces and services may have changed. The Xunta's English pages can be machine-translated from Spanish, so this book follows the Spanish originals.

Published figures differ between these sources, so every distance in this book is marked ~. Where the sources say nothing, the book says nothing and tells you to check. If you know a better figure, or a fact that has changed, tell CaminoPlus.

Thanks go to the people who keep the Way: the associations of friends of the Camino, the hosts who open the hostels, and the people in the villages who point the way and say *Buen Camino* to a stranger.

## A short glossary

- **Albergue**: a pilgrims' hostel with shared rooms.
- **Botafumeiro**: the great censer of Santiago cathedral.
- **Buen Camino**: "good Way", the greeting between pilgrims.
- **Cabildo**: the governing council of the cathedral.
- **Cofradía**: a brotherhood, such as the one at Rionegro del Puente.
- **Compostela**: the Latin certificate the Pilgrim's Office issues.
- **Corredoira**: a sunken lane between walls or trees, in Galician.
- **Credencial**: the pilgrim's passport.
- **Hospitalero**: the host of a pilgrims' hostel.
- **Junta**: the regional government of Castilla y León.
- **Magosto**: the autumn feast of chestnuts.
- **Menú del peregrino**: a set meal for pilgrims.
- **Pazo**: a Galician manor house.
- **Sello**: a stamp in the credencial.
- **Venda**: a roadside inn, from which several place names on the Serra Seca take their name.
- **Xunta**: the regional government of Galicia.
