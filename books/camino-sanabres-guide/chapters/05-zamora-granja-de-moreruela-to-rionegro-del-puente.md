# Zamora: Granja de Moreruela to Rionegro del Puente

## The character of these days

Three days cross the open country of Zamora, from the Esla to the valley of the Tera: farmland and holm-oak scrub, wide tracks, long straights, and villages of stone, adobe and slate. The stages are the Zamora tourism board's own suggestions, and the distances come from the Junta de Castilla y León's table. Both publications are from 2010 and 2011. The churches and the landscape will be the same. Waymarks, surfaces and services may not be, so check before you go.

### Day 1: Granja de Moreruela to Tábara (~26 km)

You leave the Vía de la Plata behind the village church, following the arrows for Ourense. Wikipedia says the abbey of Moreruela, in the same municipality and from the twelfth century, is often claimed as the first monastery of the Cistercian Order in Spain. Then comes a day of wide tracks across farmland and a descent to the Esla, which you cross by the Quintos bridge: nine stone arches, more than a hundred metres long, built in 1920, the Junta says.

After the river you climb to Faramontanos de Tábara, whose church of San Martín dates, the Junta says, from the thirteenth century. Tábara has a Romanesque church of Santa María, consecrated in 1132, the Junta says. It adds that the Sierra de la Culebra, a regional reserve, is close, and that eagles, wolves and deer live around the town. The poet León Felipe lived in Tábara as a small child, and the Junta's guide prints one of his poems about the pilgrim who always takes new roads.

The Zamora board describes long open tracks, so take water and sun protection. The Junta's table gives ~26 km and the Zamora board ~28 km.

### Day 2: Tábara to Santa Marta de Tera (~23 km)

The day follows the quiet valleys of Valverde and the Tera, past Bercianos de Valverde, Villanueva de las Peras and Santa Croya de Tera. The Zamora board calls the land near Bercianos beautiful: holm-oak hills with rock-rose.

Santa Marta de Tera is the reason to walk this day slowly. The Junta dates its Romanesque church to 1077, on the site of a monastery that, it says, King Ferdinand and Queen Sancha gave to the diocese of Astorga in 1063. On the south portal is St James as a pilgrim, with a bag marked with a scallop shell and a staff in his right hand, and his left hand raised, palm open, in greeting. The Junta calls the carving a symbol of this branch of the Vía de la Plata, and the Zamora board calls it the oldest sculpture of the pilgrim St James. The Junta adds that twice a year, on 21 March and 22 September, the morning sun enters the church by a round window and lights one capital.

The Junta's table gives ~23 km. The Zamora board's leaflet, which goes round by Pueblica de Valverde, gives ~27 km.

### Day 3: Santa Marta de Tera to Rionegro del Puente (~27 km)

The Tera is your companion for most of the day, through Camarzana de Tera, Calzadilla de Tera, Olleros de Tera and Villar de Farfón. At Camarzana the Junta says a late Roman villa came to light in 1861, with mosaics. Calzadilla's name recalls the Roman road number 17 of the Antonine Itinerary, between Braga and Astorga. At Villar de Farfón, the Junta says, the lands of Sanabria begin, and a reservoir drowned the old mills.

Rionegro del Puente lies in the Carballeda district. Its brotherhood, the Cofradía de los Falifos, was probably founded in the Middle Ages, the Junta says, and its main aim was to welcome pilgrims. It also cared for the sick and for foundlings, and repaired roads and bridges: some documents speak of thirty-five bridges, a figure the Junta admits may seem exaggerated, though one document states it plainly. The village was also the home of Diego de Losada, founder of Caracas.

The Junta's table gives ~27 km and the Zamora board ~23 km. Both end the stage at Rionegro del Puente. The villages that follow are very small, so settle your bed the day before.
