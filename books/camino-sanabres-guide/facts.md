## I. Notes for the owner

- **The brief said the road "crosses Zamora province and Galicia (Ourense)".** The official pages show it also crosses Pontevedra (from A Gouxa and Dozón, then Lalín, Silleda) and A Coruña (from A Ponte Ulla at Vedra). Ourense is only the middle of the Galician part.
- **The brief's "stage towns".** The book uses the stage towns of two official sources joined together: the Zamora board's own stage suggestions (Granja to A Gudiña, eight stages) and the Xunta's nine stages (Lubián to Santiago). The Junta's 2011 table and the Zamora leaflet differ from the Xunta by up to ~5 km a day, and the book says so in each day.
- **The Xunta never says "Sanabrés".** It calls the road "Vía de la Plata o Camino Mozárabe". The book explains the names in chapter 1.
- **The Xunta's first stage is titled Lubián-A Gudiña but its text starts at the pass (A Canda).** The 26.9 km figure may or may not include the walk up from Lubián; the Junta's and Zamora board's lengths (23.5 and 23 km) are shorter. The book gives the three figures.
- **The Xunta lists ~15 km for both options of Cea-Dozón**, though the way by Oseira is longer by the Junta's ~9 km to the monastery; the book says to ask locally.
- **The app's route guide ends the road at Ourense; the catalog ends it at Santiago.** The book follows the catalog.
- **Order 11** is also the order of pilgrims-progress-readings in books/series.md and books/plan.json, and camino-sanabres is listed there under routes_without_guide; neither file was touched (outside this folder), so the owner must update them (and tools/check_series.py may complain).
- **Single or secondary sources:** the Wikipedia climate tables (Zamora, Ourense, Santiago) and the 2013 pilgrim statistics; the daylight figures are computed; the Junta's guide (2010-11) is the only source for the Zamora villages' history and sights, with the Zamora leaflet (2010) as a second source for waymarks and stages.
