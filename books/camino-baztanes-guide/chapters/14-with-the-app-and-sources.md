# With the app, and sources

## With the CaminoPlus app

**CaminoPlus** is the offline-first companion app for the Camino de Santiago, with maps that work without signal, stays and cafes, a pilgrim passport with stamps, a journal and friends. The paid plan is called **Pilgrim Plus**. The app is still in development. It lists the Camino Baztanes as a route from Bayonne to Pamplona, with no stage pages for it yet, so look in it for what is available today, and use it, not this book, for beds, cafes, closures, bus times and weather.

A reminder about the passport: the stamps the Pilgrim's Office asks for are those on a credencial it recognises. A passport in CaminoPlus is a keepsake.

The Library holds two other books for walkers of this road: a guide to the French Way, which it joins, and *The Pilgrim's Tale*, seven days with the autobiography of Ignatius of Loyola, made to go with the Camino Baztanes.

## Sources and thanks

This book rests on public sources, and its fact ledger lists every fact against the page it came from. The chief ones are:

- **Visit Navarra**, the official tourism website of Navarre: the route page, its blog on the road and its six stages, the pages on the Baztán valley, Pamplona and the French Way, and a leaflet of the Policía Foral.
- **Meteo Navarra**, the regional weather and climate service: the climate of the Atlantic and Middle zones.
- **The Amis du chemin de St-Jacques Pyrénées-Atlantiques**, the French association: its topo for the voie du Baztan and its route descriptions.
- **The tourist office of Bayonne**, **Tourisme 64** and **the commune of Ainhoa**: Bayonne's cathedral and its place among the ways, and Ainhoa.
- **Eroski Consumer**, a Spanish consumer guide to the Camino: the stage descriptions. Its English pages are machine-translated, so the Spanish originals were read for the figures wherever they loaded.
- **Albergues del Camino**, a Spanish pilgrim-hostel directory: a second set of stages and the account of the road's recovery. It is a secondary source and is named where used.
- **The Pilgrim's Reception Office of the Cathedral of Santiago**: the rules of the Compostela, the credencial and the Certificate of Distance.
- **The Xunta de Galicia** and **the Confraternity of St James**: general advice for pilgrims, and a pilgrim passport.
- **Wikipedia**, English and French: the Voie du Baztan, Baztan, the cathedral of Pamplona, the San Fermín festival and Ignatius of Loyola.
- **A standard solar calculation**, for sunrise and sunset in Elizondo, and **the CaminoPlus route catalogue**, for the road's name, ends and difficulty rating. The catalogue gives no length and no stage count.

Published lengths and heights differ, sometimes by a few kilometres or a few dozen metres, and this book says so. If you know a better figure, or a fact that has changed, tell CaminoPlus.

Thanks go to the people who keep this road open: the associations of friends of the Camino on both sides of the frontier, the hosts who open the hostels, and the people in the villages who say *Buen Camino* to a stranger.

## A short glossary

- **Albergue**: a pilgrims' hostel with shared rooms.
- **Buen Camino**: "good Way", the greeting between pilgrims.
- **Col** or **puerto**: a mountain pass, in French or Spanish.
- **Credencial**: the pilgrim's passport.
- **Hospitalero**: the host of a pilgrims' hostel.
- **Policía Foral**: the regional police of Navarre.
- **Rompepiernas**: "leg-breaker", a hilly stretch.
- **Sello**: a stamp in the credencial.
- **Voie du Baztan**: the French name of this road.
- **Xunta**: the regional government of Galicia.
