# Is this Camino for you?

## How hard is it?

The app's catalogue rates the Camino Baztanés 3 on a scale of 1 to 5. That is the rating it gives the Camino Francés, one less than the Primitivo and one more than the Camino Inglés. It is a middle rating for a short road, and it hides an uneven week.

Visit Navarra's official page calls the road gentle and beautiful. The detailed stage descriptions show where the effort is. Day 2 has a hilly first ~13.5 km that its sources call *rompepiernas*, "leg-breakers". Day 3 begins with the climb to Otsondo. Day 5 crosses Belate, which Eroski Consumer calls the queen stage and measures at more than ~600 m of accumulated climbing between Almandoz and the pass. Eroski rates Days 1, 4 and 6 as easy or nearly so, though Day 6 is the longest, at ~25 km. The distances are small, ~109 km in six days, but this is a mountain road in a wet country, and it asks for a proper start.

## Who it suits

- **Walkers who want a quiet green week.** Visit Navarra says the road is less crowded than the French Way, and that it keeps away from road traffic.
- **Walkers who want to reach the Francés from the north.** Bayonne's tourist office presents the road as the way to join the French Way at Pamplona.
- **Walkers coming off the coastal road.** The French association says the road is recommended to those who start in Bayonne or who arrive on foot from the coastal route.
- **A first Camino, with care.** The days are short, but the first marks on the French side are small, and beds can be scarce in high season. Read the chapters on before you go and on safety.

## Who might choose something else

- **Anyone who wants a Compostela from this walk.** The road ends ~712 km short of Santiago, so it is the first part of a longer walk, not a route to the cathedral. The chapter on arriving explains.
- **Anyone who wants company and services at every turn.** Visit Navarra says the French Way is better waymarked and has more services than this road. Several stage ends are small villages.
- **Anyone who cannot cope with a wet day.** Rain is common here. It is a reason to carry good waterproofs, not a reason to stay at home, but it may be a reason to choose a drier month.
- **Anyone who needs a guaranteed bed each night.** Eroski says beds may be scarce in high season, and a pilgrim-hostel directory says some small hostels work by prior notice. A planner who must know where the night will be will want to book.

## How it compares

Do not expect the busy road of the Francés. Visit Navarra says that its well-known sister is better signed and better served, and that this road offers big green landscapes and, above all, fewer crowds. It adds that some people link the road with the Camino Primitivo because it keeps off the traffic. The French Way is the great road. This is a small way into it, and on the last day, where the two meet, you may see other pilgrims for the first time in days.

If two days of ~20 km running, with your loaded pack, sound like too much, plan the shorter splits in the chapter on other ways to cut the road, and take a rest day in Elizondo, the main town on the way.
