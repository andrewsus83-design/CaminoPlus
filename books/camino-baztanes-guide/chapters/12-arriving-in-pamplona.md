# Arriving in Pamplona

## The last streets

After the bridge of the Trinity at Arre you are on the French Way. Eroski Consumer describes the last ~5 km: straight on through Villava and Burlada, over the Magdalena bridge across the river Arga, and into the old city by the Portal de Francia, which the guide to the French Way in this series calls the gate pilgrims from France entered by. The tourist office of Bayonne says you can join the French Way at Pamplona by following the Baztán road from Bayonne, and that is what you have done.

Visit Navarra says the old town has kept its medieval layout. It was formed by three burghs, the Navarrería, San Cernin and San Nicolás, which were united in 1423, when King Charles III the Noble issued the Privilege of the Union. The cathedral of Santa María has, says Visit Navarra, an austere neoclassical façade that hides a Gothic interior: the alabaster tomb of Charles III and his wife Eleanor, the choir stalls, and a Gothic cloister that the page calls one of the best of Europe in its style. Wikipedia dates the Gothic church to the fifteenth century and the façade to 1783. Times for visits change, so ask. Visit Navarra also points to the city walls, which you can walk, and the Renaissance citadel.

In July the city belongs to the festival. Wikipedia says San Fermín runs from noon on 6 July to midnight on 14 July. If you will arrive then, book your bed well in advance.

## The Compostela

The Compostela is the certificate the Pilgrim's Office in Santiago gives to those who meet its rules. The Office owns the rules, and they can change. This book says what the Office says, and sends you to the Office in any doubt.

- The Compostela is for those who come to the tomb of the Apostle for religious and/or spiritual reasons and follow the routes of the Way of St James on foot, by bicycle or on horseback.
- It asks for at least the last hundred kilometres on foot or on horseback, or the last two hundred kilometres by bicycle, shown by the credencial, stamped along the route.
- On foot or on horseback, that means at least a hundred continuous kilometres on the same route to Santiago that the Office recognises. The final stage must include at least the last one leading into the cathedral.
- You may walk the Way in stages, if they follow in chronological and geographical order. A pilgrim who walks only the minimum distance must get the credencial stamped at the start and at the end of each stage, with the date.
- A pilgrim who has already walked part of a recognised route outside Spain needs no less than seventy kilometres in Spain.
- The Cabildo, the cathedral's governing council, reserves the right to grant the Compostela in special and exceptional cases.

The Office awards the Compostela to those who walk for religious or spiritual reasons, and it describes the pilgrimage it has in mind as one made "with a Christian sentiment, even if it is only with an attitude of search". If that does not describe your reason for walking, ask the Pilgrim's Office what it can give you: it also offers a Certificate of Distance, which records the kilometres you walked from wherever you started.

**This road ends at Pamplona, and Santiago is far beyond.** The guide to the French Way in this series counts ~712 km and 30 walking days from Pamplona to Santiago, and says other tables differ by several kilometres. The Baztán road, ~109 km, cannot on its own bring you to a Compostela, because the Office's rule counts the last stretch into the cathedral. The Office's pages read for this book do not list the routes it recognises and do not mention the Camino Baztanés. If you plan to carry on to Santiago, and want to know how the Office would read a credencial that begins in Bayonne, ask it before you go. This book does not say whether any start counts.

## If you carry on

The French Way continues from Pamplona, and the guide to it in this series is written for that walk. Together, the Baztán road and the French Way from Pamplona make about 820 km, a figure worked out from two estimates, so treat it as a rough guide. Make sure your credencial has room for more stamps.

## If you stay

Do not leave on the morning after you arrive. Visit Navarra says a day is hardly enough for Pamplona. Rest. Walk the walls. Sit in a square. Plan your way home with the timetables in front of you, not at the last minute.
