# The Baztán valley: Urdax to Berroeta

## The character of these days

Two days through the heart of the valley that gives the road its name. Visit Navarra describes it as one of the most idyllic of the Atlantic Pyrenean valleys of Navarre, a country of soft green hills, stone farmhouses with pinkish stonework, and cattle in the meadows. The first day begins with a climb and ends in the valley's main town. The second is short and quiet, a day to prepare for Belate.

### Day 3: Urdazubi/Urdax to Elizondo (~19 km)

Visit Navarra's blog puts this stage at ~18.8 km and about four and a half hours, and tells you to take the pass of Otsondo, at the start of the day, very calmly. The climb begins as soon as you leave the village. A pilgrim-hostel directory counts ~450 m of climbing in the first stretch, and French Wikipedia puts the pass at ~670 m. Eroski Consumer describes strong gradients on a rough track at first, then a forest track, in woods that it says are thick and easily wrapped in mist, and the French association says the same of Otsondo. Keep your track open.

The descent brings you into the valley and to Amaiur/Maya, ~10 km into the day. Visit Navarra tells its story. Navarre lost the independence of its kingdom in 1512, and troops loyal to the Navarrese crown held the castle of Amaiur until they were defeated in 1522: the last redoubt of resistance. Only ruins remain on the hill of Gaztelu, with a monolith put up in their memory, and the village has a museum about the castle. The village itself is a single street of farmhouses of every local style, entered through a porticoed arch, with a medieval church of the Assumption and a restored mill that has ground maize since the eighteenth century.

After Amaiur the way follows an old road above the valley. The directory says there are two ways out of the village, one by Urrasun and one by Bozate, and that they rejoin at Arizkun. Eroski describes the one by Urrasun, past a hermitage of Santiago restored in 2007 and 2008. Visit Navarra says Bozate is the quarter of Arizkun where the *agotes* lived, a people who suffered exclusion and about whom much has been speculated. Beyond Arizkun the path runs near the river, through Elbete, and then you cross into Elizondo.

Elizondo is the main town of the valley. Visit Navarra says its mansions and palaces reflect the wealth that emigrants to America brought home, and names the baroque palace of Arizkunenea, of 1730, and the baroque church of Santiago. Its blog says the road stops in Elizondo, where you can reach more services if you need them, and its route page praises the town's chocolate with hazelnuts.

### Day 4: Elizondo to Berroeta (~10 km)

The shortest day of the road. Visit Navarra's blog calls it a simple stage of ~9.7 km, about two and a quarter hours, and Eroski says there are no serious difficulties, only a short climb to Zigaurre.

You leave Elizondo past the town hall, cross the river and follow it, and pass the villages of Lekaroz and Irurita. Visit Navarra says Irurita is the second most populous village of the valley, with fine civil architecture. Then the way rises to Zigaurre and its chapel of San Andrés, with a viewpoint over the valley a little off the path, and comes to Ziga. Visit Navarra says its parish church of St Lawrence is surprisingly big for so small a village, and Eroski and the French association say the locals call it the cathedral. Then Aniz, and Berroeta.

Use the short day well: wash your clothes, rest your feet, and read the weather for Belate. The chapter on other ways to split the road shows how some walkers go further today, to shorten tomorrow.
