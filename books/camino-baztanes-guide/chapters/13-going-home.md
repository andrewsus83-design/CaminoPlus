# Going home

## The road after the road

A walk of six days is short enough that you may arrive home before it has finished with you. The feet recover in a day or two. The rest takes longer: the green, the quiet, the bridge where your road met another.

Write something down in the first week, while the details are still sharp. A line a day is enough. Keep the credencial, too. It is a record of a road you walked, with its stamps, and it is yours.

## Getting home

Plan your way back from Pamplona before you leave it. This book names no timetables, because they change, and no page read for it gives a current one. Look at the times with the evening in front of you, ask your hostel or the tourist office, and do not plan a train or a flight for the afternoon of your last walking day. If you have a spare day, spend it in the city.

## Walking again

Eroski Consumer says three Jacobean roads leave from Bayonne: one toward Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port, one toward Irun and the coast, and the one you have just walked, to Pamplona. You can walk the others another year. You can also pick up the French Way where this road meets it, and walk on to Santiago in pieces, a week at a time. The guide to the French Way in this series is written for that walk.

Whatever you do next, thank the people who kept the road open for you: the friends of the Camino who cleared the paths, the hosts, the villagers who said *Buen Camino* as you passed.
