# When to go

## The weather, in patterns

This road is green because it is wet. Meteo Navarra, the weather and climate service of the regional government, describes the valleys of northern Navarre as having an oceanic climate, with abundant rain, fog and drizzle, and calls this the rainiest part of Navarre. Its page on that zone uses Doneztebe/Santesteban, a town in the same part of Navarre, as its example, so the figures below are the page's, for the zone, not for one village on your route. No weather table for the French half was read for this book; for that, ask Météo-France, the national weather service.

- **Spring.** Changeable and rainy. Average daytime highs rise from ~16 °C in March to ~21 °C in May in the valleys, with ~260 to ~570 mm of rain over the three months.
- **Summer.** Mild: in July and August, the daily lows and highs average between ~11 and ~26 °C. It is the least rainy season, and rain is still frequent: ~120 to ~360 mm over the three months. The page says plainly that there is no summer drought.
- **Autumn.** September is still summery, with an afternoon average of ~25 °C in Doneztebe/Santesteban. By November it falls to ~14.8 °C and the rain is abundant again, ~250 to ~650 mm over the season.
- **Winter.** The mildest winters in Navarre, the page says, with a January average of ~7.2 °C in Doneztebe/Santesteban, and wet. Higher and further inland, it adds, winters are colder.

Over a year the zone gets between ~900 and ~2,500 mm of rain, depending on the place. The ridge of Belate and Azpirotz acts as a screen against the wet fronts from the north and north-west, and the southern valleys beyond it, among them the Ultzama, keep some of the oceanic character. Pamplona lies in a different zone. Meteo Navarra calls it sub-oceanic: drier than the valleys, with a dry season in summer and July and August maxima that average ~28 to ~30 °C.

The app's route notes suggest May to September. The pattern fits: summer is the least rainy season, though on this road no month is dry. In winter, Eroski Consumer warns that snow is easy to find at the pass of Belate.

## Daylight

These are the book's own figures, calculated from a standard solar formula for Elizondo, in local clock time. They are approximate, and you should check a sun table before you plan. Navarre keeps Central European Time, with summer time from spring to autumn.

- Late December: sunrise ~08:35, sunset ~17:35, about 9 hours of daylight.
- Mid-November: sunset ~17:45.
- Mid-April: sunset ~20:45.
- Mid-May: sunset ~21:20.
- Late June: sunrise ~06:25, sunset ~21:50, about 15 hours of daylight.
- Late September: sunset ~20:10.
- Mid-October: sunset ~19:25.

From late autumn to early spring, plan for ~9 to ~10 hours of light, start at first light, and carry a head torch. The two hard days, over Otsondo and over Belate, are the ones not to start late.

## Crowds and quiet

No count of pilgrims on this road was found in a source that could be checked: the Pilgrim's Office's statistics page is built by script and could not be read. Visit Navarra's blog says the road is far less popular than the French Way.

Pamplona is the exception, in July. Wikipedia says the San Fermín festival runs from noon on 6 July to midnight on 14 July, and the city fills. If you plan to arrive then, book your bed well ahead.

## Check before you go

Rain, mist, a path closed after a storm, snow at the pass: these change from year to year and from day to day. Meteo Navarra publishes forecasts for Navarre, and Visit Navarra's Camino page links to one made for the Camino de Santiago. Check Visit Navarra's pages and the French association's website for notices, look at the forecast the evening before and again at breakfast, and ask locally on the way.
