# The inner Way

People walk this road for many reasons, and they do not have to be yours. Some walk out of faith: the road is old, and its oldest buildings are places of welcome for pilgrims. Some walk for sport, or for a rest from work, or to find the French Way from the north, or because they wanted a quiet week among green hills. Some walk out of grief, or at a turning point. Some are curious about a valley they have only seen on a map. All are welcome on the road, and none needs to defend the reason.

## A road made of welcome

Visit Navarra names the pilgrims' hospitals and monasteries of Urdax and Belate as the road's notable places, and says they date from the eleventh and twelfth centuries. Eroski Consumer reports a letter of 1378 from the bishop of Bayonne saying that without the hospitality of the hospital at Belate, countless men would be lost in these mountains in winter. You will not need rescuing, but being welcomed in a small village may be something you did not know you needed.

The road also exists today because a few people cared. Eroski says an association of friends of the Camino began in 2000 to recover it, and a pilgrim-hostel directory says that volunteers cleared old paths and rebuilt a chapel stone by stone. You can thank them by walking well, and by leaving the places as you found them.

## A frontier on foot

On Day 2 you walk out of one country and into another with no ceremony: Eroski says you pass the old customs buildings and a small stream, the Lapitxuri, as you enter Spain. It may be worth stopping there for a minute.

## Pamplona, and a soldier

Pamplona is also where a famous life turned. Wikipedia says that at the Battle of Pamplona, on 20 May 1521, a cannonball that ricocheted off a wall fractured the right leg of a young soldier, Íñigo de Loyola, and that during his recovery he underwent a spiritual conversion. He became Ignatius of Loyola. No source read for this book says he walked this road, and this book does not say so. But if your walk ends in Pamplona, you finish in a city where a soldier's plans ended and another life began.

## Walking with a question

You do not need a question, but if you have one it will keep you company. Here is one for each day, to take or leave.

1. **Bayonne to Ustaritz.** What did you pack because you thought you might need it, and could you leave it behind?
2. **Ustaritz to Urdazubi/Urdax.** You cross a frontier on foot today. What are you leaving on this side?
3. **Urdazubi/Urdax to Elizondo.** The climb comes first. What is the first hard thing you do each day, and how do you begin it?
4. **Elizondo to Berroeta.** An easy short day, with nobody expecting anything of you. What would you do with an open afternoon?
5. **Berroeta to Olagüe.** In the mist you can see only the next few steps. What helps you when you cannot see far ahead?
6. **Olagüe to Pamplona.** Your road meets a bigger one today. Where does your own road meet another, and who will you meet on it?

## For every kind of walker

If you walk with faith, the little chapel of Santiago at Belate is a place for quiet. If you do not, it is quiet anyway. If your reason is grief, the valley and the rain will ask nothing of you. If you walk to get fit, let the green in anyway. The road does not ask for a reason at the door.
