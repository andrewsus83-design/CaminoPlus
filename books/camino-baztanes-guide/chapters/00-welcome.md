# Welcome

The Camino Baztanés is a small road through a very green country. It leaves Bayonne, in the French Basque Country, climbs over the western end of the Pyrenees by two passes, runs down the Baztán valley of Navarre, and ends in Pamplona, where it meets the Camino Francés. (The app's catalogue spells the name *Baztanes*, without the accent. Spanish sources write *Baztanés*, and so does this book.) Visit Navarra, the region's official tourism website, divides it into six walking days that add up to ~109 km.

**The promise of this book is simple.** By the last page you will know what each of the six days asks of you, what to check before you set out, and where the road leads when it ends.

## How to use it

If you are a month or more away, read the next four chapters in order, then the days, and the chapter on other ways to split them: the sources do not agree on how many days this road has. If you are on the road tonight, go to your stage, and read "Body, weather and safety" before Day 3 and again before Day 5. If you are only dreaming, start with "Is this Camino for you?" and finish with "The inner Way".

## What this book is not

It is not a live map, a timetable or a booking service. It names no hostels, cafes or shops, and it gives no prices, opening hours or bed counts. They change, and a wrong one costs a tired person dearly. For those, use the CaminoPlus app and ask locally.

This book was written with AI assistance, from public sources, and its author has not walked the road. So it gives you sources instead of memories, and it says plainly when the sources disagree.

It is also not a Compostela book. The Compostela, the certificate of the Pilgrim's Office in Santiago, belongs to a walk that arrives at the cathedral of Santiago. This road ends in Pamplona, and the French Way carries on from there.

Every distance carries a tilde, **~**, because it is approximate. Where something depends on the season (snow, mist, a muddy path), the book says *check before you go*, and means it. On a mountain road in a wet climate, that line matters.

## A few words you will meet

*Buen Camino*, "good Way", is what pilgrims and locals say as you pass. An *albergue* is a pilgrims' hostel. The *credencial* is your pilgrim's passport, a folded card that you carry and have stamped along the way; each stamp is a *sello*. A *puerto* is a mountain pass (the French word is *col*). In Navarre the official pages give most place names in Basque and Spanish together, such as Urdazubi/Urdax and Amaiur/Maya. The glossary at the end has the rest.

Pack something waterproof, start the long days early, and be kind to your feet. *Buen Camino.*
