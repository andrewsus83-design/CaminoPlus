# Belate and the road to Pamplona

## The character of these days

One mountain day and one long walk down. The first crosses the ridge of Belate from the Baztán valley into the Ultzama, the hardest ground of the whole road. The second follows rivers and valleys to the edge of Pamplona and ends where you meet the French Way. Read "Body, weather and safety" before Day 5.

### Day 5: Berroeta to Olagüe (~20 km)

This is the hard day, and Eroski Consumer and a pilgrim-hostel directory both say so. Visit Navarra's blog gives ~20.4 km, about five hours, with two thirds of the stage through woods and mid-mountain country. Eroski calls it the queen stage and counts more than ~600 m of accumulated climbing between Almandoz and the pass. The directory says the weather weighs more here than on any other day: rain, mud or fog can slow you a good deal.

You go down from Berroeta to Almandoz, ~2 km away, and then climb for a long time, first through chestnut woods. Eroski says the climb passes an inn that stands where an old hostel for wayfarers and pilgrims once did, then goes on up a steep forest path over Mount Larrondo and Mount Arluz. At the top, stone markers line the way, and close by is the hermitage of Santiago, of the twelfth century, rebuilt after a long effort and blessed in 2016, say Eroski and the directory.

Near here the way crosses the line between the Atlantic and Mediterranean river basins, says Eroski, and the pass is the boundary of the Baztán and Ultzama valleys. Below it lies the monastery of Santa María de Belate. French Wikipedia says the old hospital there received pilgrims on the Baztán road, and Visit Navarra names Belate with Urdax as the road's notable monasteries. Eroski dates the hospital to the twelfth century, and quotes a letter of 1378 from the bishop of Bayonne, reported by Padre Germán de Pamplona, saying that countless men would be lost and die in these mountains and forests, especially in winter, were it not for the food, help and guidance the hospital gave to all who passed.

Heights differ by source, as the first chapter says: ~847 m, about 900 m, ~938 m.

The forest path down to Lantz, ~15 km into the day, is the loneliest part. Eroski says the road runs close to the path as far as the monastery of Belate, so help is near on the climb. Beyond it, the path crosses the young Ultzama river and runs through beech woods where the track fades and you must watch for the yellow arrows. Lantz is on the normal route, ~4 km before Olagüe, and the directory suggests it as an earlier stop if the pass has taken longer than planned. Olagüe is the capital of the Anué valley, says Eroski, with a baroque parish church.

**Care.** Start early, and decide the evening before whether the conditions are right. If you are in doubt, ask locally, and do not cross in thick mist or on snow you cannot judge. The chapter on safety says more.

### Day 6: Olagüe to Pamplona (~25 km)

The last day is the longest, and a gentle one. Visit Navarra's blog calls it simple, though longer, ~25 km, about six hours, and Eroski says it has little or no difficulty.

The way crosses the small valleys of the Anué, the Odieta and the Olaibar, through Leazkue, Etulain, Burutain, Ostiz, Enderiz and Olaiz. Eroski says the path between Enderiz and Olaiz can be muddy after a little rain, and may have a fallen tree across it. It also says there is no place to eat until Sorauren, ~14 km into the day, which has a medieval bridge of four arches. Carry food and water. After Sorauren comes ~6 km along the river park of the Ultzama, then an industrial estate and the city's ring road, and then Arre.

At Arre you reach the medieval bridge of the Trinity, ~20 km from Olagüe. Here the Camino Baztanés meets the Camino Francés. On the far side of the bridge, says Eroski, is the old hospital of the Trinity, a pilgrims' hostel today, with its Romanesque basilica; Visit Navarra's page on the French Way describes the basilica at Arre as of Romanesque origin. The French Way goes straight on through Villava and Burlada to the river Arga and the old city. Eroski puts Arre ~4.6 km from the centre, and the directory ~4.9 km, so the ~25 km of the day are really ~20 km of the Baztán road and ~5 km of the French Way.

If your legs say stop, you can end the day at Arre: the directory ends its sixth stage there. Otherwise carry on to the old town, and read the chapter on arriving.
